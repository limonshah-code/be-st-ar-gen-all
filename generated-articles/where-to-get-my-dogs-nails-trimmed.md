## FAQ Section

### How often should my dog's nails be trimmed?
Most dogs need their nails trimmed every 3 to 4 weeks. However, this depends on their activity level and breed. Dogs who walk on hard surfaces often wear down their nails naturally. Less active dogs need more frequent trims. You should not hear your dog's nails clicking on the floor. This is a sign they are too long.

### What happens if my dog's nails are too long?
Long nails can cause significant discomfort and health problems. They can push the toes up, leading to pain in the paw and joints. This changes how your dog walks. Long nails are also more prone to breaking or tearing, which is very painful. In severe cases, they can curl and grow into the paw pad, causing infection.

### Is it better to use clippers or a grinder for dog nails?
Both clippers and grinders are effective tools. Clippers are fast and work well for most dogs. Grinders, like a Dremel, smooth the nail and can get closer to the quick without cutting it. Some dogs prefer the grinding sensation, while others dislike the vibration or noise. The best tool depends on your dog's preference and your skill level.

### Can I trim my dog's nails if they are black?
Yes, you can trim black nails, but it requires more caution. The quick is not visible in black nails. You must trim small amounts at a time. Look for a gray or pinkish dot in the center of the trimmed nail; this indicates you are close to the quick. Using a grinder can also help you go slowly. If unsure, a professional groomer or vet can do it safely.

### What should I do if I cut the quick?
If you accidentally cut the quick, do not panic. It will bleed, but it is not usually serious. Apply styptic powder or cornstarch to the bleeding nail. Apply gentle pressure until the bleeding stops. Reassure your dog and offer a treat. If bleeding continues for a long time or you are concerned, contact your vet.

### How much does it typically cost to get dog nails trimmed?
The cost varies based on the service provider. Pet supply stores often charge \$8-\$15. Professional groomers usually charge \$10-\$25 for a standalone trim. Veterinary clinics charge \$15-\$30. Mobile groomers are often \$25-\$50, reflecting their convenience. These prices can increase for large or difficult-to-handle dogs.
