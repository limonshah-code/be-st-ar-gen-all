## When Do Italian Greyhounds Start to Calm Down?

Bringing an Italian Greyhound puppy into your home brings joy and a burst of vibrant energy. These elegant, slender dogs are renowned for their playful spirits and incredible speed. However, their high energy levels often lead new owners to ask a common question: **At what age do Italian Greyhounds calm down**? It is a natural concern for anyone seeking a serene home life.

Understanding the typical developmental stages of your Italian Greyhound helps set realistic expectations. Every dog is an individual, but there are general timelines for when their boundless puppy energy begins to settle. We will explore these phases, offer insights into managing their exuberance, and provide practical strategies to help your Italian Greyhound become a calm, well-adjusted companion. Get ready to uncover the journey from a lively pup to a more composed adult dog.

### Takeaway: Key Steps for a Calmer Italian Greyhound

*   **Patience is paramount:** Italian Greyhounds mature gradually; their calm phase takes time.
*   **Consistent training:** Start early with positive reinforcement for good behavior.
*   **Adequate exercise:** Provide both physical outlets and mental stimulation daily.
*   **Establish routine:** Predictable schedules help reduce anxiety and promote stability.
*   **Socialize early:** Expose your puppy to various sights, sounds, and people calmly.
*   **Consider calming aids:** Discuss options like supplements or specific techniques with your vet.
*   **Understand breed traits:** IGs have unique needs; cater to their sensitive, intelligent nature.

### Clear Answer: When Italian Greyhounds Settle

Italian Greyhounds typically begin to calm down between 1.5 to 3 years of age, though individual variations exist. Their high puppy energy gradually transitions to a more settled adult demeanor with consistent training, proper exercise, and mental enrichment. Patience is key for their development.

### The Energetic Italian Greyhound Puppy Phase

The first few months with an Italian Greyhound puppy are incredibly exciting. These tiny bundles of joy come with an impressive amount of energy. You will see them running "zoomies" around the house, playing with toys, and exploring every corner. This high activity level is completely normal for their age.

Italian Greyhound puppies, like many small breeds, have a rapid metabolism. This means they burn through energy quickly and need frequent periods of play and rest. They are curious and eager to learn about their world. Managing this early energy involves structured play and short training sessions.

During this stage, consistent training is vital. Start teaching basic commands like "sit," "stay," and "come" early on. Positive reinforcement works best with their sensitive nature. [How do I get my dog to calm down](https://beacleaner.com/how-do-i-get-my-dog-to-calm-down) when they are so young? Focus on teaching impulse control and rewarding quiet behavior.

Remember, a tired puppy is often a well-behaved puppy. Provide opportunities for safe, off-leash play in a secure area. Short, frequent walks are good, but always ensure they are warm enough, as IGs have thin coats. Crate training can also offer a safe, quiet space for them to decompress.

### Italian Greyhound Adolescence: The Teenage Years

After the puppy phase, typically around 6 months, Italian Greyhounds enter adolescence. This period can last until they are about 1.5 to 2 years old. Think of it as their teenage phase; it comes with its own set of challenges and continued high energy. Your IG might test boundaries and display independent behaviors.

During adolescence, an Italian Greyhound's physical energy remains high, but their mental capacity for learning also grows. This is a crucial time to reinforce all prior training and introduce more advanced commands. Consistent routines become even more important. Dogs thrive on predictability.

You might notice an increase in selective hearing or a renewed burst of energy. This is not defiance, but rather a normal part of their development. Patience and persistence are your best tools. Continue with regular exercise, ensuring it is appropriate for their growing bodies.
