A very big size German Shepherd is a dog that significantly exceeds the standard height and weight ranges set by breed organizations like the AKC or FCI. ## The Official German Shepherd Breed Standards

The German Shepherd Dog has precise breed standards. These standards guide breeders and judges. They ensure the breed keeps its working characteristics and health. The American Kennel Club (AKC) and the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI) publish these standards. They specify ideal height and weight for males and females.

Male German Shepherds typically stand between 24 and 26 inches (60-65 cm) tall. This height is measured at the top of the shoulder. Their weight should fall between 65 and 90 pounds (29-41 kg). A male German Shepherd over these measurements is considered oversized. This means they are a very big size German Shepherd.

Female German Shepherds are slightly smaller. They usually stand 22 to 24 inches (55-60 cm) tall. Their weight ranges from 50 to 70 pounds (22-32 kg). A female German Shepherd exceeding these dimensions is also considered a very big size. Breeders aim for dogs within these ranges. This helps maintain the breed's functional structure.

These standards are more than just numbers. They describe a balanced, athletic dog. A dog outside these limits might struggle with movement. For example, a dog that is too heavy puts more stress on its joints. This can lead to health issues. Understanding these official numbers helps us see what "very big" truly means. It helps owners understand if their dog is within the healthy size for the breed.

Breeders play a vital role here. Reputable breeders select breeding dogs carefully. They aim to produce puppies that meet breed standards. They also prioritize health and temperament. Sometimes, a "very big size German Shepherd" comes from lines where larger dogs were preferred. This might happen even if these larger dogs exceed the standard.

Different German Shepherd lines can also show size variations. For example, some working lines might be slightly leaner. Show lines, particularly those from certain regions, can appear more substantial. This does not mean they are always "very big," but they can seem larger due to their bone structure. ## Nutrition and Environment: Key to GSD Growth

Genetics sets the potential for a German Shepherd's size. However, nutrition and environment determine if that potential is reached. Proper diet is extremely important, especially during puppyhood. A balanced diet provides all necessary nutrients. It helps puppies grow at a healthy rate.

Feeding puppies too much, or food with too many calories, can cause rapid growth. This fast growth can be harmful. It puts stress on developing bones and joints. This can lead to orthopedic problems later in life. We want steady, consistent growth, not accelerated growth. Quality puppy food formulated for large breeds is often recommended. This food supports their unique growth needs.

As German Shepherds mature, their nutritional needs change. Adult dogs require a diet that maintains their ideal weight. Overfeeding an adult German Shepherd leads to obesity. Obesity causes many health issues. It also adds strain to their joints. An oversized German Shepherd needs careful weight management. They need a diet portioned to their activity level.

Regular, appropriate exercise also plays a big role. Exercise helps build strong muscles and bones. It keeps the dog at a healthy weight. Too little exercise can lead to weight gain. Too much high-impact exercise, especially in young dogs, can damage growth plates. The environment also includes clean water and a safe space. A healthy environment supports overall well-being. This contributes to optimal growth and size.

## Health Considerations for Large German Shepherds

While a very big size German Shepherd can be impressive, their larger size comes with specific health risks. These risks are important for owners to understand. Being oversized puts extra stress on their skeletal system. This includes their bones and joints. German Shepherds are already prone to certain orthopedic conditions. Increased size can make these conditions worse.

One of the most common issues is hip and elbow dysplasia. These are genetic conditions. The joints do not form correctly. A larger, heavier dog experiences more wear and tear on these faulty joints. This leads to pain, arthritis, and reduced mobility. Early diagnosis and management are crucial. Regular veterinary check-ups can help monitor joint health.

Another concern for large breeds is Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus (GDV), also known as bloat. This life-threatening condition involves the stomach twisting. Larger, deep-chested dogs are more susceptible. Symptoms include a swollen abdomen, retching without vomiting, and restlessness. Bloat requires immediate emergency veterinary care. Feeding smaller, more frequent meals can help reduce the risk.

Spinal issues can also appear in larger German Shepherds. Degenerative Myelopathy is a progressive spinal cord disease. It causes weakness and paralysis in the hind legs. While not directly caused by size, a larger dog might show symptoms more severely. Proper nutrition and avoiding excessive jumping or strenuous exercise, especially when young, help support spinal health. Responsible breeding practices also aim to reduce the incidence of these genetic conditions.

It teaches them to respond to commands. This is important for managing their strength. Proper socialization helps them interact calmly with other people and animals. Food expenses will be higher for a very big German Shepherd. They eat more food to maintain their body weight. High-quality dog food is essential for their health. Regular vet visits also become more expensive. Larger doses of medication or anesthesia are often needed. Special equipment may be necessary. These dogs also need regular, appropriate exercise. It keeps them fit and happy. Too much vigorous exercise, especially in youth, can harm their joints. Balanced activities, like walks and controlled play, are best. Mental stimulation is also important. Provide puzzle toys or training sessions to keep their minds active. Living with an oversized German Shepherd means giving them consistent care and attention. It builds a strong, healthy bond.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: What is the average weight of a very big German Shepherd?

A very big German Shepherd often weighs significantly more than the breed standard. For males, this means over 90 pounds (41 kg). For females, it is typically over 70 pounds (32 kg). These dogs carry a larger frame. Their weight often exceeds the healthy range.

### Q2: Do very big German Shepherds have more health problems?

Yes, very big German Shepherds can face increased health risks. Their extra weight puts more stress on their joints. This can worsen conditions like hip and elbow dysplasia. They also have a higher risk for bloat. Regular vet check-ups are important for them.

### Q3: How can I tell if my German Shepherd puppy will be very big?

You can estimate your German Shepherd puppy's adult size by looking at its parents. If the parents are large, the puppy likely will be too. Growth charts also offer a guide. Remember, proper nutrition also influences their final size.

### Q4: Is it better to have a German Shepherd that is larger than average?

It is not necessarily better for a German Shepherd to be larger than average. Oversized dogs can experience more orthopedic issues. They may also have a shorter lifespan due to the stress on their bodies. Sticking to breed standards promotes better health.

### Q5: How much exercise does a very big German Shepherd need?

A very big German Shepherd needs regular, moderate exercise. This helps maintain a healthy weight and strong muscles. Daily walks, controlled play, and mental challenges are good. Avoid excessive high-impact activity, especially when young. This protects their joints.

### Q6: Can diet make a German Shepherd grow to a very big size?

Diet heavily influences a German Shepherd's growth. Overfeeding puppies or providing a diet too high in calories can cause rapid growth. This can make them larger than average. However, this growth might lead to skeletal problems. A balanced diet is key.
