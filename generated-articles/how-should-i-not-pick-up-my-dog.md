## Picking Up Your Dog Safely: What to Avoid

Imagine someone suddenly grabbing you. They lift you without warning or proper support. How would you feel? Our dogs experience similar feelings when we pick them up incorrectly. Understanding how should I not pick up my dog is vital for their physical health and emotional well-being. Incorrect lifting can cause pain, fear, or even serious injury.

This article explores common mistakes people make when lifting dogs. We will discuss specific actions to avoid. We will also cover the risks associated with improper handling. Our goal is to help you keep your furry friend safe and happy. You will learn to recognize signs of discomfort and understand better ways to interact.

### Takeaway

Here are the key points for safely handling your dog:
*   Never lift your dog by their legs, scruff, or one arm. These methods cause pain and injury.
*   Avoid surprising your dog with a lift. Give them a warning and ensure they are comfortable.
*   Do not lift dogs that show aggression or fear. This can lead to bites or falls.
*   Always support their chest and hindquarters. This protects their spine and joints.
*   Make lifting a positive experience. Use calm movements and treats.

### Concise Answer

To avoid harming your dog, you should not pick them up by their legs, scruff, or with one hand under their chest. These methods lack proper support and can cause spinal damage, joint strain, or fear. Always ensure full body support to keep them safe and comfortable.

## Understanding Your Dog's Body: Why Picking Up Matters

Your dog’s body is a complex system. It needs careful handling. The spine is especially vulnerable. It consists of many small bones and discs. Improper lifting can put immense stress on these structures. This stress can lead to severe back problems.

Joints, like hips and shoulders, also need protection. Dogs carry most of their weight on their front limbs. Lifting them incorrectly can twist or strain these joints. This is true for all dogs. However, some breeds are more prone to issues. Breeds with long backs, like Dachshunds, are susceptible to Intervertebral Disc Disease (IVDD). Bulldogs might have breathing difficulties. Small breeds can suffer easily from luxating patellas (kneecap dislocation).

Consider your dog's perspective. A sudden, unsupported lift feels like a loss of control. It can be frightening. They may not understand your intentions. This can damage their trust in you. Always consider their physical and emotional state before attempting to lift them. Understanding their body means protecting them from harm. This includes physical harm and emotional distress.

Some small dogs, like Chihuahuas, may react strongly to being picked up. They might scream or yelp. This reaction often comes from fear or past negative experiences. It is important to approach them gently. Never force a lift on a scared dog. Recognizing their discomfort signals helps prevent further issues. ## The Perils of Improper Grips: How Not to Hold Your Dog

Many people make mistakes when lifting their dogs. These common improper grips cause pain and anxiety. Avoiding them is crucial for your dog’s safety. Let us look at what you should not do.

First, never grab your dog by their legs. Lifting them this way can dislocate joints. It can also cause fractures. Dogs' legs are not designed to support their entire body weight during a lift. Their bones and ligaments are delicate. This applies especially to puppies and senior dogs.

Second, do not scruff your dog. Scruffing involves grabbing the loose skin on the back of their neck. This method is sometimes used on puppies by their mothers. However, an adult dog is too heavy for scruffing. It can cause severe neck pain and skin damage. It is not an appropriate way to handle an adult dog. This action can also make your dog fearful of your hands.

Third, avoid a one-handed lift under their chest. This leaves their hindquarters dangling. It puts extreme pressure on their spine. This can lead to serious back injuries. It creates instability. Your dog might struggle to regain balance. They may also feel unsupported and insecure.

Improper grips create discomfort. They also erode your dog's trust. They might associate being picked up with pain. This can lead to them becoming defensive or fearful when you reach for them. A dog that expects pain might nip or bite. This is a natural defense mechanism. Always prioritize their comfort and safety. This builds a stronger, more trusting relationship between you and your pet.

## When to Avoid Lifting Your Dog Completely

There are times when lifting your dog is not a good idea. Knowing these situations helps prevent injury to both you and your pet. It ensures everyone stays safe. Avoid lifting your dog if they show signs of aggression. A growl, snarl, or bared teeth are clear warnings. Lifting an aggressive dog can lead to a bite. It can also escalate the aggression. They might feel trapped or threatened.

Do not lift a dog if they are injured. Moving an injured animal can worsen the injury. This is especially true for suspected spinal or bone issues. If your dog is limping or seems in pain, seek veterinary advice first. A professional can assess the situation safely. They can provide guidance on moving your dog.

Avoid lifting dogs who are clearly afraid. A fearful dog might struggle violently. They could slip from your grasp. This could cause a fall and further injury. Signs of fear include tail tucked, ears back, trembling, or trying to hide. Respect their fear. Try to coax them gently instead.

Never lift your dog during a fight with another dog. Your hands could get caught in the middle. This puts you at risk of injury. It also prolongs the struggle. ## Risks and Injuries: What Can Happen When You Pick Up Your Dog Wrong

Picking up your dog incorrectly carries serious risks. These risks affect both your dog's health and your own safety. We need to be aware of these potential dangers. This helps us prevent them.

Spinal injuries are a major concern. When you lift a dog without supporting their back, their spine can twist or hyperextend. This can lead to slipped discs or spinal cord damage. Conditions like Intervertebral Disc Disease (IVDD) are common in breeds like Dachshunds. Improper lifting can trigger or worsen these issues. A spinal injury can result in paralysis. It often requires expensive surgery.

Joint problems are another significant risk. Lifting a dog by their front legs can strain their shoulders. Lifting by their hind legs can damage their hips. Small dogs can suffer from luxating patellas. This is when the kneecap slips out of place. These injuries cause chronic pain. They can reduce your dog's mobility. They might need lifelong medication or surgical intervention.

Furthermore, improper lifting can lead to behavioral issues. A dog that experiences pain or fear when lifted might become fearful of touch. They might start growling or biting. This is a defensive reaction. It can damage the bond between you and your dog. They might avoid you. They might also become generally anxious or aggressive. If your dog has a history of aggression, even towards other dogs, improper handling can trigger or exacerbate these behaviors. Your safety is also at risk. A struggling or frightened dog can scratch or bite you. Large dogs can cause muscle strains or falls if they struggle. Always prioritize a safe lifting technique. This protects both you and your beloved companion from avoidable harm.

## Safe Lifting Techniques: The Right Way to Pick Up Your Dog

Knowing how should I not pick up my dog leads us to the correct methods. Proper lifting techniques ensure your dog's comfort and safety. They also protect you from injury. Always approach your dog calmly. Let them see you. Speak to them in a reassuring voice.

For small to medium dogs, use both hands. Place one hand under their chest, between their front legs. Place your other hand under their hindquarters. This supports their entire body. Keep their spine straight. Lift them smoothly and close to your body. This provides stability. It reduces strain on your back.

For puppies, be extra gentle. Their bones are still developing. Use the same two-hand method. Cradle them close to your chest. Puppies can be wiggly. Ensure a firm but gentle grip. Make the experience positive with soft words.

Large dogs often cannot be lifted entirely. Instead, use a two-person lift if possible. One person supports the chest. The other supports the hindquarters. If you must lift a large dog alone for a short distance (e.g., into a car), use a harness with handles. Alternatively, you can use a towel or blanket as a sling. Slide it under their belly. This provides even support. Lift with your legs, not your back.

Always gauge your dog's reaction. If they show signs of discomfort, stop. Reassess your method. Practicing safe techniques builds trust. It reinforces a positive association with being handled. Your dog will feel secure. They will be less likely to struggle or show fear. This is key to a happy, healthy relationship.

## Training and Socialization: Reducing Fear of Being Lifted

Training and socialization are powerful tools. They help your dog feel comfortable with being handled. This includes being picked up. A well-socialized dog is less likely to fear new experiences. Start early with puppies. Gently touch their paws, ears, and belly. Give treats and praise. Make these interactions positive.

Teach your dog a "cuddle" or "up" cue. This prepares them for the lift. Say the word. Then, gently guide them into position. Offer a treat. Perform a short, comfortable lift. Immediately put them down. Give another treat and praise. Repeat this often. Keep sessions short and positive.

Desensitize your dog to touch. Many dogs dislike having their feet or rear touched. These are often the areas we need to touch for lifting. Gradually increase touch in these sensitive areas. Always pair it with high-value treats. If they show any discomfort, stop. Go back to an easier step. The goal is to create a positive association.

Use positive reinforcement consistently. Never scold or punish your dog for struggling. This increases their fear. It makes them less likely to cooperate. Reward calm behavior. Reward them for allowing you to touch them. Reward them for staying still during a lift. This builds confidence.

Some dogs, especially those with a history of neglect or abuse, may have deep-seated fears. For these dogs, patience is critical. Consult a professional dog trainer or behaviorist. They can provide tailored guidance. They can help address specific fears. A positive experience reduces stress. It makes lifting a routine part of your dog's life. ## FAQ Section

### How do I know if I am hurting my dog when I pick them up?
Your dog will show clear signs of discomfort. They might yelp, whimper, or growl. They may also struggle, squirm, or try to jump out of your arms. Look for body language cues like a tucked tail, flattened ears, or a stiff body. Any of these signs mean you should stop and reassess your method.

### Can picking up my dog incorrectly cause long-term health problems?
Yes, absolutely. Improper lifting can cause serious long-term health problems. These include spinal injuries like slipped discs (IVDD), joint damage, and muscle strains. These conditions can lead to chronic pain, mobility issues, and even paralysis. Always use correct lifting techniques to protect their health.

### Is it ever okay to lift my dog by the scruff?
No, it is never appropriate to lift an adult dog by the scruff. While mother dogs might carry puppies this way, an adult dog is too heavy. Scruffing can cause severe neck pain, skin damage, and can be very frightening for your dog. This method is harmful and should be avoided entirely.

### What should I do if my dog is too heavy for me to lift safely?
If your dog is too heavy to lift safely alone, seek help. Use a two-person lift, with one person supporting the chest and the other the hindquarters. You can also use a sling, towel, or a specialized harness with handles to provide support. Prioritize your dog's safety and your own.

### How can I teach my dog to tolerate being picked up?
Start with positive reinforcement. Gently touch areas you would usually hold. Pair this with treats and praise. Introduce a verbal cue like "up." Practice short, comfortable lifts. Always reward calm behavior. Avoid punishment. If fear persists, consult a professional dog trainer for guidance.

### What if my dog bites me when I try to pick them up?
If your dog bites, it is a clear sign of extreme fear or pain. Do not try to force the lift. Immediately stop and put your dog down if you can do so safely. Seek professional help from a veterinarian or a certified dog behaviorist. They can help identify the cause and develop a safe training plan.
