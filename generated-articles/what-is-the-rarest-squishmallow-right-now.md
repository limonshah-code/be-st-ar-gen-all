What makes one Squishmallow more desirable than another? Rarity is not always simple. It involves several key elements. I have seen many factors influence a Squishmallow's status. These elements combine to create a truly rare item.

Many rare Squishmallows are exclusive. They are sold only at certain stores. Some appear only at conventions or specific events. This limited distribution makes them hard to find. Not everyone has access to these specific locations or events.

Prototypes are early versions of Squishmallows. They are often unique samples. These are sometimes given to employees. Prototypes are extremely rare. They were never meant for general sale. Their existence is very limited.

## How to Identify and Value a Rare Squishmallow

Identifying a truly rare Squishmallow requires a keen eye. It also needs some research. I always tell new collectors to do their homework. Correct identification ensures you get what you pay for. It also helps you avoid fakes.

### Key Authentication Features

*   **Swing Tag:** The swing tag is paramount. It should be crisp and securely attached. Check for misspellings or poor printing. Authentic tags have clear graphics and correct Squishmallow branding. The date and edition information can also be on this tag.
*   **Butt Tag (Sewn-in Tag):** This fabric tag is sewn into the Squishmallow. It contains manufacturing information. Check for the Kellytoy logo or Jazwares information. The material should feel high quality. Any signs of tampering or low-quality printing are red flags.
*   **Material Quality:** Authentic Squishmallows use specific, soft fabric. The stuffing should feel uniform and squishy. Fakes often use cheaper, rougher materials. They might also be under-stuffed or over-stuffed.
*   **Stitching:** Examine the stitching closely. It should be even and strong. There should be no loose threads or uneven seams. Quality craftsmanship is a hallmark of real Squishmallows.

### Researching Current Market Values

Market values for rare Squishmallows change. Online marketplaces are a good starting point. Look at completed sales on eBay. This shows what items actually sold for. Filter by "Sold Items" to see real transaction prices. This gives a realistic idea of value.

Price charting websites might also exist for collectibles. Join Squishmallow collector groups online. Members often share current selling prices. They also discuss market trends. Be cautious of asking prices that seem too good to be true. They often are.
