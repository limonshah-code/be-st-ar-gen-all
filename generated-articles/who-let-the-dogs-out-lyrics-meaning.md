The primary message of "Who Let The Dogs Out" is a critique of disrespectful male behavior. The "dogs" symbolize men who catcall or act inappropriately towards women. The song empowers women to question and challenge this behavior. It turns frustration into a collective, energetic response.

### Who sings "Who Let The Dogs Out"?
The Baha Men sing the most famous version of "Who Let The Dogs Out." They are a Bahamian band known for their energetic Junkanoo-style music. While they popularized the song globally, the original track was created by Trinidadian artist Anslem Douglas, titled "Doggie."

### When did "Who Let The Dogs Out" become popular?
"Who Let The Dogs Out" gained massive popularity in the year 2000. It quickly became a global hit, dominating radio airwaves and becoming an anthem at sporting events worldwide. Its infectious beat and memorable chorus made it an instant sensation, defining a moment in early 2000s pop culture.

### Is "Who Let The Dogs Out" a derogatory song?
No, "Who Let The Dogs Out" is not a derogatory song towards women. Instead, it acts as a commentary against derogatory male behavior. The "dogs" refer to men who act inappropriately. The song is a call from women asking who allowed these men to behave disrespectfully, effectively turning a critique into an empowering anthem.

### What does the "Woof woof" mean in the song?
The "Woof woof" in the song represents the "dogs'" (disrespectful men) acknowledgment of their behavior. It is a response to the women's question "Who Let The Dogs Out?"
