## The Real-Life Origins of War Dogs

The fascinating premise of *War Dogs* did not come from a screenwriter's imagination. It sprang from the pages of a *Rolling Stone* article by Guy Lawson titled "Arms and the Dudes." This piece, published in 2007, detailed the astonishing rise and fall of AEY Inc. Lawson's reporting exposed how two ambitious, yet largely unqualified, young men leveraged a government loophole. They ended up with massive military contracts during the Iraq and Afghanistan wars. This was a story ripe for a cinematic adaptation.

The government sought to open up military contracts to smaller businesses. This initiative, designed to encourage competition, created an unexpected opportunity. Efraim Diveroli, still a teenager, had already started AEY Inc. He brought his childhood friend, David Packouz, into the business. Together, they navigated the complex world of international arms dealing. Their story highlights a period where global conflicts created unusual pathways to immense wealth. The events truly captured public attention, questioning government oversight and procurement processes.

### AEY Inc.: The Company at the Center

AEY Inc. was Efraim Diveroli's creation. He established the company in 2004 when he was just 19 years old. Diveroli had prior experience in the arms industry through family connections. He knew how to find small contracts and bid on them. The company specialized in bidding on smaller, less desirable contracts. These contracts were often overlooked by larger, more established defense contractors. This strategy allowed AEY Inc. to grow quickly.

The company operated out of a modest office in Miami Beach. It was a stark contrast to the massive scale of their operations. AEY Inc. landed numerous contracts with the U.S. government. These contracts involved supplying various types of weaponry and ammunition. Many of these deals were for American allied forces in active war zones. The company's sudden success drew both admiration and scrutiny from the defense industry. This rapid expansion set the stage for their eventual downfall.

This ultimately led to charges against Diveroli and Packouz. Their dream of immense wealth ended in federal prison sentences.

Both Efraim Diveroli and David Packouz faced severe legal repercussions. Their actions led to federal charges related to fraud and conspiracy. The government took these violations very seriously. Girard represents various shadowy figures and intermediaries David Packouz and Efraim Diveroli encountered. These encounters took place during their arms dealing operations. The film condensed multiple interactions into one dramatic character. This change added to the movie's narrative tension.

The film captures the general personalities of David Packouz and Efraim Diveroli. Jonah Hill's Efraim is a good representation of his aggressive nature.
