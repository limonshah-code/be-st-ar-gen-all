### FAQ Section

#### Is the 7:1 dog to human year ratio completely wrong?
Yes, the 7:1 ratio is largely incorrect. It is an outdated myth. Dogs age much faster in their first year. Their aging rate then slows down. The ratio does not account for breed differences or the non-linear nature of canine aging. Modern science offers more accurate calculations.

#### How do you really calculate a dog's age in human years?
A better guideline is: the first dog year equals about 15 human years. The second year adds about 9 human years. Each subsequent year adds approximately 4-5 human years. Breed and size also affect this. Larger dogs generally age faster than smaller dogs.

#### Do all dog breeds age at the same rate?
No, dog breeds do not age at the same rate. Larger dog breeds tend to age faster and have shorter lifespans. Smaller dog breeds typically age slower and live longer. This difference is a key reason why the simple 7:1 ratio fails.

#### Why do small dogs live longer than large dogs?
Scientists are still researching this. One theory suggests rapid growth in large dogs. This quick growth may lead to faster cell aging and a higher risk of diseases. Genetic factors and breed-specific health issues also play a role in their shorter lifespans.

#### At what age is a dog considered a senior?
A dog is generally considered a senior when it reaches the last 25% of its estimated lifespan. For small breeds, this might be around 10-12 human years. For large or giant breeds, it could be as early as 5-7 human years. This stage varies greatly by breed.
