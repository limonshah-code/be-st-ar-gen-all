## Understanding Why Your Dog Has Bald Spots

Seeing patches of missing hair on your furry friend can be unsettling. You might wonder, "Why does my dog have bald spots?" These areas of hair loss, known as alopecia, are common in dogs and can signal various underlying issues. Identifying the cause is crucial for your dog's comfort and well-being. This article will explore the most common reasons your dog might be losing hair, from parasites and allergies to more complex medical conditions. We will also discuss how to address these concerns, helping you keep your dog's coat healthy and full.

### Takeaway

*   **Consult Your Vet:** Always see a veterinarian for accurate diagnosis and treatment.
*   **Check for Parasites:** Fleas, mites, and ticks are common causes of itching and hair loss.
*   **Monitor for Allergies:** Environmental, food, or contact allergies can lead to bald spots.
*   **Watch for Infections:** Bacterial or fungal issues like ringworm often cause patchy hair loss.
*   **Address Licking/Scratching:** Excessive self-grooming can create hot spots and baldness.

### Why does my dog have bald spots?

Your dog has bald spots because of underlying issues like parasites, allergies, infections, hormonal imbalances, or self-inflicted trauma. These conditions cause irritation, itching, or direct hair follicle damage, leading to localized or widespread hair loss. A vet can determine the exact cause and best treatment.

## Parasites: The Itchy Truth Behind Bald Spots

Parasites are a common cause when you notice your dog has bald spots. These tiny creatures can make your dog incredibly itchy. Constant scratching and biting lead to hair loss and skin irritation. Understanding which parasites cause this helps you protect your pet.

Fleas are a well-known culprit. Even a few fleas can cause intense itching for sensitive dogs. They bite, leading to allergic reactions in some pets. This reaction causes widespread scratching, resulting in bald patches, especially around the tail base, hind legs, and neck. Regularly checking for fleas and using preventative treatments helps.

Mites are another microscopic threat. Mange, caused by mites, leads to severe bald spots. Sarcoptic mange, or scabies, causes intense itching, redness, and crusting. Demodectic mange, often found in puppies or immunocompromised dogs, causes patchy hair loss, usually without intense itching, but it can worsen. Ear mites, while mainly in ears, can cause head scratching that affects surrounding fur.

Ticks are larger parasites you can see. While they don't usually cause widespread bald spots, a tick bite can cause localized irritation. If your dog chews at the site, it can create a small bald patch. Regular grooming checks and preventative measures are essential to keep these pests away. Effective parasite control is key to preventing many cases of hair loss.

### Common Parasitic Culprits

*   **Fleas:** Cause intense itching, leading to scratching and biting, especially in dogs with flea allergy dermatitis. Hair loss often appears on the hindquarters and inner thighs.
*   **Mites:**
    *   **Sarcoptic Mange (Scabies):** Highly contagious, causes severe itching, redness, scabs, and widespread hair loss on ear edges, elbows, hocks, and chest.
    *   **Demodectic Mange:** Often genetic or immune-related, causes patchy hair loss, sometimes redness and scaling, usually starting around the face and forelegs.
*   **Lice:** Less common, but cause itching and dull coat. Hair loss can occur from scratching.

Regular use of veterinarian-approved parasite preventatives is the best defense. If you suspect parasites, see your vet for proper diagnosis and treatment. Early intervention prevents further discomfort and widespread hair loss.

## Allergies: When Skin Reacts and Hair Falls

Allergies are a frequent reason for your dog to develop bald spots. Just like humans, dogs can be allergic to many things. These reactions cause intense itching, which leads to your dog scratching, licking, and chewing their skin. This self-trauma is what primarily causes hair to fall out.

Environmental allergies are very common. Dogs can react to pollen, dust mites, and mold spores. Symptoms often worsen seasonally, but some dogs suffer year-round. You might notice your dog scratching their paws, belly, ears, or around their eyes. The constant irritation leads to thinning fur or complete bald spots in these areas. Your vet can help identify specific allergens.

Food allergies are another possibility. Certain ingredients in your dog's diet, most commonly proteins like beef, chicken, or dairy, can trigger an allergic response. Skin symptoms often include widespread itching, ear infections, and gastrointestinal upset. Hair loss from food allergies can appear anywhere on the body due to generalized scratching. A limited ingredient diet or a prescription hypoallergenic diet is often used to diagnose and manage food allergies.

Contact allergies are less common but can also cause bald spots. These occur when your dog's skin comes into direct contact with an irritant. This could be certain cleaning products, fabrics, or even plants. The hair loss and skin irritation will be confined to the areas that touched the allergen. Removing the offending item is the first step in treatment. Managing allergies often requires a multi-faceted approach, combining medication, diet changes, and environmental control.

### Types of Allergic Reactions Causing Hair Loss

*   **Environmental Allergies (Atopy):** Reactions to airborne allergens like pollen, dust mites, and mold. Causes itching, licking, and chewing of paws, belly, and armpits. This constant action pulls out hair and creates bald spots.
*   **Food Allergies:** Immune response to certain food ingredients, most often proteins. Symptoms include generalized itching, skin inflammation, and recurrent ear infections, leading to widespread hair loss from scratching.
*   **Contact Dermatitis:** Skin reaction to direct contact with an irritating substance. Bald spots and redness appear only where the skin touched the allergen, such as specific plants, carpets, or cleaning agents.

If you observe your dog intensely scratching or licking, especially creating bare patches, consulting your vet is vital. They can perform tests and recommend treatments, such as antihistamines, steroids, or allergy shots, to manage your dog's comfort and restore their coat.

## Infections: Bacterial and Fungal Causes of Hair Loss

Infections, both bacterial and fungal, are significant culprits behind why your dog has bald spots. These infections can develop when your dog's skin barrier is compromised, or their immune system is weakened. They often cause itching, redness, and inflammation, leading to hair loss.

Bacterial infections, also known as pyoderma, are common. They can start when your dog scratches or bites at irritated skin, introducing bacteria. Hot spots, technically acute moist dermatitis, are a prime example. These are red, inflamed, often oozing lesions that appear suddenly and grow quickly. The intense irritation causes rapid hair loss in the affected area. Other bacterial infections can manifest as pustules, scabs, or widespread redness, all of which can lead to hair shedding. Antibiotics are typically needed to clear these up.

Fungal infections, such as ringworm, are also a frequent cause of bald spots. Despite its name, ringworm is caused by a fungus, not a worm. It creates circular patches of hair loss that often have a red, scaly, or crusty border. These lesions can be itchy and spread easily to other pets and even humans. Ringworm is often more common in puppies or dogs with weakened immune systems. Antifungal medications, both topical and oral, are used for treatment.

Yeast infections, primarily caused by *Malassezia*, can also contribute to hair loss. These often occur in warm, moist areas like ear canals, paw pads, or skin folds. The skin becomes greasy, red, and itchy, often with a distinct odor. Chronic yeast infections can lead to thickened, hyperpigmented skin and hair loss due to constant scratching. Medicated shampoos and antifungal treatments help manage yeast overgrowth. Prompt diagnosis and treatment are essential for resolving these infections and helping your dog's fur grow back.

### Recognizing Different Types of Skin Infections

*   **Bacterial Infections (Pyoderma):** Often secondary to other issues like allergies or parasites. Can appear as red bumps, pustules, scabs, or widespread inflammation. [Hot spots](https://beacleaner.com/what-do-hot-spots-look-like-on-dogs) (acute moist dermatitis) are rapidly developing, painful, and oozing lesions with quick hair loss. Treating hot spots often involves cleaning and medication. [How to get rid of hot spots on dogs](https://beacleaner.com/how-to-get-rid-of-hot-spots-on-dogs) explains various treatments.
*   **Fungal Infections (Ringworm):** Characterized by circular, scaly, crusty patches of hair loss. These lesions are often itchy and can spread easily. The skin within the patch may appear red or darkened.
*   **Yeast Infections (Malassezia Dermatitis):** Causes greasy skin, redness, itching, and a strong, unpleasant odor. Often found in skin folds, ears, and paw pads. Chronic cases can lead to thickened, dark skin and hair loss.

Treating these infections typically involves antibiotics, antifungals, or medicated shampoos, as prescribed by your vet. Early treatment prevents the spread of infection and allows the skin to heal, promoting hair regrowth.

## Hormonal Imbalances: Internal Factors Affecting Your Dog's Coat

Sometimes, the reason your dog has bald spots isn't an external irritant but an internal issue: hormonal imbalance. A dog's hormones play a crucial role in maintaining healthy skin and a shiny coat. When these hormones are out of balance, it can lead to noticeable hair loss without much itching.

Hypothyroidism is a common hormonal condition in dogs. This occurs when your dog's thyroid gland does not produce enough thyroid hormone. A slow metabolism results, affecting many body functions, including hair growth. Dogs with hypothyroidism often develop thinning fur or symmetrical bald spots, especially on their flanks and tail. Their skin might also become dry, flaky, and sometimes darken. Other symptoms include weight gain, lethargy, and a dull coat. A simple blood test can diagnose this condition.

Cushing's Disease, or hyperadrenocorticism, is another hormonal disorder causing hair loss. This condition results from an overproduction of cortisol by the adrenal glands. Dogs with Cushing's often display a "pot-bellied" appearance, increased thirst, and frequent urination. Their skin becomes thin and fragile, and they develop symmetrical hair loss on their body, typically sparing the head and legs. The hair loss is usually non-itchy. This disease can be complex to diagnose and manage but is treatable with medication.

Other hormonal influences can also affect a dog's coat. Sex hormone imbalances, though less common, can sometimes lead to hair loss, especially in unspayed or unneutered dogs. These types of hair loss are usually gradual and do not involve intense itching. Recognizing these internal causes is important because topical treatments for skin issues will not resolve them. Your veterinarian will need to perform specific blood tests to identify these hormonal conditions and prescribe the appropriate systemic medication.

### Key Hormonal Conditions Causing Alopecia

*   **Hypothyroidism:** Underactive thyroid gland leading to insufficient thyroid hormone production. Causes symmetrical hair loss, especially on the trunk and tail, often with dry, scaly skin. Dogs may also show lethargy and weight gain.
*   **Hyperadrenocorticism (Cushing's Disease):** Overproduction of cortisol. Leads to thin, fragile skin and symmetrical hair loss on the body, sparing the head and legs. Other signs include a pot-belly, increased thirst, and muscle weakness.
*   **Sex Hormone Imbalances:** Can cause patchy or generalized hair loss, typically in intact (unspayed/unneutered) dogs. Often affects the flanks and perineal region.

For hormonal causes, a vet diagnosis is crucial. Blood tests measure hormone levels to confirm the condition. Treatment involves medication to regulate hormone production, which can effectively resolve the hair loss and other symptoms.

## Hot Spots and Self-Trauma: The Cycle of Licking and Scratching

Hot spots are a common and frustrating reason why your dog might have bald spots. These rapidly developing skin lesions are formally known as acute moist dermatitis. They appear as red, inflamed, circular areas that are often oozing, painful, and intensely itchy. The hair loss associated with hot spots is directly caused by your dog's persistent licking, chewing, and scratching.

The cycle often begins with something minor: an insect bite, a small scratch, or even just matted fur. This initial irritation makes your dog start to lick or scratch the area. This constant self-trauma quickly breaks down the skin's barrier. Bacteria, which are always present on the skin, then proliferate in the moist, warm environment created by the licking. The infection makes the spot even itchier, prompting more licking and scratching. This vicious cycle causes the hot spot to grow rapidly in size, and the surrounding hair is lost as your dog grooms excessively.

Hot spots can appear almost anywhere on your dog's body, but they are most common on the head, neck, hips, and limbs. Breeds with thick coats or those prone to allergies, like Golden Retrievers or German Shepherds, are often more susceptible. Humidity and heat can also exacerbate their development.

Treating hot spots requires breaking this self-trauma cycle. This usually involves clipping the hair around the lesion to allow air circulation and cleaning the area with an antiseptic solution. Vets often prescribe topical creams, oral antibiotics, and anti-inflammatory medications. An Elizabethan collar (e-collar) is essential to prevent your dog from continuing to lick or chew the healing skin. Prompt action helps to clear the infection and prevent further spread, allowing the fur to grow back.

### How Self-Trauma Leads to Baldness

*   **Initial Irritation:** A minor itch, insect bite, or allergy triggers a dog to start licking or scratching a specific spot.
*   **Self-Inflicted Damage:** Persistent licking and chewing cause physical damage to the skin and hair follicles, leading to immediate hair breakage and loss.
*   **Bacterial Overgrowth:** The constant moisture from licking creates an ideal breeding ground for bacteria, leading to a secondary infection and the formation of a [hot spot on dogs](https://beacleaner.com/what-do-hot-spots-look-like-on-dogs).
*   **Intensified Itching:** The infection makes the area even more intensely itchy and painful, prompting the dog to lick and chew more, worsening the condition and expanding the bald area.
*   **Prevention and Treatment:** To [treat hot spots on dogs](https://beacleaner.com/how-do-you-treat-hot-spots-on-a-dog), you must stop the dog from licking. This involves trimming hair, cleaning, medication, and often using an e-collar. Prompt treatment is key to stop the spread and allow healing.

The most important step is to stop your dog from self-traumatizing the area. Your vet can provide medication to reduce itching and pain, along with protective measures. ## Nutritional Deficiencies and Stress: Hidden Causes of Alopecia

Sometimes, the reasons your dog has bald spots are less obvious, stemming from their diet or emotional state. Nutritional deficiencies and stress can both have a profound impact on your dog's coat health, leading to hair loss. These factors often go unnoticed until the bald patches appear.

A poor diet lacking essential nutrients can severely affect hair quality and growth. Hair is primarily made of protein, so if your dog's food lacks adequate protein or amino acids, their fur can become brittle and thin. Essential fatty acids, like Omega-3 and Omega-6, are also crucial for a healthy skin barrier and a shiny coat. Deficiencies in these fats can lead to dry, flaky skin, dull fur, and eventually hair loss. Vitamins and minerals, such as zinc and biotin, also play a vital role. If your dog is on a low-quality diet, or if they have an underlying condition preventing nutrient absorption, their coat will suffer. Feeding a complete and balanced diet, ideally a high-quality commercial dog food or a carefully prepared home-cooked meal under veterinary guidance, is key to preventing diet-related hair loss.

Stress and anxiety can also manifest as hair loss in dogs. Just like humans can lose hair due to stress, dogs can too. Chronic stress can lead to physiological changes that impact hair follicles. More commonly, however, stress leads to behavioral issues like excessive licking or chewing. A dog might incessantly lick their paws, belly, or flanks as a coping mechanism for anxiety. This repetitive action irritates the skin and physically removes hair, creating bald spots. Changes in environment, new pets, separation anxiety, or lack of enrichment can all contribute to a dog's stress levels.

Identifying and addressing the source of stress is crucial. This might involve behavior modification techniques, increased exercise, environmental enrichment, or, in some cases, anxiety-reducing medication. Both nutrition and stress are important but often overlooked factors contributing to your dog's skin and coat health. A holistic approach that includes a proper diet and a low-stress environment can make a significant difference in preventing and resolving hair loss.

### Addressing Underlying Nutritional and Stress Factors

*   **Nutritional Deficiencies:**
    *   **Protein and Amino Acids:** Hair is protein-rich. A diet low in quality protein leads to weak, brittle hair and poor growth.
    *   **Essential Fatty Acids (Omega-3 & Omega-6):** Crucial for skin barrier health and coat luster. Deficiencies cause dry skin, dull fur, and increased shedding.
    *   **Vitamins and Minerals:** Zinc and biotin are vital for healthy skin and hair. Insufficient levels can contribute to hair loss.
    *   **Solution:** Ensure your dog eats a high-quality, balanced diet. Consider supplements only under vet supervision.
*   **Stress and Anxiety:**
    *   **Behavioral Hair Loss:** Stressed dogs may lick, chew, or pull out their fur excessively as a self-soothing behavior. This creates bald spots, often on the paws, legs, or flanks.
    *   **Physiological Impact:** Chronic stress can impact hormone levels, which in turn affect hair follicle cycles.
    *   **Solution:** Identify and remove stressors. Provide a stable environment, regular exercise, mental stimulation, and consistent routines. Consult with a vet or a certified dog behaviorist for severe anxiety.

By ensuring good nutrition and managing stress, you can prevent these less obvious causes of bald spots. A healthy mind and body lead to a healthy coat.

## Genetic and Autoimmune Conditions: Inherited Hair Loss

Sometimes, your dog's bald spots are due to factors beyond their environment or simple infections. Genetic predispositions and autoimmune conditions can cause unique patterns of hair loss, often without itching or other typical skin issues. These are internal issues that require careful veterinary diagnosis.

Certain breeds are genetically predisposed to specific types of alopecia. For example, "Pattern Baldness" or "Follicular Dysplasia" affects breeds like Dachshunds, Greyhounds, and Doberman Pinschers. Hair loss typically occurs in specific areas like the ears, chest, and flanks, often starting in young adulthood. Another condition, Alopecia X, common in "plush-coated" breeds like Pomeranians and Alaskan Malamutes, results in progressive hair loss, leading to "puppy coat" or complete baldness on the trunk, while the head and legs remain furred. These conditions are not curable but can sometimes be managed with various treatments to stimulate hair growth. For example, [why is my Frenchie have a bald spot](https://beacleaner.com/why-is-my-frenchie-have-a-bald-spot) can be due to pattern baldness or other specific breed issues.

Autoimmune diseases occur when your dog's immune system mistakenly attacks its own healthy cells, including those involved in hair production. Pemphigus and Lupus are examples of such conditions. These can cause a range of skin issues, including blistering, crusting, and hair loss. The pattern of hair loss varies depending on the specific disease and can affect different parts of the body. These conditions are serious and often require lifelong management with immunosuppressive medications.

Endocrine disorders, which we touched upon earlier, also have a strong genetic component. Breeds like Golden Retrievers and Dobermans are more prone to hypothyroidism, which results in symmetrical hair loss. While not strictly genetic alopecia, the predisposition is inherited. Diagnosing genetic and autoimmune causes often involves skin biopsies, specialized blood tests, and a thorough medical history review. While these conditions can be challenging, understanding the cause is the first step toward effective management and ensuring your dog's quality of life.

### Inherited and Immune-Mediated Hair Loss

*   **Genetic Alopecia:**
    *   **Pattern Baldness:** Inherited hair loss in specific areas (e.g., ears, chest, thighs) in breeds like Dachshunds, Greyhounds, and Boxers. It is non-itchy and often symmetrical.
    *   **Alopecia X (Black Skin Disease):** Affects plush-coated breeds (e.g., Pomeranians, Alaskan Malamutes). Causes progressive hair loss on the trunk, often with darkened skin.
    *   **Follicular Dysplasia:** A group of disorders where hair follicles develop abnormally, leading to fragile, broken hair and bald spots. Often breed-specific.
*   **Autoimmune Skin Diseases:**
    *   **Pemphigus:** The immune system attacks skin cells, causing blisters, crusts, and hair loss.
    *   **Lupus (Discoid Lupus Erythematosus):** An autoimmune disorder primarily affecting the skin, often around the nose, eyes, and ears, leading to crusting, scaling, and hair loss.
    *   **Treatment:** Requires careful diagnosis through biopsies and blood tests. Management often involves long-term immunosuppressive therapy or other specific treatments to control the condition and reduce symptoms.

When your dog's bald spots don't fit the typical infectious or allergic picture, genetic or autoimmune factors might be at play. Consulting a veterinary dermatologist for these complex cases can provide specialized expertise and treatment options.

## Diagnosis and Treatment Options for Your Dog's Bald Spots

When your dog has bald spots, accurate diagnosis is the most important first step. Since many different conditions can cause hair loss, your veterinarian needs to act like a detective to find the root cause. Without a proper diagnosis, any treatment you attempt might be ineffective or even harmful.

Your vet will start with a thorough physical examination. They will look at the pattern of hair loss, examine the skin for redness, scaling, pustules, or parasites, and check for any signs of discomfort. They will also ask you many questions about your dog's history. This includes diet, lifestyle, exposure to other animals, previous skin issues, and any changes in behavior or overall health. This information helps them narrow down the possibilities.

Several diagnostic tests might be performed in-clinic. A skin scraping is a common procedure where your vet gently scrapes a small amount of skin to look for mites under a microscope. A "tape prep" or cytology involves pressing clear tape onto the skin or collecting samples with a swab to check for bacteria or yeast. A fungal culture might be done to test for ringworm, as it takes a few days for results. Blood tests are used to check for internal issues like hormonal imbalances (e.g., thyroid levels, cortisol levels). In some cases, a skin biopsy, where a small tissue sample is removed and sent to a lab, might be necessary for definitive diagnosis of complex conditions like autoimmune diseases or specific genetic alopecias.

Once a diagnosis is made, treatment will vary greatly. For parasites, topical or oral medications are usually prescribed. Allergies might require dietary changes, antihistamines, steroids, or allergy shots. Bacterial infections are treated with antibiotics, while fungal infections need antifungals. Hormonal imbalances often require lifelong medication. For self-trauma, preventing licking with e-collars and addressing the underlying itch is key. Sometimes, a combination of treatments is necessary. Your vet will create a specific treatment plan tailored to your dog's condition, helping them get back to a full, healthy coat.

### Veterinary Steps to Uncover the Cause

*   **Comprehensive Physical Exam:** Your vet assesses the bald patches, skin condition, and overall health, looking for clues like redness, scales, or scabs.
*   **Detailed History:** Questions about diet, lifestyle, allergies, and changes in behavior help narrow down potential causes.
*   **Skin Scrapings and Cytology:** Quick tests to identify mites, bacteria, or yeast under a microscope.
*   **Fungal Culture:** Used to diagnose ringworm, results typically take several days.
*   **Blood Tests:** Checks for hormonal imbalances (e.g., thyroid, cortisol) or other systemic issues.
*   **Skin Biopsy:** A small tissue sample sent to a lab for definitive diagnosis of complex conditions like autoimmune diseases or genetic alopecias.

### Tailored Treatment Approaches

*   **Parasites:** Specific antiparasitic medications (topical, oral).
*   **Allergies:** Dietary changes, antihistamines, steroids, immunotherapy.
*   **Infections:** Antibiotics for bacteria, antifungals for fungi, medicated shampoos.
*   **Hormonal:** Lifelong medication to regulate hormone levels.
*   **Self-Trauma:** E-collars to prevent licking, treating underlying itch.

Always follow your vet's advice and complete the full course of treatment. This ensures the best chance of recovery and prevents recurrence.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: Can stress cause my dog to have bald spots?
A1: Yes, stress can definitely contribute to your dog having bald spots. Chronic anxiety often leads to behavioral issues like excessive licking, chewing, or scratching specific areas. This self-trauma physically removes hair, creating bare patches. Stress can also weaken the immune system, making your dog more susceptible to skin infections that cause hair loss. Addressing the source of stress is crucial for their well-being and coat health.

### Q2: Are certain dog breeds more prone to bald spots?
A2: Yes, some dog breeds are more prone to bald spots due to genetic predispositions. Breeds like Dachshunds, Greyhounds, Boxers, and Doberman Pinschers can develop pattern baldness. Plush-coated breeds such as Pomeranians and Alaskan Malamutes are susceptible to Alopecia X. Additionally, breeds prone to allergies (e.g., Golden Retrievers, German Shepherds) or hormonal issues (e.g., Golden Retrievers, Dobermans for hypothyroidism) may also experience more frequent hair loss.

### Q3: How quickly do bald spots develop, and should I be worried?
A3: The speed at which bald spots develop varies. Hot spots can appear very rapidly, sometimes overnight, due to intense self-trauma. Hair loss from parasites or acute allergies might develop over days or weeks. Hormonal conditions often cause more gradual hair thinning over months. You should always be worried if your dog has a bald spot, especially if it's accompanied by redness, itching, pain, or changes in behavior. Consult your vet immediately for proper diagnosis and care.

### Q4: Can I treat my dog's bald spots at home?
A4: You should be careful with home treatments. While some mild cases might benefit from gentle cleaning or over-the-counter soothing products, it's best to consult a veterinarian first. Incorrect treatment can worsen the condition or delay proper diagnosis. For example, if it's a fungal infection, using an antibiotic cream won't help. A vet can accurately diagnose the cause and recommend safe, effective treatments, which might include specific shampoos, topical medications, or oral drugs.

### Q5: What is the difference between general hair loss and a hot spot?
A5: General hair loss, or alopecia, is a broad term for any missing hair. It can be diffuse thinning or distinct bald patches, often caused by allergies, parasites, or hormonal issues. A hot spot (acute moist dermatitis) is a specific type of localized hair loss. It appears as a rapidly developing, red, inflamed, oozing lesion caused by intense self-trauma (licking/chewing) and secondary bacterial infection. While hot spots *are* a form of hair loss, they are distinct in their rapid onset, appearance, and immediate cause.

### Q6: What should I do if my dog suddenly has a bald spot?
A6: If your dog suddenly has a bald spot, the first thing to do is to contact your veterinarian. Observe the spot closely: Is it red, itchy, scabby, or oozing? Is your dog licking or scratching it? Are there other symptoms like lethargy or loss of appetite? This information will be helpful for your vet. Avoid applying any unprescribed medications or home remedies, as they could interfere with diagnosis or irritate the skin further. Early vet intervention is crucial for effective treatment.
