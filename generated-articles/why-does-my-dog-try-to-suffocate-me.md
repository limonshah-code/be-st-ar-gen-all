## Territorial Instincts and Resource Guarding (of You)

While often loving, a dog's desire to lay on you can also have a subtle territorial component. Dogs mark their territory with their scent. When your dog lies on you, they are transferring their scent onto you. This tells other animals, and even other people, that you are "theirs." It is a gentle assertion of belonging. They are not being aggressive; they are simply making a statement.

This behavior is often seen as a form of "resource guarding." You are a valuable resource to your dog. By lying on you, they are claiming you. This is especially true if you have multiple pets. My dog might push another dog away to get closer to me. This shows they want to secure their spot right next to their favorite human. It is a natural instinct to protect valuable things.

### Is It Dominance?

The idea that a dog lying on you is a sign of dominance is a common misconception. In most cases, it is not about dominance over you. It is about a desire for closeness, comfort, and security. True dominance in dogs involves resource control, not simply physical proximity. A dog that lies on you is typically seeking warmth and affection. They do not aim to control your actions or assert a higher social status.

However, if your dog growls, snaps, or prevents you from moving when they are on you, this could be a sign of resource guarding that needs addressing. This is distinct from simple affectionate "suffocation." ## When "Suffocation" Becomes a Problem: Addressing the Behavior

While your dog trying to suffocate you is often a loving gesture, there are times when the behavior becomes problematic. Maybe your dog is too heavy, making it hard to breathe or causing discomfort. Perhaps they interfere with your sleep or your ability to move around. It is okay to set boundaries. You can gently teach your dog that there are appropriate times and places for cuddling. Your comfort matters too.

Start by consistently redirecting your dog. When they try to lie on you at an inconvenient time, gently move them to their own bed or a designated spot nearby. Reward them with praise and a treat when they settle in their correct spot. Use a clear command like "place" or "bed." This teaches them where they should be. Consistency is key for them to understand the new rules. You are not rejecting their affection; you are teaching manners.

### Consulting a Professional Trainer or Vet

If the "suffocation" behavior is persistent, disruptive, or accompanied by other concerning signs, consider seeking professional help. A certified dog trainer can offer personalized strategies. They can help you understand the root cause of the behavior. Sometimes, medical issues can also influence a dog's clinginess. A vet can rule out any underlying health problems. They ensure your dog is comfortable and healthy.

Teaching basic obedience commands can also help manage the behavior. A dog that understands and respects commands like "off" or "stay" is easier to redirect. ## FAQ Section

### Is it bad if my dog tries to suffocate me?
No, generally it is not bad. When your dog tries to "suffocate" you by lying on you, it is typically a loving gesture. They are showing affection, seeking comfort, and feeling secure in your presence. It reinforces your bond. Only worry if it causes you discomfort or if your dog shows aggression when you try to move them.

### How can I get my dog to stop sleeping on my chest?
You can gently redirect your dog by moving them to a designated, comfortable spot nearby, such as their bed. Use a verbal cue like "off" or "place" and reward them when they settle in their own space. Consistency is vital. Offer plenty of praise and treats for choosing their bed.

### Does my dog sleeping on me mean they are dominant?
In most cases, no. A dog sleeping on you is usually a sign of affection, trust, and a desire for security and warmth. The old idea of dominance as a primary driver for such behaviors is largely outdated. They simply want to be close to their favorite person.

### What if my dog only "suffocates" me and not other family members?
If your dog only lies heavily on you, it often indicates you are their primary caregiver or their favorite person. They have a particularly strong bond with you. This can also mean they feel the most secure and comfortable in your presence compared to others in the household.

### Are certain dog breeds more prone to this behavior?
Yes, some dog breeds, especially companion breeds like many toy dogs, are naturally inclined to be "lap dogs" and seek constant physical closeness. Larger breeds can also be very cuddly. Individual personality and early socialization also play a big role in this behavior.

### When should I worry about my dog lying heavily on me?
You should worry if the behavior becomes uncomfortable or painful for you, or if your dog growls, snaps, or acts aggressively when you try to move them. Also, if the clinginess is new and sudden, it could indicate underlying anxiety or a medical issue. Consult a vet or professional trainer in these situations.
