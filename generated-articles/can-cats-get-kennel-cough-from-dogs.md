### Can Cats Get Kennel Cough From Dogs? The Direct Answer

Yes, cats can get kennel cough from dogs. The primary pathogen involved in canine kennel cough, Bordetella bronchiseptica, is a bacteria. This bacterium is capable of infecting cats. While it is less common in felines, cross-species transmission does occur. Cats exposed to infected dogs can develop respiratory symptoms. This often presents as an upper respiratory infection.

## What Exactly is Kennel Cough? A Look at Its Components

Kennel cough, known medically as canine infectious tracheobronchitis, is a highly contagious respiratory disease in dogs. It gets its name because it spreads quickly wherever dogs gather. These places include kennels, dog parks, and grooming facilities. People often think it is caused by one specific pathogen. This is not entirely accurate.

Kennel cough is a complex disease. Multiple agents can cause it. The most common culprits are the bacterium Bordetella bronchiseptica and several viruses. These viruses include parainfluenza virus and canine adenovirus. Sometimes, canine herpesvirus can also be involved. When dogs breathe in these infectious agents, they can infect the trachea and bronchi. This leads to inflammation and characteristic coughing. Understanding these components helps us see why it can affect cats.

Bordetella bronchiseptica is the key player here. It is a bacterium. It can live in the respiratory tracts of various animals. Dogs are its primary host for kennel cough. But this bacterium is not exclusive to dogs. It can cross the species barrier. Cats are one species that can become infected. Other animals, like rabbits, can also get it. This widespread host range makes it a concern in multi-pet households.

The other common agents, like canine parainfluenza virus, are typically species-specific. This means they are less likely to jump to cats. For instance, you will not find a cat getting sick from canine distemper virus. However, Bordetella is different. Its adaptable nature allows it to colonize the respiratory systems of both dogs and cats. This is why it is the main reason **cats can get kennel cough from dogs**. Keep this bacterium in mind when considering risks.

When we talk about kennel cough, we are discussing a syndrome. It is not just one bug. A dog with kennel cough might have Bordetella, a virus, or both. The combination often makes symptoms worse. For cats, the risk primarily comes from Bordetella. ## The Link: How Cats Get Kennel Cough From Dogs

Understanding how infections spread is key to prevention. When **cats get kennel cough from dogs**, it usually involves direct exposure to airborne droplets. Dogs with kennel cough sneeze and cough. These actions release tiny particles into the air. These particles contain the Bordetella bronchiseptica bacteria. Cats breathing in these particles can then become infected.

Direct contact is another common transmission route. Dogs might lick or groom their cat housemates. If the dog is shedding the bacteria, it can transfer directly to the cat. Sharing food bowls, water dishes, or bedding can also facilitate spread. The bacteria can survive for a short time on surfaces. A cat coming into contact with these contaminated items can pick up the pathogen. This makes a shared environment a risk factor.

The bacteria primarily target the respiratory system. Once inhaled, Bordetella attaches to the lining of the cat's trachea and bronchi. It then multiplies, causing inflammation. This inflammation leads to the characteristic symptoms of a respiratory infection. The cat's immune system tries to fight off the invaders. This battle causes the cat to cough, sneeze, and often develop a runny nose. The same process happens in dogs, but feline symptoms can be milder.

Cats in multi-pet households face a higher risk. If your dog comes home from boarding or a dog park with kennel cough, your cat is automatically exposed. There is no absolute barrier between species in a home environment. Even if they do not directly interact, airborne transmission is a strong possibility. This is why isolating an infected dog is a wise step.

Immune status plays a big role. Young kittens, senior cats, or cats with compromised immune systems are more vulnerable. They might experience more severe symptoms. Healthy adult cats might fight off the infection more easily. They could even become carriers without showing strong signs. Early recognition of dog symptoms helps protect your cat.

## Identifying Kennel Cough Symptoms in Cats

Recognizing symptoms in your cat is vital. While dogs often show a distinct "honking" cough, symptoms in cats can be less obvious. They often mimic a common upper respiratory infection, or "cat cold." If your cat has been around a dog with kennel cough, be extra vigilant. Look for any changes in their breathing or behavior.

Common signs of Bordetella infection in cats include:

*   **Sneezing:** This is one of the most frequent symptoms. Cats will often sneeze repeatedly.
*   **Coughing:** While not always the harsh, dry cough of a dog, cats can develop a soft, moist cough. It might sound like they are trying to clear their throat.
*   **Runny Nose:** Clear or yellowish discharge from the nostrils is common.
*   **Watery Eyes:** Your cat's eyes might appear teary or have a mild discharge.
*   **Lethargy:** Infected cats may seem less energetic. They might sleep more and play less.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Illness can reduce a cat's desire to eat. This can lead to weight loss if prolonged.
*   **Fever:** Your cat might feel warm to the touch. This is a sign their body is fighting an infection.
*   **Difficulty Breathing:** In severe cases, cats might show labored breathing or open-mouth breathing. This requires immediate veterinary attention.

It is important to note that these symptoms are not unique to Bordetella. Other feline respiratory viruses cause similar signs. Feline herpesvirus and feline calicivirus are examples. A veterinarian can distinguish between these conditions. Early detection in cats is important for successful treatment. Do not delay if you suspect your cat is unwell.

## Diagnosing and Treating Feline Kennel Cough

If you suspect your cat has a respiratory infection, a vet visit is the next step. Diagnosing Bordetella in cats involves a physical examination and sometimes specific tests. Your veterinarian will listen to your cat's lungs. They will check for fever and other signs of illness. It is important to tell your vet if your cat has been exposed to a dog with kennel cough. This information guides the diagnostic process.

Diagnostic tests might include a nasal swab or a tracheal wash. These samples are sent to a lab. The lab can then culture the bacteria. This confirms the presence of Bordetella bronchiseptica. Sometimes, PCR tests are used. These tests detect the genetic material of the bacteria. These methods help rule out other causes of respiratory issues in cats. Getting an accurate diagnosis ensures the right treatment.

Treatment for feline Bordetella typically involves antibiotics. Bordetella is a bacterium, so antibiotics are effective. Your vet will prescribe a course of antibiotics. It is crucial to administer the full course, even if your cat seems better. Stopping early can lead to a relapse. It also contributes to antibiotic resistance. The veterinarian might also prescribe supportive care.

Supportive care for a cat with kennel cough may include:

*   **Humidifiers:** A humidifier can help ease congestion. It makes breathing more comfortable.
*   **Nasal Decongestants:** In some cases, your vet might recommend specific decongestants.
*   **Fluid Therapy:** If your cat is not eating or drinking, fluids might be needed. This prevents dehydration.
*   **Good Nutrition:** Encourage your cat to eat. Offer palatable, strong-smelling foods. This helps maintain their strength.
*   **Rest:** Provide a quiet, comfortable place for your cat to rest and recover.

Never give human medications or dog medications to your cat without vet approval. Cats metabolize drugs differently. What is safe for a dog or human can be toxic to a cat. Always follow your vet's instructions closely. Prompt and appropriate treatment helps your cat recover quickly. ## Preventing Cross-Species Transmission in Your Home

Preventing your cat from catching kennel cough from your dog is possible. Implementing smart preventive measures greatly reduces the risk. Proactive steps keep all your pets healthy. It also minimizes stress for you. I always tell clients that prevention is better than cure, especially in a multi-pet home.

Here are key strategies for prevention:

1.  **Vaccination:** There is a Bordetella vaccine available for cats. It targets the same bacterium that affects dogs. If your dog is often exposed to other dogs, consider vaccinating your cat. This is especially true if your cat is young, old, or has a weakened immune system. Discuss this option with your veterinarian. They will help you decide if it is right for your cat.
2.  **Isolate Sick Pets:** If your dog develops kennel cough, separate them from your cat immediately. Confine the infected dog to a specific room. This room should have minimal shared air circulation with the rest of the house. Use separate food and water bowls. Wash bedding frequently. This limits the spread of airborne particles.
3.  **Practice Good Hygiene:** Wash your hands thoroughly after handling an infected dog. This prevents transferring bacteria to your cat. Clean and disinfect shared items. This includes toys, pet carriers, and common areas. Use pet-safe disinfectants to kill bacteria on surfaces.
4.  **Manage Environmental Exposure:** Reduce your dog's exposure to kennel cough if possible. If your dog frequently visits crowded places, they are at higher risk. Consider the risks of dog parks or boarding kennels. Vaccinate your dog against Bordetella if they socialize often. This protects both your dog and, by extension, your cat.
5.  **Monitor Both Pets:** Keep a close eye on both your dog and cat for any signs of illness. If your dog starts coughing, act quickly. 6.  **Maintain Strong Immune Systems:** A healthy diet and regular exercise support a strong immune system in both pets. Ensure they get all necessary nutrients. This helps their bodies fight off infections more effectively. A robust immune system is the first line of defense against any pathogen.

## Distinguishing Feline Kennel Cough from Other Cat Respiratory Issues

When your cat coughs or sneezes, it is natural to worry. Is it kennel cough, or something else? Many conditions cause similar respiratory symptoms in cats. It is crucial to understand these differences. This helps you communicate effectively with your vet. It ensures your cat gets the correct diagnosis and treatment.

Feline Upper Respiratory Infections (URIs) are very common. They are often called "cat colds." Viruses typically cause these. The main viral culprits are:

*   **Feline Herpesvirus (FHV-1):** This virus is widespread. It causes sneezing, runny eyes, nasal discharge, and sometimes mouth ulcers. Cats often carry FHV-1 for life. Stress can trigger flare-ups.
*   **Feline Calicivirus (FCV):** Symptoms include sneezing, nasal discharge, conjunctivitis, and oral ulcers. Some strains can cause lameness or severe systemic disease.

Both FHV-1 and FCV are highly contagious among cats. They do not typically spread to dogs. This is a key difference from Bordetella. If you have multiple cats, these viral infections can sweep through the household quickly.

Other causes of feline respiratory signs include:

*   **Allergies:** Cats can develop allergies to pollen, dust, or certain foods. This leads to sneezing, coughing, or asthma-like symptoms.
*   **Asthma:** Feline asthma causes difficulty breathing, coughing, and wheezing. It is a chronic inflammatory condition of the airways.
*   **Foreign Objects:** Sometimes, a cat might inhale a foreign object. This could be a blade of grass or a piece of food. This can cause sudden coughing or choking.
*   **Dental Disease:** Severe dental infections can sometimes cause nasal discharge. This happens if the infection spreads to the sinuses.
*   **Fungal Infections:** Less common, but certain fungal infections can cause respiratory symptoms in cats.

Bordetella infection in cats can resemble these conditions. The specific diagnosis requires veterinary expertise. Your vet will consider your cat's symptoms, exposure history, and possibly diagnostic tests. If your cat has been exposed to a dog with kennel cough, mention it. This vital piece of information helps your vet narrow down the possibilities. Knowing the distinction helps your cat get better quickly.

## FAQ Section

### Can a cat get a dog's cough?

Yes, a cat can get a dog's cough, particularly if the cough is caused by the bacterium Bordetella bronchiseptica. This bacteria is a common agent in canine kennel cough. It can cross the species barrier to infect cats. Cats typically develop symptoms similar to an upper respiratory infection.

### Is Bordetella contagious from dogs to cats?

Bordetella bronchiseptica is contagious from dogs to cats. Dogs carrying the bacteria can transmit it to cats through airborne droplets when they cough or sneeze. Direct contact and shared contaminated items can also spread the infection. This makes multi-pet households a risk factor.

### What are the symptoms of kennel cough in a cat?

Symptoms of kennel cough (Bordetella infection) in cats include sneezing, a mild cough, runny nose, and watery eyes. Cats might also show lethargy, loss of appetite, and a fever. These signs often resemble a common feline cold.

### How do I treat kennel cough in my cat?

Treating kennel cough in cats requires veterinary consultation. Your vet will likely prescribe antibiotics to target the Bordetella bacteria. Supportive care such as humidifiers, good nutrition, and rest can also help. Always follow your vet's specific instructions for medication and care.

### Can cats get sick from dog viruses?

Generally, viruses that cause dog illnesses are species-specific and do not infect cats. However, bacteria like Bordetella bronchiseptica, a cause of kennel cough, can affect both species. Cats typically catch only the bacterial component of kennel cough from dogs.

### Should I separate my cat if my dog has kennel cough?

Yes, you should separate your cat if your dog has kennel cough. Isolate the infected dog to prevent the spread of Bordetella bronchiseptica. Use separate food/water bowls and avoid shared spaces. Practice good hygiene to protect your cat from cross-infection.
