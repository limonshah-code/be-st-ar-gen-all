## Exploring the Biggest German Shepherds: What Are They Called?

Many people admire German Shepherds for their intelligence, loyalty, and protective nature. Some individuals find themselves drawn to an even larger version of this impressive breed. You might wonder if there's a specific name for these extra-large dogs. I understand this curiosity, as the sheer size of some German Shepherd-type dogs is truly striking.

This article will answer the question: **What are the biggest German Shepherds called?** We will explore the specific breeds and types known for their larger stature. I will discuss their origins, distinct characteristics, and how they compare to a standard German Shepherd. Understanding these larger variations helps you appreciate the diversity within this canine family. We will also cover important aspects of caring for these magnificent, larger dogs.

### What are the biggest German Shepherds called?

The biggest German Shepherds are primarily known as **King Shepherds**. This is a distinct, large breed developed from combining German Shepherds with other giant breeds. While standard German Shepherds can vary in size, the King Shepherd was specifically bred for greater size and stature. They represent the largest of the German Shepherd-type dogs.

## Introducing the King Shepherd: A Majestic Large Breed

When we talk about the biggest German Shepherds, the King Shepherd stands out immediately. This breed is not just a larger German Shepherd; it is a separate, recognized breed with its own unique traits. King Shepherds possess a truly commanding presence. Their size and powerful build make them unmistakable.

The development of the King Shepherd began in the United States during the 1990s. Breeders aimed to create a larger, calmer, and healthier companion dog. They wanted to combine the best qualities of several large breeds. The result is a dog that embodies majesty and strength. I find their history fascinating, as it speaks to a deliberate effort to enhance specific desired characteristics.

King Shepherds are notably larger than the average German Shepherd. Males can weigh up to 150 pounds and stand over 30 inches tall at the shoulder. Females are also substantial, often exceeding 100 pounds. This impressive size requires a strong, confident owner. Despite their size, they are known for their gentle and loving nature within their family. They are truly gentle giants.

### The Origins of the King Shepherd Breed

The King Shepherd breed developed from a carefully planned breeding program. Breeders crossed German Shepherds with other large working breeds. These breeds include the Great Pyrenees, Alaskan Malamute, and sometimes the Akita. The goal was to produce a dog with the intelligence of a German Shepherd but with greater size, a calmer temperament, and improved health.

This crossbreeding introduced new genetic lines, which helped achieve their desired characteristics. For instance, the Great Pyrenees contributed to the King Shepherd's impressive size and often its thick coat. Alaskan Malamutes added to the robustness and endurance of the new breed. This careful selection ensured the King Shepherd inherited desirable traits from each parent breed. It is a testament to thoughtful breeding practices.

The founders, David Turkheimer and Shelley Watts-Cross, are credited with much of the breed's initial development. They focused on creating a dog that was not only large but also sound in body and mind. This dedication ensured the King Shepherd grew into the magnificent animal it is today. Their vision created a dog that truly embodies a regal presence.

## Key Characteristics: Size, Temperament, and Appearance

King Shepherds possess distinct characteristics that set them apart. Their size is the most obvious trait. They are considerably larger and more heavily built than typical German Shepherds. This makes them truly stand out in any crowd.

### Impressive Size and Structure

Male King Shepherds often stand between 27 to 31 inches tall at the shoulder. They can weigh anywhere from 90 to 150 pounds. Females are slightly smaller but still very large, typically 25 to 29 inches tall and weighing 75 to 110 pounds. Their build is robust and muscular, giving them a powerful look. I am always amazed by their sheer physical presence. This substantial frame needs a well-balanced diet and regular exercise to stay healthy. Their large size makes them less agile than some smaller breeds.

### Gentle and Loyal Temperament

Despite their imposing size, King Shepherds are renowned for their gentle and calm temperament. They are highly intelligent, making them easy to train with consistent methods. They are fiercely loyal to their families and make excellent protective companions. They often get along well with children and other pets when properly socialized from a young age. Their calm demeanor makes them suitable for family life. I find their blend of strength and gentleness truly appealing. This balance is what makes them such beloved family members.

### Distinctive Coat and Appearance

King Shepherds come in two coat varieties: long and rough-coated. Both types feature a dense double coat that protects them from various weather conditions. Common coat colors include sable, black and tan, black and cream, and bi-color. Their appearance is noble and majestic, reflecting their German Shepherd heritage mixed with the grandeur of the other giant breeds. Their large, expressive eyes and erect ears contribute to their alert and intelligent expression. A King Shepherd’s presence is always felt, drawing attention wherever they go.

## Comparing King Shepherds to Standard German Shepherds

Understanding the differences between King Shepherds and standard German Shepherds helps clarify why "King Shepherd" is the answer to our main question. While they share common ancestry, key distinctions exist. These differences go beyond just size and touch upon various aspects of the dog. I often get asked how they truly compare.

### Size and Build Differences

The most noticeable difference is size. Standard German Shepherds typically weigh 50-90 pounds for males and 45-70 pounds for females. Their height ranges from 22-26 inches. As we discussed, King Shepherds far exceed these measurements, often doubling the weight of a smaller German Shepherd. Their bones are also heavier, giving them a more substantial frame. This increased mass means they require more space and more food. I always emphasize this point to potential owners.

### Temperament and Energy Levels

While both breeds are intelligent and loyal, King Shepherds are often described as having a calmer and more laid-back temperament. Standard German Shepherds, especially those from working lines, can be intensely driven and have very high energy levels. King Shepherds still need regular exercise but might not demand the same intensity of activity as some standard German Shepherds. Their calmer nature makes them potentially more suitable for homes seeking a giant, gentle companion. However, proper socialization and training are crucial for both breeds.

### Health Considerations and Lifespan

Both breeds are prone to certain health issues, such as hip and elbow dysplasia. Due to their larger size, King Shepherds can have an increased risk for conditions like bloat (gastric torsion). Responsible breeding practices for King Shepherds aim to reduce these genetic predispositions. Their lifespan is comparable, typically 10-11 years, though giant breeds sometimes have slightly shorter lifespans than medium-sized dogs. Regular veterinary check-ups are vital for both. I believe proper preventative care is key to a long, healthy life for any dog.

## Other Large German Shepherd Variants: American vs. Working Lines

While King Shepherds are the largest specific breed, it is worth noting that some standard German Shepherds also reach impressive sizes. These variations often depend on their specific breeding lines. The German Shepherd breed itself has diverged into different types over time. I find it important to understand these distinctions.

### American Show Line German Shepherds

American Show Line German Shepherds are often bred for a specific appearance that is popular in dog shows. They tend to be larger than their German Working Line counterparts. These dogs often have a more sloped back and a more refined, elegant look. While they are not as massive as King Shepherds, they can still be quite substantial. Breeders focus on aesthetics and a generally calmer temperament for the show ring. Their size can sometimes approach the lower end of King Shepherd measurements.

### German Working Line German Shepherds

German Working Line German Shepherds are bred primarily for their working abilities, such as police work, protection, and search and rescue. These dogs are known for their high drive, intelligence, and athleticism. They are typically very muscular and powerfully built, though not always the absolute largest in terms of sheer mass. However, some individual working line dogs can be quite large and imposing. Their focus is on performance and temperament for demanding jobs. I admire their unwavering focus and dedication.

### European and Canadian German Shepherds

Other regional variations exist. European lines, particularly those adhering to German standards, often prioritize working ability and a more moderate size compared to the extreme slop found in some American lines. Canadian lines can also produce very large individuals. These variations highlight the diversity within the German Shepherd breed globally. Each line has its own fan base and distinct characteristics. The genetic pool is quite wide.

## Caring for a Large German Shepherd: Diet, Exercise, and Health

Owning a large German Shepherd-type dog, such as a King Shepherd, brings unique responsibilities. Their size impacts almost every aspect of their care. Proper nutrition, adequate exercise, and vigilant health monitoring are crucial. I always tell owners that preparation is key for these magnificent animals.

### Essential Nutritional Requirements

Large breed dogs, especially puppies, require specialized diets. Puppy food for large breeds helps manage their rapid growth rate. This prevents bone and joint problems later in life. Adult King Shepherds need high-quality dog food formulated for large breeds. This provides the necessary protein and nutrients without excessive calories. Obesity can exacerbate joint issues, so portion control is important. I always recommend consulting a veterinarian for specific dietary advice. Fresh water should always be available.

### Adequate Exercise and Mental Stimulation

Despite their calmer demeanor, King Shepherds still need regular exercise. Daily walks, playtime in a secure yard, and mental challenges are vital. They are intelligent dogs and can become bored or destructive without enough stimulation. Activities like obedience training, puzzle toys, and interactive games keep their minds engaged. Their joints are sensitive due to their size, so avoid high-impact activities, especially when they are young. I find that a good balance of physical and mental activity keeps them happy and healthy.

### Common Health Concerns and Preventative Care

Large breeds like King Shepherds are prone to certain health conditions. Hip and elbow dysplasia are significant concerns, as is bloat. Regular veterinary check-ups are essential for early detection and management of these issues. Maintaining a healthy weight and providing joint supplements (under vet guidance) can help. Responsible breeders screen their dogs for genetic health problems. I believe choosing a reputable breeder is the first step in ensuring your dog's long-term health. Regular grooming is also necessary to maintain their thick double coat.

## Is a King Shepherd Right for You? Considerations for Ownership

Deciding to bring a King Shepherd into your home is a big commitment. These dogs are magnificent, but they require significant resources and dedication. It is important to consider if your lifestyle and living situation are suitable for such a large and powerful animal. I encourage thorough reflection before making this decision.

### Space and Environment

King Shepherds need ample space. A small apartment is not ideal for these giant dogs. They thrive in homes with a large, securely fenced yard where they can move freely. Inside, they need room to stretch out and maneuver comfortably. Their size means they can accidentally knock things over, so a spacious home is beneficial. Consider the impact of a 100+ pound dog living in your house. I always picture them needing a grand space to match their grand personality.

### Time Commitment and Training

These intelligent dogs thrive on interaction and training. They need consistent, positive reinforcement training from a young age. Early socialization is crucial to ensure they grow into well-adjusted adults. King Shepherds form strong bonds with their families and do not do well when left alone for long periods. They require a significant time commitment for exercise, training, grooming, and companionship. I often tell people they are not just pets; they are family members requiring active participation.

### Financial Investment

Owning a King Shepherd involves a substantial financial investment. Their purchase price can be high due to their rarity and breeding efforts. Beyond that, their sheer size means higher costs for food, veterinary care (medications are often dosed by weight), grooming supplies, and larger equipment like crates, beds, and leashes. Emergency vet bills can also be more expensive. I always advise potential owners to budget carefully for all aspects of their care. This ensures you can provide for them throughout their lives.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How big can a King Shepherd get?
Male King Shepherds can stand 27 to 31 inches tall at the shoulder and weigh 90 to 150 pounds. Females are slightly smaller, typically 25 to 29 inches tall and weighing 75 to 110 pounds. Their impressive size makes them one of the largest dog breeds. They have a powerful, muscular build.

### Are King Shepherds aggressive?
No, King Shepherds are generally not aggressive. They are known for their calm, gentle, and loyal temperament. They are protective of their families but not inherently aggressive. Proper socialization and training from an early age are essential to ensure they grow into well-adjusted adults. They are typically good with children and other pets.

### What is the lifespan of a large German Shepherd?
The lifespan of a large German Shepherd, including King Shepherds, is typically 10 to 11 years. Like many giant breeds, they may have a slightly shorter lifespan compared to smaller dogs. Good nutrition, regular exercise, and consistent veterinary care can help maximize their lifespan. Responsible breeding also plays a key role in their health.

### Do King Shepherds shed a lot?
Yes, King Shepherds have a dense double coat and are known to be heavy shedder. They shed year-round, with heavier shedding periods often occurring seasonally. Regular grooming, including brushing several times a week, is necessary to manage their shedding and keep their coat healthy. Daily brushing during peak shedding times is often needed.

### Are King Shepherds good family dogs?
Yes, King Shepherds can be excellent family dogs. They are loyal, protective, and known for their gentle nature, especially with children. Their calm demeanor makes them well-suited for family life. Early socialization, consistent training, and enough space are vital for them to thrive as family pets. They form strong bonds with their human companions.

### How much exercise does a King Shepherd need?
King Shepherds need a moderate amount of daily exercise. This includes daily walks, playtime in a securely fenced yard, and mental stimulation. Aim for at least 30-60 minutes of physical activity each day. Avoid overly strenuous or high-impact activities, especially when they are young, to protect their developing joints. Mental challenges are also important for their intelligent minds.
