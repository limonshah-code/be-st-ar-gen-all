## Understanding How Nexgard Works Against Fleas

Nexgard is an oral medication. You give it to your dog as a flavored chew. This makes administration easy for most pet owners. The active ingredient in Nexgard is Afoxolaner.

Afoxolaner is an insecticide and acaricide. It targets the nervous system of fleas and ticks. Once your dog ingests Nexgard, the medication enters their bloodstream. Fleas must bite your dog to be exposed to Afoxolaner. They ingest the active ingredient as they feed. This exposure quickly overstimulates their nervous system. The fleas lose control and die. This is how Nexgard kills fleas already on dog.

Nexgard provides systemic protection. This means the active ingredient spreads throughout your dog's body. It does not wash off during baths or swimming. This ensures continuous protection for a full month. The unique action means fleas cannot develop resistance easily. The consistent dose helps maintain flea control.

### How Afoxolaner Targets Fleas

Afoxolaner works by blocking chloride channels in the insect's nervous system. These channels are vital for normal nerve function. When blocked, the fleas experience uncontrolled neurological activity. This leads to paralysis and death. This targeted action is very effective against fleas and ticks. It is also safe for dogs when used as directed. The compound specifically binds to insect receptors. It has a much lower affinity for mammalian receptors. This contributes to its safety profile for dogs.

### Addressing the Flea Life Cycle

The flea life cycle has four stages: egg, larva, pupa, and adult. Only adult fleas live on your dog. Eggs, larvae, and pupae are found in the environment. Nexgard breaks this cycle by killing adult fleas. This prevents them from laying new eggs. However, existing eggs and pupae in your home can still hatch.

You might see "new" fleas appearing for several weeks. These are not fleas Nexgard failed to kill. Instead, they are new adults emerging from pupae in your home. These new fleas jump onto your dog. They bite, ingest Nexgard, and then die. This is part of the clean-up process. It can take several months of consistent treatment to fully clear a severe environmental infestation. Serious side effects are rare. However, they can occur. These might include seizures, tremors, or ataxia (loss of coordination). If you observe any severe or unusual symptoms, contact your vet immediately. It is crucial to get professional medical advice right away. Your vet can determine if the symptoms relate to Nexgard. They can recommend appropriate action.

## FAQ Section: Common Questions About Nexgard and Fleas

### Q: How fast does Nexgard start killing fleas already on a dog?
A: Nexgard is very fast-acting. It begins killing fleas within 4-8 hours of administration. Studies show it reaches near 100% effectiveness within 24 hours. This rapid action provides quick relief for dogs with existing flea infestations.

### Q: Will I still see fleas after giving my dog Nexgard?
A: You might still see some fleas for a few weeks after the initial dose. These are likely new fleas hatching from eggs or pupae in your home environment. These new fleas will jump onto your dog, bite, and then quickly die because of the Nexgard in your dog's bloodstream.

### Q: Does Nexgard kill flea eggs or larvae?
A: No, Nexgard specifically targets and kills adult fleas and ticks after they bite your dog. It does not directly kill flea eggs or larvae in the environment. However, by killing adult fleas, it prevents them from laying new eggs, thereby breaking the flea life cycle.

### Q: Can I bathe my dog after giving Nexgard?
A: Yes, Nexgard is an oral medication, so it works systemically within your dog's body. Bathing, swimming, or grooming does not affect its efficacy. You can bathe your dog at any time without reducing the effectiveness of the flea treatment.

### Q: How often should I give my dog Nexgard?
A: Nexgard should be given monthly, every 30 days, to ensure continuous protection against fleas and ticks. Consistent, year-round administration is crucial for effective flea prevention and control, even if you don't currently see fleas on your dog.

### Q: What should I do if my dog vomits after taking Nexgard?
A: If your dog vomits shortly after taking Nexgard, especially within an hour or two, contact your veterinarian. They may advise you on whether to give another dose or monitor your dog. Giving Nexgard with a meal can sometimes help reduce digestive upset.
