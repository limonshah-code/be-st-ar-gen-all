### FAQ Section

**Q1: Can I own a monkey as a pet in California?**
A1: No, owning a monkey as a pet is illegal in California. Monkeys are considered restricted exotic animals under state law. They require special permits typically issued only to zoos, research facilities, or licensed exhibitors. Private individuals cannot obtain these permits for personal pet ownership.

**Q2: Are there any exotic birds I can legally own in California?**
A2: Yes, many species of exotic birds are legal to own in California. Common examples include parrots, parakeets, cockatiels, and finches. However, certain wild native birds and some large, dangerous exotic birds may be restricted. Always verify with the CDFW if you are unsure about a specific species.

**Q3: Is it possible to get a permit for a unique snake or reptile?**
A3: Some non-venomous snakes and reptiles are legal in California without a permit, such as corn snakes and bearded dragons. However, venomous snakes, large constrictor snakes (like pythons over a certain length), and dangerous reptiles like alligators are prohibited. Permits for these are extremely rare and for institutional use only.

**Q4: What if I already own an exotic animal and move to California?**
A4: If you move to California with an exotic animal that is illegal in the state, you face legal consequences. You cannot simply "grandfather" in an illegal pet. It is best to contact the CDFW *before* moving. They can advise you on legal alternatives or proper surrender procedures to avoid penalties.

**Q5: Are hybrid animals, like wolf-dogs, legal in California?**
A5: Hybrid animals like wolf-dogs (wolf-hybrids) are generally illegal to own in California. The state views them as wild animals or derivatives of wild animals. Their possession is restricted. This is due to safety concerns and the unpredictable nature of wild animal genetics.
