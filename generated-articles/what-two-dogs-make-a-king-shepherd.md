## What Two Dogs Make A King Shepherd

Have you ever seen a dog so impressive it makes you stop and stare? I know I have. The King Shepherd is one such breed. Its regal presence, massive size, and calm demeanor capture attention. Many people wonder about the origins of such a magnificent animal. Today, we will explore the fascinating story of the King Shepherd. We will uncover which specific dog breeds contributed to its creation.

This article will explain the King Shepherd's parentage. We will also look at the breed's history and the goals of its developers. Understanding their unique characteristics helps you appreciate them more. We will discuss their temperament, physical traits, and specific care needs. By the end, you will know if a King Shepherd is the right companion for your home.

### Takeaway:
*   The King Shepherd results from combining German Shepherd lines with giant guardian breeds.

### What two dogs make a King Shepherd?

The King Shepherd is not a direct first-generation cross of just two specific dogs. Instead, it is a selectively bred composite breed. It primarily combines the German Shepherd with several large guardian breeds. These breeds include the Akita and the Great Pyrenees. Alaskan Malamute lines also play a role in their ancestry. Breeders carefully selected these different dogs to achieve the King Shepherd's unique traits.

### The Royal Lineage: Unpacking the King Shepherd's Ancestry

The King Shepherd’s lineage began with a clear purpose. Breeders wanted to create a larger, more gentle German Shepherd type. They aimed for a dog with a calmer temperament and fewer health issues. This goal led them to combine the beloved German Shepherd with other powerful dog breeds. The result is the impressive King Shepherd we see today.

The primary foundation breed is the German Shepherd. This breed provides intelligence, loyalty, and trainability. German Shepherds are famous for their working ability. They are excellent in various roles, from police work to family protection. However, some German Shepherd lines experienced health problems and temperament shifts. This prompted the search for a new, improved breed. Breeders wanted to retain the German Shepherd's best qualities. They also wished to enhance size and overall robustness.

To achieve the desired size and a more relaxed disposition, breeders introduced giant guardian breeds. The Akita is one significant contributor. Akitas bring strength, a dense coat, and a calm, dignified nature. They are known for their loyalty to their families. Another key breed is the Great Pyrenees. This breed offers immense size, a thick double coat, and a gentle guardian instinct. Great Pyrenees dogs are historically flock protectors. They possess a patient and watchful temperament. Sometimes, Alaskan Malamute lines are also integrated. Malamutes contribute their substantial size, power, and endurance. Their arctic heritage also adds to the King Shepherd's impressive coat.

The careful blending of these breeds was not a simple task. It involved many generations of selective breeding. Breeders chose dogs with the best traits from each line. They focused on health, size, temperament, and appearance. This thoughtful process helped establish the King Shepherd as a distinct and formidable breed.

### The Visionaries Behind the Breed: Development and Goals

The King Shepherd breed came into existence in the United States. It was the vision of two American breeders, Shelley Watts-Cross and David Turk. Their work started in the late 1980s. They shared a common goal: to create a new type of dog. This dog would possess the intelligence and loyalty of the German Shepherd. It would also have the impressive size and gentle nature of giant breeds.

Their idea was to produce a superior companion dog. They wanted a dog that was physically sound and mentally stable. The aim was to move away from some health issues and extreme temperaments seen in certain purebred lines. Watts-Cross and Turk carefully selected foundation dogs. They began cross-breeding German Shepherds with various large breeds. The specific breeds chosen were the Akita and the Great Pyrenees. Sometimes, the Alaskan Malamute was also part of this early genetic pool.

The development process involved meticulous record-keeping. Breeders tracked health, temperament, and physical traits. They only allowed dogs showing desired qualities to reproduce. This selective breeding was crucial. It ensured the King Shepherd would consistently display its characteristic traits. They wanted a dog that was not just big but also proportionate and athletic. The vision included a dog with a calm, confident, and protective temperament.

The goal was to produce a dog with a striking appearance. This appearance would combine the German Shepherd’s noble head and strong body with the larger size and dense coat of its giant ancestors. They aimed for a family-friendly dog. This dog would be protective yet gentle with children. The breed's developers successfully created a dog that truly embodies a "kingly" presence. Their dedication established the foundation for this impressive breed.

### King Shepherd Characteristics: Size, Temperament, and Appearance

The King Shepherd stands out among dog breeds. Its characteristics reflect the careful blending of its ancestors. When you see one, its sheer size immediately catches your eye. These dogs are truly majestic.

#### Size and Physical Traits

King Shepherds are genuinely giant dogs. Males typically stand between 27 to 31 inches tall at the shoulder. They can weigh anywhere from 110 to 150 pounds or even more. Females are slightly smaller, usually 25 to 29 inches tall. Their weight ranges from 90 to 120 pounds. Their build is solid and powerful. They have a strong, muscular body. Their gait is graceful and athletic despite their large size.

They possess a dense double coat. This coat protects them from various weather conditions. It comes in a range of colors and patterns. Common colors include black and tan, sable, and bi-color. Their coats require regular grooming to stay healthy. They have a proud head and strong jaws. Their ears are typically erect, like those of a German Shepherd. Their eyes convey intelligence and alertness.

#### Temperament and Personality

The King Shepherd’s temperament is a hallmark of the breed. They are known for being calm, confident, and very loyal. They possess a natural protective instinct for their families. However, this protectiveness is usually tempered with a gentle disposition. They are generally good with children and other pets if properly socialized from a young age. My own experience with large dogs shows that early exposure to different situations is key.

These dogs are intelligent and eager to please. This makes them highly trainable. They respond well to positive reinforcement methods. Harsh training can damage their trust and lead to fear. A King Shepherd forms strong bonds with its family. It thrives on being an active part of daily life. They do not enjoy being left alone for long periods. They can become destructive or anxious without enough interaction. They need a consistent leader. This leadership should be firm yet fair. Understanding their strong drive to protect helps owners manage their behavior well.

### Living with a King Shepherd: Care and Training Needs

Bringing a King Shepherd into your home is a big commitment. These dogs are magnificent, but they require significant care. Their size alone dictates many aspects of their daily routine. Proper care ensures they live a happy, healthy life.

#### Exercise Requirements

King Shepherds need regular exercise. They are large and have a working dog heritage. Daily walks or runs are essential. I find that a good hour of activity each day helps keep them balanced. This can include brisk walks, playtime in a securely fenced yard, or even hiking. Mental stimulation is just as important as physical activity. Puzzle toys and training sessions keep their intelligent minds engaged. Without enough exercise, they can become bored or restless. This can lead to unwanted behaviors like chewing or excessive barking.

#### Grooming and Health

Their dense double coat requires consistent grooming. I typically brush my large dogs several times a week. This helps prevent matting and reduces shedding. King Shepherds shed heavily, especially during seasonal changes. Regular brushing also distributes natural oils and keeps their skin healthy. Occasional baths are necessary, but not too frequent. Over-bathing can strip essential oils from their skin and coat. Nail trims, ear cleaning, and dental care are also vital. Start these routines early in life to get your King Shepherd comfortable with them.

#### Training and Socialization

Training a King Shepherd should begin early. These dogs are intelligent and can learn quickly. Basic obedience commands are a must. They need to understand what you expect from them. Early socialization is crucial for giant breeds. Expose your puppy to many different people, places, sounds, and other animals. This helps them grow into well-adjusted adults. Without proper socialization, their protective instincts can become a problem. They might become overly wary or fearful of new experiences. Attending puppy classes is an excellent way to socialize your King Shepherd. It also strengthens your bond. Remember, a well-trained and socialized King Shepherd is a joy to live with.

### Health and Longevity: Understanding Common King Shepherd Issues

Every dog breed has potential health concerns. The King Shepherd is no exception. Understanding these issues helps owners provide the best possible care. This proactive approach can lead to a longer, healthier life for your pet. Breeders aimed to create a healthier dog. They carefully selected foundation stock to minimize inherited conditions. However, some risks remain due to their large size and ancestral lines.

#### Common Health Concerns

Like many large breeds, King Shepherds are prone to certain orthopedic problems. Hip and elbow dysplasia are conditions that affect the joints. These can cause pain and mobility issues. Responsible breeders screen their dogs for these conditions before breeding. This helps reduce the incidence in puppies. Bloat, also known as gastric torsion, is another serious concern for deep-chested dogs. It is a life-threatening condition where the stomach twists. Owners must know the symptoms and seek immediate veterinary care if bloat is suspected.

Eye conditions, such as progressive retinal atrophy, can also affect King Shepherds. Regular eye exams by a veterinary ophthalmologist can help detect these issues early. Heart conditions and certain types of cancer are also possible, as with most breeds. Staying informed about your dog's health history is important. Discuss any concerns with your veterinarian.

#### Promoting Longevity

The average lifespan of a King Shepherd is typically 10 to 11 years. Some individuals may live longer with excellent care. Proper nutrition is fundamental for their health. Feed a high-quality diet appropriate for large breed dogs. This diet supports healthy growth and maintains their ideal weight. Obesity can worsen joint problems and other health issues. Regular veterinary check-ups are vital. These appointments allow for preventative care and early detection of problems.

Exercise, as discussed earlier, contributes significantly to their overall well-being. Keeping them physically fit and mentally stimulated helps them thrive. Maintaining a clean living environment and practicing good hygiene also prevents infections. Providing a loving home reduces stress. This also plays a role in their longevity. Ultimately, a King Shepherd's health is a partnership between the owner and the veterinarian.

### Is a King Shepherd Right for You? Making an Informed Decision

Deciding to bring any dog into your family is a significant choice. This decision becomes even more important with a large and powerful breed like the King Shepherd. I always advise potential owners to consider their lifestyle carefully. A King Shepherd requires a specific environment and dedicated commitment.

#### Lifestyle Considerations

First, consider your living space. King Shepherds need ample room. An apartment living situation is generally not ideal for this breed. They thrive in homes with large, securely fenced yards. This allows them to stretch their legs and play freely. Daily outdoor activity is essential. If you lead a sedentary lifestyle, a King Shepherd might not be the best fit. They need an active owner who can provide consistent exercise.

Time commitment is another critical factor. These dogs form strong bonds with their families. They require significant attention and interaction. They do not do well when left alone for extended periods. If your work schedule keeps you away from home for most of the day, consider if you can meet their needs. They need playtime, training sessions, and companionship. I believe quality time spent with your dog strengthens your bond and improves their behavior.

#### Experience and Training Ability

King Shepherds are intelligent and trainable. However, their size and strength mean they need an owner with some experience. First-time dog owners might find managing a large, powerful breed challenging. Consistent training and early socialization are non-negotiable. If you are not confident in your ability to train a large dog, consider professional help. A well-trained King Shepherd is a pleasure. An untrained one can be difficult to control.

Financial considerations also play a role. Larger dogs generally cost more to feed. Veterinary care for a giant breed can also be more expensive. This includes medications, surgeries, and preventative treatments. Think about these financial aspects before committing to a King Shepherd. Owning a King Shepherd is a rewarding experience. They are loyal, protective, and loving companions. However, it is a commitment that requires careful planning and dedication.

### FAQ Section

#### What is the typical size of a King Shepherd?
Male King Shepherds are large, standing 27-31 inches tall and weighing 110-150 pounds. Females are slightly smaller, 25-29 inches tall and 90-120 pounds. Their impressive size is a key characteristic of the breed.

#### Is the King Shepherd a good family dog?
Yes, King Shepherds can be excellent family dogs. They are loyal, protective, and generally good with children. Early socialization and consistent training are necessary for them to thrive in a family environment.

#### Do King Shepherds shed a lot?
Yes, King Shepherds have a dense double coat and are known to shed significantly. They shed moderately year-round and heavily during seasonal changes. Regular brushing helps manage the shedding.

#### What kind of exercise does a King Shepherd need?
King Shepherds require daily exercise. This includes brisk walks, runs, or playtime in a securely fenced yard. They need at least an hour of activity each day. Mental stimulation is also important for their intelligent minds.

#### Are King Shepherds easy to train?
King Shepherds are intelligent and eager to please, making them generally easy to train. They respond best to positive reinforcement methods. Consistent training and early socialization are key to developing a well-behaved dog.

#### What is the average lifespan of a King Shepherd?
The average lifespan for a King Shepherd is typically 10 to 11 years. Proper nutrition, regular exercise, and consistent veterinary care can help them live a long and healthy life.
