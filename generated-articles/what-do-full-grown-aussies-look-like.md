## Discovering the Look of Full Grown Aussies

Australian Shepherds, fondly known as Aussies, capture hearts with their intelligent gaze and vibrant coats. If you have ever wondered **what do full grown Aussies look like**, you are in the right place. These dogs possess a distinctive appearance that sets them apart. We will explore their size, build, stunning coat colors, and captivating eye variations.

Understanding the unique characteristics of a full grown Aussie helps owners and enthusiasts appreciate their beauty. This guide provides a comprehensive look at their physical traits. We cover everything from their athletic physique to their unique facial expressions. Join me as we uncover the true aesthetic of these wonderful dogs.

### Takeaway: Key Features of a Full Grown Aussie

*   **Size:** Medium-sized, athletic build, typically 40-65 pounds and 18-23 inches tall.
*   **Coat:** Medium-length, double coat, often merle, bi-color, or tri-color, requiring regular grooming.
*   **Eyes:** Known for striking blue, amber, brown, or heterochromatic (bi-colored) eyes.
*   **Expression:** Intelligent, alert, and keen, reflecting their working dog heritage.
*   **Tail:** Can be naturally bobbed or have a full length, often historically docked.

### What do full grown Aussies look like?

Full grown Aussies present a striking, athletic appearance. They have a medium build, a beautiful medium-length double coat, and often showcase unique merle patterns. Their expressive eyes can be blue, amber, brown, or multi-colored, and they carry an intelligent, alert demeanor. This combination creates a truly distinctive and handsome dog.

### Understanding the Standard Full Grown Aussie Size and Build

When we talk about **what full grown Aussies look like**, their size and build are a primary feature. These dogs are considered medium-sized, balancing strength with agility. Their physique reflects their heritage as active herding dogs. They need a strong body to perform their demanding tasks.

An adult Australian Shepherd carries a well-muscled and balanced frame. This structure allows for quick changes in direction and sustained endurance. They are not overly bulky, but rather lean and powerful. I always admire their graceful movement, which speaks volumes about their athletic capabilities.

#### Height and Weight Ranges

Full grown Aussies generally fall within a specific size range. Males typically stand taller and weigh more than females. A male Aussie usually measures between 20 to 23 inches at the shoulder. Their weight can range from 50 to 65 pounds. Females are slightly smaller, standing 18 to 21 inches tall. They typically weigh between 40 to 55 pounds.

These numbers are general guidelines, and individual dogs might be slightly outside these ranges. Diet, genetics, and activity levels all influence a dog's final size. We always ensure our Aussies get the right nutrition to support their growth.

#### Athletic and Agile Physique

The body of a full grown Aussie is a testament to its working background. They possess a deep chest and a straight, strong back. Their legs are muscular and well-angulated, providing powerful propulsion. This physical design makes them exceptional at running, jumping, and herding.

Their agility is truly remarkable to watch. They move with purpose and grace, easily navigating various terrains. This athletic build also makes them excellent companions for active families. Whether hiking, playing fetch, or participating in dog sports, their bodies are built for action. It's fascinating to see how perfectly adapted they are for their original purpose. ### The Signature Coat: Colors and Patterns of Adult Aussies

The coat is perhaps the most defining feature when considering **what full grown Aussies look like**. Their medium-length double coat is not only beautiful but also functional. It offers protection against various weather conditions, from heat to cold. The texture is usually straight to wavy, never curly, with feathering on the legs and britches.

Grooming a full grown Aussie's coat requires regular attention. Their double coat needs brushing several times a week to prevent mats and reduce shedding. I spend time each week brushing my Aussie, and it helps keep her coat looking fantastic. It also helps distribute natural oils, keeping their skin healthy. This maintenance contributes significantly to their overall appearance.

#### The Striking Merle Pattern

Many full grown Aussies exhibit the captivating merle pattern, which creates patches of diluted color. This pattern is perhaps the most iconic for the breed. Blue merle and red merle are the two primary merle variations. A blue merle Aussie has patches of black, gray, and white. A red merle Aussie has patches of red, liver, and cream.

Each merle Aussie has a unique pattern, making no two dogs look exactly alike. This genetic trait is truly fascinating and contributes to their striking appearance. It is crucial to understand merle genetics, as breeding two merle dogs can lead to health issues in puppies. We discuss the importance of responsible breeding practices often. Breeders must avoid breeding two merle dogs together, a practice known as "double merle" breeding. #### Bi-Color and Tri-Color Variations

Beyond merle, full grown Aussies also come in solid bi-color and tri-color coats. A bi-color Aussie will have two distinct colors, typically black and white or red and white. These colors appear in defined patches, often with white on the chest, muzzle, and paws. Tri-color Aussies have three distinct colors. They combine black or red with white and tan (copper) markings.

The tan markings usually appear on the eyebrows, cheeks, and legs. These tri-color patterns are also very popular and equally beautiful. My own Aussie is a tri-color, and her copper points truly highlight her expressive face. Each color variation adds to the diversity of the breed. Beyond solid colors, full grown Aussies frequently exhibit heterochromia, meaning they have two different colored eyes. For example, one eye might be blue, and the other brown or amber. Sometimes, an Aussie might even have "parti-colored" eyes. This means one eye contains multiple colors, such as a swirl of blue and brown within a single iris. ### Tail Variations in Full Grown Australian Shepherds

The tail is another characteristic that helps define **what full grown Aussies look like**. Unlike many breeds with a standard tail length, Aussies show significant variation. Some Aussies are born with a naturally short tail, known as a natural bobtail (NBT). Other Aussies are born with full, long tails.

Historically, many Australian Shepherds had their tails docked. This was common practice for working dogs to prevent injury while herding livestock. Today, tail docking is a controversial practice. Many countries have banned it. The decision to dock a tail depends on local laws and personal preferences.

#### Natural Bobtail Trait

A natural bobtail is a genetic trait in Australian Shepherds. Dogs born with an NBT have a tail that ranges from virtually absent to a few inches long. This trait is part of the breed standard. It does not cause any discomfort or health issues for the dog. I have met several NBT Aussies, and their unique look is quite charming.

It is important for owners to know if their Aussie has an NBT or if their tail was docked. This information helps understand the dog's lineage and any potential genetic implications. An NBT is a fascinating example of genetic diversity within the breed.

#### Historical Docking Practices

For many years, it was customary to dock an Aussie's tail shortly after birth. This practice was believed to protect the tail from injuries that could occur while working in rough terrain or with livestock. A full tail could get stepped on or caught. The docked tail became a common appearance for the breed.

Today, ethical considerations often outweigh historical working reasons. Many breeders and owners prefer to leave naturally long tails intact. When you see a full grown Aussie with a very short tail, it could be either a natural bobtail or a docked tail. The long, feathered tail of an undocked Aussie is also very beautiful.

### Understanding the Difference: Aussies vs. Other Breeds

When discussing **what full grown Aussies look like**, it's helpful to differentiate them from similar-looking breeds. People sometimes confuse Australian Shepherds with other herding dogs. While some breeds share characteristics, Aussies have distinct features. Understanding these differences clarifies their unique appearance.

A common point of confusion arises from the name itself. Many wonder if Australian Shepherds are actually from Australia. In fact, they developed in the western United States, primarily to herd sheep. Their name likely comes from their association with Basque shepherds who arrived from Australia with their sheep.

#### Aussies vs. Australian Cattle Dogs

One frequent confusion is between Australian Shepherds and Australian Cattle Dogs. While both are herding breeds, their appearances differ significantly. Full grown Australian Cattle Dogs have a stockier build and a much shorter, smoother coat. Their coats are typically blue or red speckle/mottle patterns. They do not have the medium-length double coat or the merle patterns commonly seen in Aussies.

Australian Cattle Dogs also have prick ears, meaning they stand upright. Aussie ears typically fold over. Their facial structures also differ, with Cattle Dogs having a broader head. Understanding [if Aussies and Australian Cattle Dogs are the same](https://beacleaner.com/are-aussies-and-australian-cattle-dogs-the-same) helps clarify these distinct breeds.

#### Aussie vs. Border Collie Similarities

Another comparison often made is between Australian Shepherds and Border Collies. Both are highly intelligent herding dogs. They share some coat colors, but a full grown Aussie usually has a slightly more robust build. Border Collies often appear leaner and have a finer bone structure. Their coats can also vary, with Border Collies having rough or smooth varieties.

The most notable distinction often lies in their heads and expressions. Aussies tend to have a slightly broader head and a more direct gaze. Border Collies often have a more intense "herding stare." ### FAQ Section

#### Q1: How tall are full grown Aussies?
Full grown Australian Shepherds are medium-sized dogs. Males typically stand 20 to 23 inches tall at the shoulder. Females are slightly smaller, generally ranging from 18 to 21 inches in height. These measurements define their athletic and balanced build, reflecting their working dog heritage.

#### Q2: What is the most common coat color for an adult Aussie?
The most iconic and common coat patterns for an adult Aussie are the merle variations. This includes blue merle and red merle. These patterns feature patches of diluted color over a base coat, often with white and tan (copper) markings. Bi-color and tri-color coats are also very common and equally striking.

#### Q3: Do all full grown Aussies have blue eyes?
No, not all full grown Aussies have blue eyes. While blue eyes are a well-known trait, especially in merle-coated dogs, Aussies can have a variety of eye colors. These include brown, amber, green, or a combination of colors (heterochromia), where one eye is blue and the other is a different color.

#### Q4: What is a "Mini Aussie" and how big do they get?
A "Mini Aussie" is a Miniature Australian Shepherd, a distinct breed developed to be smaller than the standard Aussie. Full grown Mini Aussies typically stand 14 to 18 inches tall and weigh between 20 to 40 pounds. They retain all the classic appearance and energetic temperament of their larger relatives.

#### Q5: Are full grown Aussies high-energy dogs?
Yes, full grown Aussies are inherently high-energy dogs. They were bred for herding and possess a strong work drive. They require significant daily exercise, including vigorous play, long walks, or running. Mental stimulation through training and puzzle toys is also crucial to keep them happy and well-behaved.

#### Q6: At what age do Aussies stop growing?
Most Australian Shepherds reach their full height and weight by 12 to 18 months of age. While their rapid growth phases largely conclude by one year, some may continue to fill out in muscle and chest depth until they are about two years old. Toy and Mini Aussies generally mature a bit faster.
