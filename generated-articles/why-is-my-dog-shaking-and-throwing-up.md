## Why Is My Dog Shaking and Throwing Up?

Worry hits hard when your dog acts unwell. One moment, they are fine. The next, your dog is shaking and throwing up. These symptoms are clear signs of distress. It is important to know what these actions mean. I know how scary this can feel as a pet owner.

This article helps you understand why your dog might be shaking and throwing up. We explore common causes, from minor upsets to serious conditions. We also discuss when you need immediate vet care. My goal is to give you clear steps and peace of mind. Let us make sure your furry friend gets the care they need.

### Takeaway

When your dog is shaking and throwing up, immediate action is important.
*   **Observe your dog closely:** Note details like frequency, vomit content, and other symptoms.
*   **Evaluate the severity:** Determine if symptoms are mild or severe.
*   **Restrict food and water:** Offer small amounts of water later if no more vomiting occurs.
*   **Contact your vet quickly:** Seek professional advice for persistent or severe symptoms.
*   **Prevent future issues:** Focus on a consistent diet and safe environment.

When your dog is shaking and throwing up, it signals a problem. This might be a mild stomach upset or a serious medical emergency. Common causes include dietary indiscretion, infections, organ disease, or toxin exposure. Observe other symptoms and contact your veterinarian quickly. They can diagnose the issue and provide appropriate treatment for your dog's health.

### Immediate Actions and When to See Your Vet

Seeing your dog shaking and throwing up is alarming. Your first reaction might be panic. However, staying calm helps you assess the situation. Observe your dog's other symptoms.

You need to act quickly. Your dog’s well-being depends on your decisions. Knowing when to call the vet is crucial. Sometimes, waiting too long can make things worse.

**What to Do Immediately**

When your dog starts shaking and vomiting, take specific steps. First, remove food and water bowls. This prevents further stomach upset. Keep your dog in a quiet, comfortable space.

Monitor them closely for new symptoms. Check for weakness, lethargy, or repeated vomiting. These signs point to a serious issue. If your dog is shaking, you can find more guidance on [what should I do if my dog is shaking](https://beacleaner.com/what-should-i-do-if-my-dog-is-shaking).

*   **Observe Vomit:** Note its color, consistency, and if it contains blood or foreign objects.
*   **Check Hydration:** Gently lift your dog’s lip and touch their gums. They should feel moist. Pinch the skin on their back. It should snap back quickly.
*   **Monitor Energy Levels:** Lethargy combined with vomiting is a red flag.
*   **Check for Pain:** Watch for whining, guarding their abdomen, or difficulty moving.

**When to Seek Urgent Veterinary Care**

Some situations require immediate vet attention. Do not delay if you see certain warning signs. These include persistent vomiting, blood in vomit, or severe lethargy. Your dog’s life might depend on fast care.

Always consult your vet if symptoms worsen. A professional diagnosis ensures proper treatment. This keeps your dog safe and healthy. *   **Blood in Vomit:** Any trace of fresh or digested blood (looks like coffee grounds).
*   **Severe Lethargy or Weakness:** Your dog cannot stand or move easily.
*   **Abdominal Pain:** Your dog cries or guards their belly when touched.
*   **Known Toxin Ingestion:** If you suspect your dog ate something harmful.
*   **Bloating:** A swollen, hard abdomen, especially if accompanied by retching.
*   **Gum Color Changes:** Pale, blue, or yellow gums are serious.
*   **High or Low Body Temperature:** Use a rectal thermometer if comfortable.

### Common Medical Reasons for Dog Shaking and Throwing Up

When a dog shakes and throws up, it often points to an underlying medical issue. Many conditions can cause these combined symptoms. Some are minor, others are severe. Understanding these common causes helps you provide better information to your vet. This speeds up diagnosis and treatment.

**Gastrointestinal Distress**

Stomach problems are a frequent cause. Dogs often eat things they should not. This can lead to an upset stomach. Vomiting clears the irritant. Shaking might come from pain or nausea.

*   **Dietary Indiscretion:** Eating spoiled food, trash, or foreign objects. This irritates the digestive system.
*   **Sudden Diet Change:** Introducing new food too quickly. The digestive system needs time to adjust.
*   **Food Allergies or Sensitivities:** Certain ingredients can trigger a reaction. Vomiting and shaking follow.
*   **Intestinal Parasites:** Worms or giardia can cause severe GI upset. They lead to inflammation and discomfort.

**Pancreatitis**

Pancreatitis is inflammation of the pancreas. This organ helps with digestion and blood sugar regulation. When inflamed, it causes severe pain. This pain often leads to shaking and vomiting.

*   **Symptoms:** Intense abdominal pain, hunched posture, fever, diarrhea, lethargy.
*   **Causes:** Often triggered by fatty meals. Some medications can also cause it.
*   **Treatment:** Requires veterinary care. It involves pain management, fluid therapy, and dietary changes.

**Kidney or Liver Disease**

These organ diseases affect a dog's ability to process toxins. When kidneys or liver do not work correctly, toxins build up. This build-up makes a dog feel sick. It causes nausea, vomiting, and weakness. Shaking might occur from weakness or discomfort.

*   **Symptoms:** Increased thirst, frequent urination, loss of appetite, weight loss, yellow gums (jaundice in liver disease).
*   **Diagnosis:** Blood tests show organ function.
*   **Prognosis:** Varies greatly based on severity and early diagnosis.

**Infections**

Various infections can make a dog sick enough to shake and vomit. Viruses, bacteria, or other pathogens can attack the body. They cause systemic illness.

*   **Viral Infections:** Canine distemper, parvovirus, or canine influenza. These cause severe gastrointestinal and respiratory symptoms.
*   **Bacterial Infections:** Salmonella or E. coli. These lead to food poisoning-like symptoms.
*   **Leptospirosis:** A bacterial infection spread through contaminated water or urine. It affects multiple organs.

**Other Underlying Conditions**

Sometimes, the cause is less obvious. Conditions like Addison's disease or certain cancers can also lead to these symptoms. Addison's disease affects the adrenal glands. It causes symptoms similar to shock. Brain tumors or neurological disorders can also cause shaking. They might indirectly lead to vomiting due to nausea or disorientation. This is why a vet visit is always necessary for a proper diagnosis.

### Gastrointestinal Issues and Dietary Problems

Many times, a dog shaking and throwing up traces back to issues within their digestive system or what they have eaten. The canine digestive tract is sensitive. It reacts strongly to irritants or changes. Understanding these common causes helps you prevent them. It also guides your initial response.

**Dietary Indiscretion**

Dogs are curious eaters. They often explore with their mouths. This means they might eat things they should not. I know my dog has tried to snatch food from the counter many times! This habit leads to what vets call "dietary indiscretion."

*   **Spoiled Food:** Eating food past its prime from the trash.
*   **Foreign Objects:** Swallowing toys, socks, or sticks. These can cause blockages or irritation.
*   **Toxic Plants:** Ingesting harmful plants in the garden or house.
*   **Rich Human Foods:** Feeding dogs fatty scraps or foods they cannot digest well.

These items upset the stomach lining. The body tries to expel them through vomiting. Shaking might come from discomfort, pain, or nausea.

**Food Sensitivities and Allergies**

Just like people, dogs can have food sensitivities or allergies. A sensitivity causes digestive upset. An allergy triggers an immune response. Both can manifest as shaking and vomiting.

*   **Common Allergens:** Proteins like beef, chicken, dairy, or wheat.
*   **Symptoms:** Vomiting, diarrhea, itchy skin, ear infections, shaking.
*   **Management:** A vet can help identify triggers. A hypoallergenic diet may be necessary.

If your dog is throwing up, you need to know [what to do if your dog is throwing up](https://beacleaner.com/what-to-do-if-your-dog-is-throwing-up). This guide gives clear steps.

**Sudden Diet Changes**

A dog’s digestive system adjusts to its regular food. Sudden changes can disrupt this balance. Introducing a new food without a slow transition often leads to stomach upset.

*   **Gradual Transition:** Mix new food with old food over 7-10 days. Increase the new food amount daily.
*   **Puppies and Seniors:** These dogs are more sensitive to diet changes. Their digestive systems are either still developing or slowing down.

**Intestinal Parasites**

Parasites live in a dog's intestines. They feed on nutrients or blood. This causes irritation and inflammation. Common parasites include roundworms, hookworms, whipworms, and tapeworms. Giardia and Coccidia are also prevalent.

*   **Symptoms:** Vomiting, diarrhea, weight loss, dull coat, distended abdomen.
*   **Diagnosis:** A vet performs a fecal test to identify parasites.
*   **Treatment:** Deworming medication is usually effective. Regular deworming helps prevent re-infestation.

For dogs experiencing both vomiting and diarrhea, it is important to know [what to give a dog for throwing up and diarrhea](https://beacleaner.com/what-to-give-a-dog-for-throwing-up-and-diarrhea). Bland diets and hydration are key initial steps.

**Bloat (Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus - GDV)**

Bloat is a life-threatening condition. It affects large, deep-chested dog breeds mostly. The stomach fills with gas and twists. This cuts off blood flow. It is a true emergency.

*   **Symptoms:** Retching without vomiting, swollen abdomen, restlessness, pain, rapid shallow breathing, shaking, collapse.
*   **Immediate Action:** This requires emergency vet care. Do not wait.

Recognizing these gastrointestinal and dietary problems helps you respond correctly. Always consult your vet for persistent or severe symptoms. Your dog's health is paramount.

### Pain, Anxiety, and Stress as Triggers

Sometimes, the causes behind a dog shaking and throwing up are not directly physical. Emotions like pain, anxiety, or stress can also trigger these reactions. Dogs experience feelings just like humans do. These feelings can manifest as physical symptoms. Understanding this link helps you address the root cause effectively.

**Pain-Related Shaking and Vomiting**

Pain is a powerful trigger for physical symptoms in dogs. When a dog hurts, their body reacts. Shaking can be an involuntary response to discomfort. Vomiting might occur due to intense pain or associated nausea. I have seen dogs shiver when they have a minor injury.

*   **Acute Pain:** Sudden injury, like a sprain or cut. Internal pain from an organ issue.
*   **Chronic Pain:** Arthritis, disc disease, or dental problems. These can lead to ongoing discomfort.
*   **Symptoms of Pain:** Whining, limping, guarding a body part, changes in posture, loss of appetite. Shaking and vomiting can be additional signs.

If you observe your dog shaking when nothing seems wrong, it might still be pain. You can read more about [why is my dog shaking when nothing is wrong](https://beacleaner.com/why-is-my-dog-shaking-when-nothing-is-wrong). Subtle signs often mean something is amiss internally.

**Anxiety and Fear Responses**

Dogs can feel anxious or fearful. Loud noises, separation, or new environments trigger these emotions. Their bodies respond with physical signs. Shaking is a common sign of anxiety. Stress hormones can also upset their stomach.

*   **Separation Anxiety:** Destructive behavior, excessive barking, and sometimes shaking or vomiting when left alone.
*   **Noise Phobias:** Thunderstorms, fireworks. Dogs might hide, pace, shake, or vomit due to extreme fear.
*   **New Situations:** Moving to a new home, meeting new people or animals. These events create stress.

**Stress and Its Physical Manifestations**

Prolonged stress affects a dog’s overall health. It weakens the immune system. It also impacts digestive function. A stressful environment can cause chronic shaking and intermittent vomiting. My own dog gets stressed by strangers. He shakes and sometimes refuses food.

*   **Changes in Routine:** Disruptions to feeding, walking, or sleep schedules.
*   **Environmental Stressors:** A new pet, construction noise, or lack of exercise.
*   **Emotional Trauma:** Past negative experiences or abuse.
*   **Symptoms of Stress:** Pacing, panting, excessive licking, changes in appetite, shaking, vomiting.

Addressing the underlying cause of pain, anxiety, or stress is vital. This might involve pain medication from your vet. It could also involve behavior modification or creating a calmer environment. ### Environmental Factors and Toxin Exposure

Your dog's environment plays a big role in their health. Toxins and extreme temperatures can quickly cause severe symptoms. When your dog is shaking and throwing up, consider what they might have encountered. This category often represents an emergency situation.

**Toxin Ingestion**

Dogs explore with their mouths. This curiosity can lead them to ingest harmful substances. Many household items are poisonous to dogs. I always keep cleaning supplies locked away.

*   **Common Household Toxins:**
    *   **Human Medications:** Even common painkillers like ibuprofen are toxic.
    *   **Cleaning Products:** Bleach, detergents, disinfectants.
    *   **Pest Control Products:** Rodenticides, insecticides, slug bait.
    *   **Antifreeze:** Sweet-tasting but deadly.
    *   **Certain Foods:** Chocolate, grapes, xylitol (artificial sweetener), onions, garlic.
    *   **Toxic Plants:** Lilies, sago palms, oleander, marijuana.

Symptoms of poisoning vary. They often include shaking, vomiting, diarrhea, weakness, seizures, and collapse. If you suspect toxin ingestion, seek emergency vet care immediately. Take the suspected item with you if possible.

**Heatstroke**

Dogs do not sweat like humans. They cool down by panting. On hot days or during intense exercise, they can overheat quickly. Brachycephalic (flat-faced) breeds are especially prone. I make sure my dog always has shade and fresh water on warm days.

*   **Symptoms:** Excessive panting, drooling, lethargy, weakness, uncoordinated movement, bright red gums, vomiting, diarrhea, shaking, collapse.
*   **Immediate Action:** Move your dog to a cooler area. Offer small amounts of water. Apply cool (not cold) water to their fur and paw pads. Seek emergency vet care.

**Cold Exposure (Hypothermia)**

Just as heat is dangerous, extreme cold can also cause problems. Small dogs or those with thin coats are at higher risk. Hypothermia occurs when a dog's body temperature drops too low.

*   **Symptoms:** Shivering, lethargy, weakness, stiff muscles, shallow breathing, pale gums, collapse. Shaking can be the body's attempt to warm itself. Vomiting might happen from overall system shock.
*   **Immediate Action:** Bring your dog indoors. Wrap them in warm blankets. Offer warm (not hot) water. Seek vet care for severe cases.

**Insect Stings or Bites**

An insect sting or bite can cause localized pain and swelling. In some cases, it triggers an allergic reaction. This systemic reaction can be serious.

*   **Symptoms:** Swelling, itching, pain, shaking (from discomfort or fear), vomiting (in severe allergic reactions), difficulty breathing.
*   **Action:** Monitor for swelling around the face or difficulty breathing. Seek vet care for severe reactions or if multiple stings occur.

Being aware of these environmental hazards helps protect your dog. Prevention is always the best approach. Keep your home safe and be mindful of outdoor conditions. Your dog relies on you for this protection.

### Neurological Conditions and Systemic Illnesses

Sometimes, the combination of shaking and throwing up points to more complex issues. These can involve a dog's nervous system or impact their entire body. These conditions often require careful diagnosis and specialized treatment. It is important not to overlook these possibilities.

**Neurological Disorders**

The brain and nervous system control many body functions. Problems here can cause involuntary shaking. They can also lead to disorientation or nausea, resulting in vomiting. My friend's dog started shaking uncontrollably. It turned out to be a neurological issue.

*   **Seizures:** While often thought of as convulsions, seizures can sometimes present as tremors or localized shaking. Vomiting can occur immediately after a seizure.
*   **Brain Tumors:** These growths can affect brain function, causing tremors, changes in behavior, and nausea leading to vomiting.
*   **Vestibular Disease:** Affects balance. Symptoms include head tilting, loss of coordination, circling, rapid eye movements (nystagmus), nausea, and vomiting. Shaking might occur due to disorientation or fear.
*   **Tremors:** Can be benign (essential tremors) or signs of underlying neurological issues. Some tremors are stress-induced.

Any suspicion of a neurological issue warrants immediate veterinary attention. A vet can perform specific tests. These include neurological exams, MRI, or CT scans.

**Systemic Illnesses Affecting Multiple Organs**

Some diseases affect multiple body systems simultaneously. These systemic illnesses often cause a general feeling of being unwell. This includes both shaking and vomiting.

*   **Addison's Disease (Hypoadrenocorticism):** This condition results from insufficient production of adrenal hormones. It mimics many other diseases.
    *   **Symptoms:** Lethargy, weakness, loss of appetite, vomiting, diarrhea, increased thirst, shaking, and can progress to a life-threatening "Addisonian crisis."
    *   **Diagnosis:** Specific blood tests are needed.
    *   **Treatment:** Lifelong hormone replacement therapy.
*   **Diabetes Mellitus:** Uncontrolled diabetes can lead to diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA). This is a serious complication.
    *   **Symptoms of DKA:** Vomiting, lethargy, sweet-smelling breath, increased thirst, and urination, shaking from weakness.
    *   **Action:** DKA is an emergency requiring immediate veterinary care.
*   **Cancer:** Various types of cancer can cause systemic illness. Tumors can affect organs, release toxins, or cause pain.
    *   **Symptoms:** Weight loss, loss of appetite, lethargy, pain, vomiting, and shaking.
    *   **Diagnosis:** Requires advanced imaging and biopsies.
*   **Tick-Borne Diseases:** Diseases like Lyme disease, ehrlichiosis, or anaplasmosis can cause widespread inflammation.
    *   **Symptoms:** Fever, joint pain, lethargy, loss of appetite, and sometimes vomiting and muscle tremors/shaking.
    *   **Prevention:** Tick control is crucial.

These conditions highlight the importance of a thorough veterinary examination. The cause of shaking and throwing up is not always simple. A vet can piece together symptoms. They perform tests to find the correct diagnosis.

### Preventative Measures and Ongoing Care

Preventing your dog from shaking and throwing up is better than treating it. Many causes are preventable with careful management. Ongoing care helps maintain your dog's health and reduces risks. I always focus on a proactive approach for my own dog.

**Dietary Management and Safety**

What your dog eats is critical. A consistent, high-quality diet prevents many issues. Avoid sudden changes in food.

*   **High-Quality Food:** Feed a balanced, appropriate diet for your dog's age and breed. Choose reputable brands.
*   **No Table Scraps:** Resist giving human food, especially fatty or spicy items. Many common human foods are toxic to dogs.
*   **Slow Diet Transitions:** Introduce new foods gradually over 7-10 days. Mix increasing amounts of new food with decreasing amounts of old food.
*   **Secure Trash Cans:** Prevent access to garbage. Dogs can easily find spoiled food or harmful items.
*   **Monitor Chew Toys:** Ensure toys are safe and cannot be swallowed. Replace damaged toys immediately. A bland diet is often recommended.

**Environmental Safety**

Your home and yard must be safe for your dog. Remove potential hazards. This helps prevent accidental poisoning or injury.

*   **Pet-Proof Your Home:** Store cleaning supplies, medications, and chemicals in locked cabinets.
*   **Identify Toxic Plants:** Remove or restrict access to plants harmful to dogs. Research common houseplants and garden flora.
*   **Secure Outdoor Areas:** Fence your yard. Check for holes. Remove any dangerous objects.
*   **Avoid Overheating/Overcooling:** Provide shade and water in summer. Use coats and limit time outdoors in winter.

**Regular Veterinary Check-ups and Vaccinations**

Preventative vet care is crucial. Regular visits catch issues early. Vaccinations protect against serious diseases.

*   **Annual Check-ups:** These allow your vet to assess overall health. They can detect subtle changes.
*   **Vaccinations:** Keep vaccinations up-to-date. This protects against parvovirus, distemper, leptospirosis, and other serious illnesses.
*   **Parasite Control:** Administer regular deworming and flea/tick prevention. This prevents internal and external parasite infestations.
*   **Dental Care:** Good dental health prevents infections that can spread systemically.

**Stress and Anxiety Management**

A calm dog is a healthy dog. Address sources of stress or anxiety in your dog's life. This prevents stress-induced physical symptoms.

*   **Consistent Routine:** Dogs thrive on predictability. Maintain regular feeding, walking, and playtime schedules.
*   **Enrichment:** Provide plenty of mental and physical stimulation. This includes toys, puzzles, and walks.
*   **Safe Space:** Give your dog a quiet, comfortable area where they can retreat.
*   **Professional Help:** For severe anxiety, consult a vet or a certified dog behaviorist. They can offer strategies or medications.

By following these preventative measures and providing ongoing care, you significantly reduce the chances of your dog shaking and throwing up. You contribute to their long, healthy life. ### Frequently Asked Questions About Dogs Shaking and Throwing Up

**Q1: What is the most common reason a dog shakes and throws up?**
A1: The most common reason is often dietary indiscretion. Dogs might eat something they should not, like spoiled food, trash, or too much human food. This irritates their stomach, leading to vomiting. Shaking can then result from nausea, discomfort, or general feeling unwell. It is usually a temporary upset, but still needs careful monitoring.

**Q2: Can stress or anxiety cause a dog to shake and throw up?**
A2: Yes, absolutely. Dogs experience stress and anxiety just like humans. Intense fear, loud noises, separation anxiety, or major changes in routine can trigger a "fight or flight" response. This response releases stress hormones that can cause gastrointestinal upset, leading to vomiting. Shaking is a common physical manifestation of anxiety in dogs.

**Q3: When should I take my dog to the emergency vet if they are shaking and throwing up?**
A3: Seek emergency vet care if your dog is vomiting repeatedly, showing signs of severe lethargy, has blood in their vomit, or appears to be in significant pain. Other red flags include a swollen abdomen (bloat), suspected toxin ingestion, or inability to keep water down for several hours. Do not delay care in these situations.

**Q4: What can I give my dog at home if they are only mildly shaking and throwing up?**
A4: For mild cases, first, withhold food for 12-24 hours. Offer small, frequent sips of water. If vomiting stops, introduce a bland diet like boiled plain chicken and white rice in small amounts. Monitor closely for any worsening symptoms. Always consult your vet if you are unsure or if symptoms persist beyond a few hours.

**Q5: Can diet changes cause my dog to shake and throw up?**
A5: Yes, sudden changes in your dog's diet can easily upset their digestive system. A quick switch to new food without a gradual transition period can lead to stomach upset, vomiting, and sometimes shaking due to discomfort. Always introduce new foods slowly over 7-10 days, mixing them with their old food.

**Q6: Are there specific dog breeds more prone to shaking and throwing up?**
A6: While any dog can experience these symptoms, certain breeds might be more prone to specific causes. For example, large, deep-chested breeds like Great Danes or German Shepherds are at higher risk for bloat. Smaller breeds might shiver more easily from cold or anxiety. Some breeds also have predispositions to certain medical conditions like pancreatitis.
