## The Majestic Alaskan Malamute: Defining Its Appearance

Before we explore other breeds, let us clearly understand what an Alaskan Malamute looks like. Malamutes are large, strong, and heavily boned working dogs. They possess a dense double coat that protects them from harsh Arctic conditions. Their coat colors vary, including shades of gray and white, black and white, sable and white, or red and white. They often have white markings on their face, paws, and parts of their body.

Their heads are broad with a moderate stop. Their eyes are almond-shaped and typically brown, always brown. Blue eyes do not occur in purebred Malamutes. Their ears are triangular, medium in size, and stand erect. The Malamute's tail is well-furred and carried over the back, often in a plume-like curl. They move with a proud, efficient gait. Understanding these features helps us identify similar dogs. For a deeper look into their appearance, you can read [what does the Alaskan Malamute look like](https://beacleaner.com/what-does-the-alaskan-malamute-look-like).

Their sheer size is another defining characteristic. Males can weigh 85 pounds and females 75 pounds. Their powerful build reflects their original purpose: pulling heavy sleds over long distances. They are not built for speed like a Husky but for endurance and strength. This combination of size, coat, and specific facial features makes them easily recognizable. We see why so many people admire their look.
