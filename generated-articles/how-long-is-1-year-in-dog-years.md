## The Science Behind Dog Years: A Modern Approach

Modern science provides a more accurate way to understand dog years. Researchers now use genetic markers to determine a dog's biological age. This is called an "epigenetic clock." This clock looks at chemical changes in DNA. It offers a precise measure of aging.

One significant study from UC San Diego introduced a new formula. This formula is `human_age = 16 ln(dog_age) + 31`. The 'ln' stands for the natural logarithm. This calculation shows that dogs age quickly at first. Then, their aging process slows down. This formula provides a much better estimate than the old 7-year rule.

For example, a 1-year-old dog is roughly 31 human years using this formula. A 2-year-old dog is around 42 human years. This shows how quickly their initial years pass. After that, the gap between human and dog years becomes smaller. The formula captures the non-linear nature of dog aging accurately.
