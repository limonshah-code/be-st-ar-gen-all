## Common Snapping Turtle vs. Alligator Snapping Turtle: Protection Status

Distinguishing between the Common Snapping Turtle and the Alligator Snapping Turtle is vital. Their legal protection status differs significantly. Most wildlife laws treat these two species very differently. The Common Snapping Turtle (Chelydra serpentina) is widespread across North America. It often exists in large numbers. Many states consider it a game animal. This means you can harvest it during certain seasons. You need a license and must follow specific rules. These rules dictate size, number, and method of taking.

The Alligator Snapping Turtle (Macrochelys temminckii), however, faces much stricter protection. This species is the largest freshwater turtle in North America. Its population has declined significantly due to habitat loss and over-harvesting. As a result, many states classify the Alligator Snapping Turtle as a threatened or endangered species. Some states list it as a species of special concern. Killing an Alligator Snapping Turtle is illegal in most, if not all, states where it lives. Penalties for harming this species are severe. They include heavy fines and possible jail time.

It is easy to confuse the two species, especially young ones. The Alligator Snapping Turtle has a more prehistoric look. It features three prominent ridges (keels) on its shell. Its head is larger and more triangular. Its beak is more pronounced. The Common Snapping Turtle has a smoother shell, though it can become rough with age. Its head is smaller and rounder.
