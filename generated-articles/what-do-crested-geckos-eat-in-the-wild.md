## FAQ Section

### Do crested geckos eat plants in the wild?

Yes, crested geckos consume parts of plants in the wild. Their diet includes ripe fruits and flower nectar. These provide sugars, vitamins, and hydration. They do not typically eat leaves or woody parts of plants. They focus on the sweeter, softer components. This is for nutritional value and ease of digestion.

### How often do wild crested geckos eat?

Wild crested geckos likely eat frequently, but in small amounts. They are nocturnal. They forage throughout the night, taking advantage of various food sources. Their opportunistic nature means they will eat whenever suitable food is found. This helps them maintain energy levels and hydration.

### Do crested geckos eat their shed skin in the wild?

Yes, crested geckos often eat their shed skin in the wild. This behavior is common among many reptile species. The shed skin contains valuable nutrients. It includes calcium and other minerals. Eating it helps them recover these nutrients. It also removes traces of their presence from predators.

### What are the main nutrients a wild crested gecko needs?

A wild crested gecko needs a balance of carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals. Carbohydrates come from fruits and nectar. Proteins and fats come from insects. Fruits also provide many essential vitamins. Calcium is a crucial mineral for bone health and egg production.

### Can wild crested geckos eat small vertebrates?

It is extremely rare for wild crested geckos to eat small vertebrates. Their primary diet consists of fruits, insects, and nectar. While they are opportunistic, their mouth structure and hunting style are not well-suited for larger vertebrate prey. They focus on smaller, easier-to-catch invertebrates.

### How do wild geckos find their food?

Wild geckos use their senses to find food. They have keen eyesight adapted for low light. They use this to spot insects and ripe fruits. They also possess an excellent sense of smell. This helps them locate overripe fruits and flowering plants. Their arboreal mobility allows them to access food high in the canopy.
