## FAQ Section

### Q1: Do ferrets smell bad?
Ferrets have a natural, musky odor. This smell comes from their skin glands. It is distinct but usually not overwhelming with proper care. Regular cage cleaning, frequent bedding changes, and bathing (not too often) help manage the scent. Desexing ferrets also reduces the musky smell.

### Q2: Are ferrets high-maintenance pets?
Ferrets are moderately high-maintenance. They require daily attention, including playtime outside their cage. Their cages need daily spot cleaning and thorough weekly cleaning. They also have specific dietary needs and benefit from regular veterinary check-ups. Ferret-proofing your home is also important.

### Q3: Can ferrets be left alone for long periods?
Ferrets are social animals. They prefer companionship. They should not be left alone for extended periods without interaction. If you work long hours, consider getting two ferrets so they have a companion. They still need human interaction and daily playtime.

### Q4: Are ferrets good pets for people with anxiety?
Ferrets can be good pets for people with anxiety. Their playful nature and engaging antics provide a distraction from worries. Their need for routine and care offers a sense of purpose. The physical comfort of holding or cuddling a ferret also helps reduce anxiety symptoms.

### Q5: What kind of environment do ferrets need?
Ferrets need a large, multi-level cage for sleeping and eating. They require a space that is ferret-proofed for supervised playtime. This area must be free of hazards like small openings, toxic plants, or electrical cords. They also need a comfortable bed, litter box, food and water bowls, and plenty of toys.

### Q6: Do ferrets bite often?
Young ferrets may nip during play. This is a common behavior. With consistent training and socialization, ferrets learn not to bite hard. They are generally not aggressive. Adult ferrets are usually gentle. They enjoy human interaction without biting.
