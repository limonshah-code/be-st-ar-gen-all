## The High Demands of Shih Tzu Grooming

One major reason people say "Shih Tzu are the worst dog" is their intensive grooming needs. Their long, flowing coat looks beautiful. However, maintaining this look requires daily effort. Neglecting their coat leads to serious problems.

Matted fur causes pain and skin infections. Brushing must be a regular part of their routine. I have seen many Shih Tzu with painful mats because owners underestimated the work. This constant grooming becomes a burden for busy people.

### Daily Brushing: A Must for Shih Tzu Hair

Shih Tzu hair grows quickly and constantly. It is more like human hair than typical dog fur. This means it tangles very easily. Daily brushing is not just a suggestion; it is a necessity.

You must use specific brushes and combs to get through their thick undercoat. Without this, mats form close to the skin. These mats pull on their skin, causing discomfort and irritation. Regular brushing sessions can take 15-30 minutes each day.

### Professional Grooming and Costs

Beyond daily care, professional grooming is often required. Many owners choose a shorter "puppy cut" to manage the coat more easily. Even with a short cut, regular trims every 4-6 weeks are important. This adds to the cost of ownership.

Professional grooming ensures the coat stays healthy and clean. It also addresses tricky areas like around the eyes and ears. Shih Tzu also need special attention to their eyes and ears. Their long facial hair often irritates their eyes. This can lead to tear staining and infections. Wiping their eyes daily is important to prevent problems.

## Common Health Problems in Shih Tzu Dogs

Shih Tzu dogs are cute, but they are also prone to specific health issues. Their unique physical structure, especially their flat faces, contributes to many problems. These health concerns can be painful for the dog and expensive for owners. They are a big reason why some consider Shih Tzu the worst dog breed. Understanding these issues helps prepare potential owners.

### Brachycephalic Syndrome

Shih Tzu are a brachycephalic breed, meaning they have short snouts. This flat face causes Brachycephalic Airway Syndrome. Dogs with this condition have narrowed nostrils and elongated soft palates. They struggle to breathe, especially in hot weather or during exercise.

You might hear them snorting, snoring, or reverse sneezing often. Severe cases may require surgery to improve airflow. This condition impacts their quality of life. Owners must monitor them closely for breathing difficulties.

### Eye Issues

Their large, prominent eyes are another source of problems. Shih Tzu often suffer from various eye conditions. These include cataracts, progressive retinal atrophy, and corneal ulcers. Their eyes are also vulnerable to injury because they protrude.

Eyelashes can grow inward, rubbing against the eye. This causes irritation and pain. Regular vet check-ups are important to catch these issues early. Eye problems can lead to blindness if not treated.

### Dental Problems

Shih Tzu often have crowded teeth due to their small jaws. This overcrowding makes them prone to dental disease. Food particles get trapped easily, leading to plaque and tartar buildup. Dental problems can cause pain, infection, and tooth loss.

Regular dental cleanings, both at home and professionally, are vital. Without proper dental care, these issues worsen quickly. Poor dental health affects their overall well-being. This is another example of specific care requirements for the breed.

### Patellar Luxation and Other Orthopedic Issues

Like many small breeds, Shih Tzu are susceptible to patellar luxation. This is when the kneecap slips out of place. It causes pain and lameness. It can range from mild to severe, sometimes requiring surgery.

Other orthopedic problems, such as hip dysplasia, can also occur. While less common than in larger breeds, it is still a risk. Responsible breeders screen for these conditions. Owners must be aware of the potential for joint problems. ## Training Difficulties with Shih Tzu Dogs

Training a Shih Tzu can be a test of patience. Many owners find their Shih Tzu stubborn and difficult to housebreak. This resistance to training is a common complaint. It contributes to the idea that Shih Tzu are the worst dog breed for some people. I have observed that consistency is key, but even then, results vary.

Their independent nature often clashes with training efforts. They have a mind of their own. This makes teaching basic commands a longer process.

### Housebreaking Challenges

Housebreaking is one of the biggest hurdles for Shih Tzu owners. They are notoriously hard to potty train. This can be frustrating for anyone. They often have small bladders and can be prone to accidents indoors.

Using pee pads can help, but transitioning to outdoor potty breaks may take a long time. Accidents can lead to stress for both the dog and the owner. I recommend a strict, consistent schedule and positive reinforcement. Crate training can also assist. Patience is absolutely essential during this period.

### Stubbornness and Independence

Shih Tzu dogs have a strong, independent streak. They were bred as companions, not working dogs. This means they often do not feel a strong need to "please" their owners. Their stubbornness can manifest as ignoring commands.

They might choose to do what they want, when they want. This requires owners to be firm but gentle. Harsh training methods do not work with this sensitive breed. Positive reinforcement and reward-based training are most effective. You must make training fun and engaging for them.

### Socialization Importance

Proper socialization from a young age is crucial for Shih Tzu. Without it, they can become shy or aggressive towards strangers or other dogs. Early exposure to various sights, sounds, and people helps them grow into well-adjusted adults. This helps to prevent fear-based behaviors.

Lack of socialization can also lead to excessive barking. This is another trait that causes frustration for owners. A well-socialized Shih Tzu is generally happier and easier to live with. I always encourage puppy classes to help with this process.

## The Temperamental Side of Shih Tzu

Some people might call Shih Tzu the worst dog because of their temperament quirks. While generally affectionate, they can exhibit behaviors that challenge owners. Their small size does not mean they lack personality. In fact, they often have big personalities in small bodies. Understanding these traits helps manage expectations.

### Excessive Barking

Shih Tzu dogs are known for their love of barking. They bark at strangers, other dogs, and even leaves blowing in the wind. This can be a major issue for apartment dwellers. It can also annoy neighbors. I have heard many stories of Shih Tzu barking at every little sound.

Their barking often serves as an alarm system. They want to alert you to everything happening around them. Training to reduce excessive barking is possible, but it takes time and consistency. It is important to address it early. You might wonder, [do Shih Tzu dogs bark a lot](https://beacleaner.com/do-shih-tzu-dogs-bark-a-lot)? Yes, many do.

### Separation Anxiety

Shih Tzu are companion dogs. They thrive on human interaction. This strong bond can sometimes lead to separation anxiety. They become distressed when left alone. Symptoms include barking, destructive chewing, or inappropriate urination.

This can be heartbreaking for owners and harmful to the dog. Dealing with separation anxiety requires specific training and management strategies. It might mean not leaving them alone for long periods. This can restrict an owner's lifestyle.

### Demanding Nature

Their desire for attention can make them quite demanding. Shih Tzu often want to be the center of your world. They may follow you everywhere. They might whine or paw at you for cuddles. This intense need for interaction can be overwhelming for some.

While charming, this demanding nature is not for everyone. It requires an owner who enjoys constant canine companionship. If you prefer a more independent dog, a Shih Tzu may not be a good fit.

### Potential Aggression Issues

Though rare, some Shih Tzu can display aggression. This often stems from fear, poor socialization, or pain. They can snap or bite if they feel threatened. Small dog syndrome, where tiny dogs act tough, can also play a role.

Proper training and socialization are key to preventing these issues. It is important to handle them gently. Always respect their boundaries. Aggression, even in a small dog, is a serious concern. ## The Physical Appearance and Its Drawbacks

While the Shih Tzu's appearance is often seen as their greatest asset, it also contributes to their potential drawbacks. ### Brachycephalic Features and Breathing

The Shih Tzu's short, flat face, known as brachycephaly, is their defining characteristic. However, this feature causes breathing difficulties. Their narrowed nostrils and elongated soft palates hinder airflow. This leads to snorting, snoring, and heavy panting. They are very sensitive to heat and humidity.

Over-excitement or strenuous exercise can cause respiratory distress. In severe cases, they may even collapse. Owners must always monitor their breathing. ### Eye Prominence and Vulnerability

Shih Tzu have large, prominent eyes that contribute to their sweet expression. Unfortunately, these eyes are highly vulnerable to injury. Since they protrude, they are easily scratched or poked. This can happen during play or even just from facial hair.

Their eyes are also prone to dryness and various inherited conditions. These include cataracts and progressive retinal atrophy. Daily eye cleaning is important to prevent tear staining and irritation. Constant vigilance is needed to protect their delicate eyes.

### Long Coat and Environmental Issues

Their luxurious long coat, while beautiful, is a magnet for dirt and debris. It easily picks up leaves, twigs, and mud from the ground. This means they often need cleaning after outdoor activities. This is especially true if you keep their coat long.

During hot weather, their thick coat can cause overheating, especially with their breathing issues. Owners must keep them cool and consider shorter haircuts in summer. ### Small Size and Fragility

Shih Tzu are small, delicate dogs. Their small stature makes them prone to injuries from falls or rough play. Children must be taught how to handle them gently. Larger dogs can easily injure a Shih Tzu unintentionally.

Their small bones are more fragile. Jumping off furniture can lead to fractures or joint issues like patellar luxation. This fragility requires owners to be constantly aware of their environment. They need protection from accidental harm.

## FAQs About Shih Tzu Ownership

### Are Shih Tzu easy to train for beginners?
No, Shih Tzu are often challenging for beginners. They have a stubborn nature. Housebreaking is a common difficulty. They need consistent, positive reinforcement training methods. Patience is key. Beginners might find their independent spirit frustrating.

### Do Shih Tzu dogs require a lot of exercise?
Shih Tzu dogs do not require extensive exercise. Short daily walks and indoor play are usually sufficient. They can easily overheat due to their flat faces. Too much strenuous activity is not good for them. They are generally content with moderate activity.

### Are Shih Tzu good with children and other pets?
Shih Tzu can be good with older, respectful children. Their small, delicate bodies are vulnerable to rough play. Early socialization helps them get along with other pets. Supervision is always important. They are often best in homes with older kids or no children.

### How often should a Shih Tzu be groomed professionally?
A Shih Tzu should be groomed professionally every 4 to 6 weeks. This helps maintain their coat health. It prevents matting, especially if the coat is kept long. Regular professional grooming is important even with daily home brushing. This is a significant ongoing cost.

### What is the average lifespan of a Shih Tzu?
The average lifespan of a Shih Tzu is between 10 to 16 years. Some Shih Tzu can live even longer. This means owners must commit to their care for a significant period. A long life means more time for potential health issues to develop.

### Do Shih Tzu dogs bark a lot?
Yes, many Shih Tzu dogs bark a lot. They often bark at strangers, other dogs, and various sounds. This can be a problem in apartments or quiet neighborhoods. Early training can help reduce excessive barking. Their barking is often a form of alerting their owners.
