## The Genetics Behind Blue Aussie Eyes

Understanding why some purebred Australian Shepherds have blue eyes requires a look into their genetics. The primary reason for blue eyes in this breed is the Merle gene. This gene is responsible for the unique mottled coat patterns, such as blue Merle or red Merle. It also plays a significant role in eye color.

The Merle gene dilutes random patches of a dog's base coat color. It can also dilute pigment in the eyes. When the Merle gene affects the eyes, it reduces the amount of melanin in the iris. Less melanin results in a blue color. This is the same principle that gives human babies blue eyes.

### Understanding the Merle Gene

The Merle gene is a dominant gene. A dog only needs one copy of the Merle gene (Mm) to display the Merle coat pattern and potentially have blue eyes. If a dog inherits two copies of the Merle gene (MM), it is known as a "double Merle." Double Merles can have extensive white markings and are at a higher risk for vision and hearing problems. Responsible breeders never breed two Merles together to avoid this risk.

You might wonder [why some Aussies have blue eyes](https://beacleaner.com/why-do-some-aussies-have-blue-eyes) more often than others. The answer lies directly in this genetic lottery. The Merle gene does not guarantee blue eyes, but it greatly increases the likelihood. Some Aussies with Merle coats might still have brown or amber eyes. Other genes can also influence eye color, but Merle is the most common factor for blue.

Some people think that [all Merle Australian Shepherds have blue eyes](https://beacleaner.com/do-all-merle-australian-shepherds-have-blue-eyes). This is not true. While many do, it is not a 100% guarantee. The expression of the Merle gene is complex. This means that a Merle Aussie can have any combination of eye colors.

### Heterochromia: Two Different Eye Colors

Another fascinating genetic trait is heterochromia. This means a dog has two different colored eyes. For example, an Australian Shepherd might have one blue eye and one brown eye. This is common in Aussies, especially those with the Merle gene. It can also happen when one eye is partially blue and partially another color, known as sectoral heterochromia.

Heterochromia is perfectly normal and does not indicate any health issue. It is simply a result of the pigment distribution in the eyes. I find these dogs particularly striking. The unique eye combination adds to their charm. So, if you see an Aussie with two different eye colors, know it is a natural genetic variation. Many wonder [do blue merle Australian Shepherds always have blue eyes](https://beacleaner.com/do-blue-merle-australian-shepherds-always-have-blue-eyes). The truth is, even Blue Merles can have mixed or non-blue eye colors.

## Do Blue Eyes in Aussies Mean Health Problems?

It is a common concern among potential dog owners: do blue eyes mean health problems for Australian Shepherds? I want to reassure you that for most purebred Australian Shepherds, blue eyes themselves do not indicate poor health or vision issues. A blue-eyed Aussie is typically just as healthy as one with brown eyes. The color is simply a genetic trait.

However, there is an important distinction to make. While blue eyes alone are not a problem, the *reason* for blue eyes in some cases can be linked to health risks. This applies specifically to "double Merle" Australian Shepherds. Understanding this difference is crucial for responsible ownership.

### Blue Eyes and Hearing/Vision Concerns

In Australian Shepherds, blue eyes are often associated with the Merle gene. This gene causes the beautiful mottled coat patterns and can reduce pigment in the eyes. In a single Merle (Mm) Australian Shepherd, blue eyes are generally not a cause for concern regarding health. These dogs typically have normal vision and hearing. [Do Australian Shepherds with blue eyes have eye problems](https://beacleaner.com/do-australian-shepherds-with-blue-eyes-have-eye-problems)? Generally no, not directly because of the blue color.

The problem arises with double Merles (MM). These dogs inherit two copies of the Merle gene, one from each parent. This often results in a predominantly white coat and frequently, two blue eyes. The excessive dilution of pigment in double Merles can lead to serious health issues. These issues include:

*   **Deafness:** Partial or complete deafness is a significant risk for double Merles.
*   **Blindness or microphthalmia:** This refers to abnormally small eyes, which can be blind or have severely impaired vision. Other eye defects are also possible.

These conditions are not due to the blue color itself. They are due to the severe genetic dilution caused by two copies of the Merle gene. I cannot stress enough how important it is for breeders to avoid producing double Merles. Responsible breeders will never breed two Merle dogs together.

### The Double Merle Conundrum

When considering an Australian Shepherd puppy, especially one with a lot of white on its coat and blue eyes, always ask about the parents' genetics. A reputable breeder will know the Merle status of both parents. They will not knowingly breed two Merles. If a breeder cannot provide this information, it is a red flag.

My personal advice is to be cautious with puppies advertised as "extreme Merles" or "white Merles." These descriptions might indicate a double Merle dog. While these dogs are still loving companions, their potential for health problems is much higher. Owners of double Merles must be prepared for potential medical expenses and special care requirements. They might need a special environment due to their sensory impairments.

So, while [dogs with blue eyes do not necessarily have more problems](https://beacleaner.com/do-dogs-with-blue-eyes-have-more-problems), it is essential to understand the underlying genetics. For the average purebred Australian Shepherd with blue eyes, you can enjoy their beauty without worrying about direct health impacts from the eye color itself. Always choose a breeder who prioritizes health and ethical breeding practices.

## Puppy Eye Color: When Do Aussie Eyes Settle?

One of the most exciting parts of watching Australian Shepherd puppies grow is seeing their eyes develop. All Australian Shepherd puppies, regardless of their adult eye color, are born with blue eyes. This is a temporary puppy blue, much like in human babies. Their true eye color usually starts to show as they mature.

Many new owners often ask me, "When can I tell if my Australian Shepherd puppy will have blue eyes?" The answer is, you have to be patient. That initial blue color is not necessarily permanent. The pigment in a puppy's eyes needs time to develop. This process can take several weeks or even months.

### The Changing Colors of Puppy Eyes

For the first few weeks, all Aussie puppies have those clear, bright blue eyes. As they grow, melanocytes, which are pigment-producing cells, begin to produce melanin in the iris. The amount and type of melanin determine the adult eye color.

*   **Week 0-3:** Eyes are usually closed at birth. When they open, they are blue. This is a very light, almost cloudy blue.
*   **Week 4-8:** The blue might start to deepen or change. You might see hints of brown, amber, or green appearing around the pupil or at the edges of the iris. This is the first sign of their permanent color.
*   **Week 9-16:** By this stage, most puppies' eye colors have largely settled. You can generally get a good idea of their adult eye color. If the eyes are going to be brown or amber, they will usually have shifted significantly by now.
*   **Beyond 16 Weeks:** Some subtle changes can still occur. However, the dominant color will be apparent. If the eyes remain blue, it is likely their permanent color. If they are going to be marbled, the marbling will usually be visible.

It is rare for eyes to change significantly after 16 weeks. However, sometimes minor shifts can happen up to 6 months of age. So, don't jump to conclusions too early. Enjoy the puppy blues while they last.

If you are trying to [determine if your Australian Shepherd puppy will have blue eyes](https://beacleaner.com/how-to-determine-if-your-australian-shepherd-puppy-will-have-blue-eyes), look for a persistent, vibrant blue. If you see any speckles or shifts towards other colors, it is a sign that the eyes are changing. For puppies whose parents both carry the Merle gene, the likelihood of permanent blue eyes is higher.

My personal experience with Aussie litters shows that it is a waiting game. I always tell new puppy owners to simply enjoy the development. The final eye color reveals itself in time. Patience is key when monitoring a puppy's changing appearance. If you want to know [when you can tell if a dog has blue eyes](https://beacleaner.com/when-can-you-tell-if-a-dog-has-blue-eyes), it is usually by four months of age, but sometimes earlier.

The key points from the breed standard regarding eye color are:

*   **Expression:** Eyes should show alertness, intelligence, and a keen, watchful expression. The color itself does not impact this.
*   **Health:** The eyes should be healthy, clear, and free from disease.
