## Official Recognition: What's in a Name for Long Coated German Shepherds?

It is common to wonder about the formal name for these dogs. Many people believe they are a separate breed. However, Long Coated German Shepherds are still simply German Shepherds. Their longer hair is a coat variation, not a different breed entirely.

The American Kennel Club (AKC) initially considered long coats a fault. Dogs with long coats could not compete in conformation shows. This rule changed in 2010. Now, long-haired German Shepherds with an undercoat can compete in AKC shows. The presence of an undercoat is key for AKC recognition.

The Federation Cynologique Internationale (FCI) recognizes two types of German Shepherds. They differentiate between the long coat with an undercoat and the long coat without an undercoat. Both are accepted, but only the type with an undercoat can participate in shows. Different kennel clubs have different rules. It is important to check their specific guidelines.

## The Genetics of a Flowing Coat: How Long Hair Develops

The long coat on a German Shepherd is not a random occurrence. A specific genetic trait causes it. This trait is inherited through a recessive gene. Both parent dogs must carry this gene to produce long-haired puppies.

If a puppy inherits two copies of this recessive gene, one from each parent, it will have a long coat. If a puppy inherits only one copy, it will be a carrier. A carrier dog does not show the long coat trait itself. However, it can pass the gene to its offspring. This genetic process explains why some litters can have both short-haired and long-haired puppies.

Breeders can test their dogs for the long coat gene. This helps them plan litters. Knowing the genetic makeup allows responsible breeders to produce specific coat types. This ensures health and predictability in their breeding programs.

## Distinguishing Features: Beyond Just Length

The most obvious difference is the coat length. Long Haired German Shepherds have hair that is noticeably longer. This hair often creates a majestic, flowing appearance. Their coats can be straight or slightly wavy.

Beyond length, you will see feathering. Feathering refers to longer hair on the ears, legs, and tail. This feathering gives them a softer outline. Their tail will have a full, bushy appearance. The long hair often forms a "mane" around their neck.

Despite the coat difference, their body structure remains the same as short-haired German Shepherds. They have the same strong build, intelligent expression, and characteristic gait. Their longer coat only enhances their already noble look. This visual appeal makes them quite popular.

## Grooming Needs: Keeping Your Long Haired German Shepherd Pristine

A long coat requires more attention than a short one. Regular grooming is essential for a Long Haired German Shepherd. This helps keep their coat healthy and free of mats. Neglecting their coat can lead to painful tangles.

I find that daily brushing works best. A pin brush and a slicker brush are my go-to tools. They help remove loose hair and prevent mats. Pay extra attention to areas behind the ears, under the legs, and on the tail. These spots tend to mat more easily.

Long Haired German Shepherds also shed. They shed year-round, with heavier shedding during seasonal changes. Consistent grooming reduces the amount of hair around your home. It also keeps their skin healthy underneath. Occasional baths, every 2-3 months, can help keep their coat clean. However, too many baths can strip natural oils. Investing time in [dedicated grooming care](https://beacleaner.com/how-long-can-german-shepherds-be-alone) benefits both the dog and the owner.

## Temperament and Training: Are They Different?

These dogs are highly trainable and eager to please. They thrive on having a job to do. Early socialization and consistent training are very important. This helps them grow into well-behaved and confident adults. They are excellent family pets when properly raised. They bond deeply with their owners.

Long Haired German Shepherds excel in various dog sports. This includes obedience, agility, and protection work. Their impressive intellect and work ethic make them versatile companions. ## Health Considerations for Long Coated GSDs

When considering the health of a Long Haired German Shepherd, remember they are still German Shepherds. This means they are prone to the same health issues as their short-haired counterparts. The long coat itself does not introduce new health problems. Awareness of common breed-specific conditions is important.

One of the most well-known issues is hip and elbow dysplasia. This is a genetic condition affecting the joints. Responsible breeders screen their dogs for these issues. They use X-rays and certifications from organizations like the Orthopedic Foundation for Animals (OFA). Regular veterinary check-ups are important. These check-ups help monitor overall health.

Other potential health concerns include degenerative myelopathy and certain eye conditions. Bloat, a life-threatening stomach condition, is also a risk for large, deep-chested breeds. Providing a high-quality diet and regular exercise supports their well-being. Knowing the health history of the parents helps ensure a healthy puppy. Proper care, including exercise and mental stimulation, helps them live a full life. ## FAQ Section

### Are Long Haired German Shepherds rare?

No, Long Haired German Shepherds are not rare. The gene for long hair is recessive. This means both parents must carry it to produce long-haired puppies. They are less common than short-haired GSDs. However, dedicated breeders purposefully produce them. Their numbers have grown in popularity over recent years.

### Do Long Haired German Shepherds shed more?

Long Haired German Shepherds do shed. Their long coat can make the shedding more noticeable. They shed year-round, with heavier periods seasonally. While they do not necessarily shed more hair by volume, the longer strands are more visible. Regular grooming helps manage the shedding effectively.

### Are they recognized by the AKC?

Yes, the American Kennel Club (AKC) now recognizes Long Haired German Shepherds. This change happened in 2010. For AKC conformation shows, the long-haired variant must have an undercoat. Without an undercoat, they are still considered a fault. They can compete in other performance events regardless of undercoat status.

### Can a long-haired GSD be a working dog?

Absolutely. Long Haired German Shepherds possess the same intelligence and drive as their short-haired counterparts. They excel in various working roles. These include search and rescue, obedience, agility, and therapy work. Their coat length does not impact their ability to perform tasks. Their strong work ethic remains consistent.

### What is the lifespan of a long-haired German Shepherd?

The lifespan of a Long Haired German Shepherd is the same as any other German Shepherd. On average, they live between 9 to 13 years. Factors influencing their lifespan include genetics, diet, exercise, and overall healthcare. Regular vet visits and a healthy lifestyle contribute to a longer, happier life.

### Do they have a different temperament?

No, Long Haired German Shepherds do not have a different temperament. Their personality traits are identical to short-haired German Shepherds. They are known for being intelligent, loyal, confident, and protective. Early socialization and consistent training are key to developing a well-rounded and friendly companion. The coat length does not alter their behavior.
