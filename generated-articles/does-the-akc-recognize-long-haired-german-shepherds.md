### Does the AKC Recognize Long Haired German Shepherds for Conformation?

The American Kennel Club (AKC) considers the long coat a disqualifying fault in the German Shepherd Dog breed standard for conformation events. This means Long Haired German Shepherds cannot compete in AKC dog shows where physical appearance is judged. However, they are purebred German Shepherds and are registerable with the AKC. Their registration allows participation in many other AKC events.

### Understanding the German Shepherd Breed Standard and Coat Types

The German Shepherd Dog breed standard sets specific guidelines. These guidelines describe the ideal physical traits and temperament of the breed. The American Kennel Club defines these standards. Breeders aim to produce dogs that meet these exact specifications.

The AKC standard specifies the accepted coat type for German Shepherds. The preferred coat is a double coat of medium length. The outer coat should be dense and straight. It should lie close to the body. An undercoat provides insulation.

Long coats are a variation of this standard. They feature longer hair all over the body. This includes feathering on the ears, legs, and tail. This coat type differs significantly from the accepted short or medium coat.

The standard explicitly states that a "long outer coat, soft, with long tufts" is a disqualifying fault. This means dogs with such coats cannot earn championship points in conformation shows. This rule applies regardless of their pedigree or other desirable traits. Understanding these nuances helps explain the AKC's position. ### The AKC's Official Stance on Long Haired German Shepherds

The American Kennel Club maintains a clear position on Long Haired German Shepherds. They view the long coat as a deviation from the established breed standard. For this reason, these dogs face disqualification in conformation shows. This means judges will not place them in the competition ring.

This stance does not question their purity. Long Haired German Shepherds are purebred dogs. They come from two AKC-registered German Shepherd parents. The AKC will register them. Their lineage is fully documented.

Registration allows participation in many other AKC-sanctioned events. These include obedience trials, agility courses, and herding tests. The disqualification applies only to conformation events. Conformation judges assess how well a dog matches the breed standard.

The history of the German Shepherd breed standard plays a role. Early breeders focused on dogs with specific working coat types. A longer coat was not considered practical for their original purpose. It tended to collect debris and ice more easily. This historical preference influenced the standard we see today. The AKC's stance reflects a long-standing tradition. It defines the breed's appearance for showing purposes. ### Genetic Basis of the Long Coat in German Shepherds

The long coat in German Shepherds is a genetic trait. It results from a recessive gene. This gene is responsible for hair length. A dog must inherit two copies of this recessive gene to have a long coat.

Parents with standard coats can carry this gene. If both parents carry one copy of the recessive gene, they are "carriers." They do not show the long coat trait themselves. However, they can pass it to their offspring. This means two seemingly standard-coated German Shepherds can produce long-haired puppies.

Breeders who test their dogs can identify carriers. Genetic testing helps them make informed breeding decisions. It prevents unexpected long-coat puppies. Responsible breeders strive to understand these genetic factors.

#### The L/L, L/l, and l/l Genotypes

*   **L/L (Homozygous Dominant):** This dog has two dominant genes for short hair. It will have a standard, short, or medium coat. This dog cannot produce long-haired offspring.
*   **L/l (Heterozygous):** This dog carries one dominant gene for short hair and one recessive gene for long hair. It will have a standard, short, or medium coat. This dog is a carrier of the long-haired gene. It can pass the recessive gene to its puppies.
*   **l/l (Homozygous Recessive):** This dog has two recessive genes for long hair. It will have a long coat. All its offspring will inherit at least one copy of the long-haired gene.

Understanding these genotypes is crucial for breeders. It helps them predict coat types in litters. Genetic science explains why long coats appear even in standard lines. ### Distinguishing Long Haired German Shepherds from Other Breeds

Long Haired German Shepherds possess the same noble characteristics as their standard-coated counterparts. They are German Shepherds through and through. The only difference lies in their coat length. Their overall build, temperament, and health predispositions remain consistent with the breed.

These dogs are not a separate breed. They are a coat variation within the German Shepherd Dog breed. People sometimes confuse them with other long-coated shepherd-type breeds. These might include the Shiloh Shepherd or the King Shepherd. Those are distinct breeds developed independently.

King Shepherds, for example, are a relatively newer breed. They resulted from crosses involving German Shepherds, Alaskan Malamutes, and other large breeds. They often have very long, thick coats. ### Frequently Asked Questions About Long Haired German Shepherds

People often have many questions about these distinctive dogs. Here are answers to some common inquiries about Long Haired German Shepherds.

#### Are Long Haired German Shepherds rare?
No, they are not rare. The recessive gene for long hair is present in many German Shepherd lines. This means long-haired puppies appear regularly. Their numbers are increasing in some regions. They are quite common among pet German Shepherds.

#### Can a Long Haired German Shepherd compete in AKC obedience or agility?
Yes, absolutely! As long as they are AKC-registered purebreds, Long Haired German Shepherds can compete in most AKC performance events. These include obedience, agility, rally, and herding. These events judge a dog's skills, not its coat length.

#### Do Long Haired German Shepherds shed more than short-haired ones?
They often appear to shed more due to the length of their hair. The volume of hair is greater. Both coat types shed, especially during seasonal changes. Long-haired dogs require more frequent and thorough grooming to manage their shedding.

#### Is a Long Haired German Shepherd considered a purebred?
Yes, they are purebred German Shepherds. The long coat is a genetic variation within the breed. It does not indicate mixed lineage. They can be registered with the AKC. Their pedigree is identical to short-haired German Shepherds.

#### What are the grooming needs for a Long Haired German Shepherd?
Long Haired German Shepherds require regular grooming. Daily or weekly brushing is necessary. This prevents mats and tangles. It also helps remove loose hair and dirt. Occasional trimming around the paws or ears may be needed.
