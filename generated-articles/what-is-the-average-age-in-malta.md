## FAQ Section

### Q1: Is Malta's population getting older?

Yes, Malta's population is indeed getting older. The median age has steadily increased over the past decades. This trend mirrors many other developed countries. It results from lower birth rates and longer life expectancies. An aging population requires specific adjustments in social and economic planning.

### Q2: What is Malta's birth rate?

Malta's birth rate has seen a decline in recent years. It is generally below the replacement level, meaning not enough babies are born to replace the current population. This low birth rate is a key factor contributing to the rise in the country's average age. Government policies sometimes aim to encourage more births.

### Q3: How does migration affect Malta's age?

Migration significantly impacts Malta's average age. Inward migration often brings younger working-age individuals. This inflow can help to offset the aging trend. It injects youth into the labor force. Outward migration of young Maltese citizens, however, can exacerbate the aging process.

### Q4: What is Malta's life expectancy?

Malta boasts a high life expectancy. Maltese citizens, on average, live longer lives, similar to many Western European countries. This high life expectancy is a positive indicator of good healthcare and living standards. It also directly contributes to the increasing average age of the population.

### Q5: Are there enough young people in Malta?

The proportion of young people in Malta is decreasing relative to older age groups. This is a common characteristic of an aging population. While there are still young individuals, their share of the total population is shrinking. This trend raises concerns for future workforce supply and innovation.
