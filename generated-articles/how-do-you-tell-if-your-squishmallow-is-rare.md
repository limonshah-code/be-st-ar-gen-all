### Decoding Squishmallow Tags and Biographies: Your First Clue

The tags on your Squishmallow are your most important tool for identifying rarity. There are two main types of tags. First, the hang tag, which is the paper tag usually attached to the ear or seam. Second, the tush tag, which is a fabric tag sewn directly onto the Squishmallow's body. Both contain vital information.

The hang tag is often the most informative. It usually features the Squishmallow's name, its biography, and sometimes specific release information. Look for clues like "Limited Edition" or a specific "Collector Number." Some special hang tags might have a holographic sticker or unique artwork. These small details can indicate a rarer version. Always keep hang tags in pristine condition; a damaged or missing tag can reduce value.

The tush tag provides details like the production date, materials used, and sometimes the factory location. Newer Squishmallows often have multiple tush tags stacked on top of each other. Examine all sides of these tags carefully. An older production date can sometimes hint at an earlier, potentially rarer, release. Also, unique symbols or manufacturing codes might signify a special variant. Never cut off your tush tag. It is an essential part of your Squishmallow's identity and authenticity.

#### The Hang Tag: A Treasure Map

The hang tag is a collector's best friend. It typically shows the character's name and a short story. This story can offer hints about the Squishmallow's personality or origin. Some hang tags will also display a special collection name or an edition number. These numbers are very important for rarity. For example, a tag might say "Founder's Edition" or "Special Release." Keep these tags clean and attached. A pristine hang tag greatly boosts a Squishmallow's appeal to collectors.

#### The Tush Tag: Essential Details

The tush tag is sewn into the Squishmallow fabric. It contains important manufacturing details. This tag will show the year it was made. It also lists the materials used in its construction. Sometimes, the tush tag includes specific product codes. Collectors often check these codes against online databases. Older production dates on tush tags can indicate a retired or earlier version. This makes the Squishmallow potentially rarer. Always make sure the tush tag is present and readable.

Here are key pieces of information to look for on your Squishmallow's tags:

*   **Production Date:** Found on the tush tag, often year-specific. Older dates can mean rarer items.
*   **Series/Collection Name:** Sometimes on the hang tag, indicating a special line.

*   **Holographic Stickers/Special Artwork:** Unique design elements on the hang tag itself.

### Limited Editions, Exclusives, and Special Releases

Many Squishmallows gain rarity by being part of a limited release. These are not everyday finds. They are often produced for specific events or sold only through certain retailers. Knowing these categories helps you pinpoint potential rarities. I always keep an eye out for these special editions.

Store exclusives are a common form of limited release. Retailers like Target, Walgreens, Walmart, and even grocery chains often commission their own unique Squishmallow designs. These plush toys are only available at those specific stores. Once they sell out, they become harder to find elsewhere. This exclusivity drives their rarity. Some characters, like a particular [Australian Shepherd Squishmallow](https://beacleaner.com/what-is-the-name-of-the-australian-shepherd-squishmallow), might appear as a store exclusive, making that specific version rare.

Seasonal and event-specific editions are another category. Think about Halloween, Christmas, or Valentine's Day Squishmallows. These are only sold for a short period each year. Once the season ends, they are no longer available. This limited window makes them rare over time. Convention exclusives, like those sold at Comic-Con, are even rarer. Only attendees can buy them, making their numbers very low.

Sometimes, Squishmallow releases include "Chase" variants. These are rare versions of a standard Squishmallow. They might have a slight color difference, a unique accessory, or a special tag. Finding a Chase variant is like finding a golden ticket. These unexpected surprises are highly sought after by collectors. Always compare your Squishmallow to common versions to spot these subtle differences.

### Discontinued and Retired Squishmallows: The Rarest of All

Among Squishmallow collectors, retired characters hold a special status. These are Squishmallows that Kellytoy, the manufacturer, no longer produces. Once production stops, the existing supply becomes finite. This limited availability drives up their rarity and often their market value. I always consider retired Squishmallows to be the true "grails" of any collection.

Identifying a retired Squishmallow can be a bit tricky. There is no single official list that is constantly updated. However, a good starting point is the production date on the tush tag. Older dates, especially those from the early years of Squishmallow production (e.g., 2017-2019), often belong to retired characters. If a character has not been seen in new retail stock for a long time, it's a strong indicator.

Online resources are invaluable for confirming retirement status. Fan-created Squishmallow wikis and databases often track production timelines. Collector communities on social media platforms like Instagram and Reddit frequently discuss retired characters. If you can't find recent information about a specific Squishmallow being sold in stores, it is probably retired. Remember, the longer a Squishmallow has been retired, the rarer it generally becomes.

#### The Concept of Retirement in Squishmallow Collecting

When a Squishmallow is "retired," it means production has officially ceased. Kellytoy stops making that particular character or design. This makes the existing plush toys limited in number. Retirement is a key factor in increasing a Squishmallow's rarity. Collectors highly value retired pieces because they can no longer be bought new. This drives up their desirability on the secondary market.

#### Identifying Retired Characters

Identifying retired characters requires a bit of detective work. First, check the tush tag for the production year. Older dates are a good sign. Second, observe retail availability. If you haven't seen a character in stores for a long time, it is likely retired. Third, consult online collector communities. Websites and forums often have discussions or lists about retired Squishmallows. Cross-referencing these sources will confirm its retired status.

Here are steps to check for retirement status:

1.  **Check the Tush Tag:** Look for the production year. Earlier years (e.g., pre-2020) are often associated with retired lines.
2.  **Monitor Retail Stores:** Has this Squishmallow appeared in new stock recently? A long absence suggests retirement.
3.  **Consult Online Databases:** Websites like the Squishmallow Wiki or fan-made databases often list release and retirement dates.
4.  **Join Collector Communities:** Ask in Facebook groups, Reddit communities, or Instagram fan pages. Experienced collectors can often confirm retirement status quickly.
5.  **Look for "OG" Status:** Early versions of popular characters, sometimes called "Original Gangster" or "OG," are almost always retired and highly rare.

### The Importance of Condition and Authenticity

A Squishmallow's condition dramatically impacts its rarity and value. Even the rarest Squishmallow will lose value if it is not in good shape. Collectors prioritize items that look brand new. Maintaining your Squishmallow's condition is crucial for its long-term appeal.

For a rare Squishmallow, "mint condition" is the ideal state. This means it is clean, free of stains or tears, and retains its original shape. Crucially, all tags must be intact. The paper hang tag should be attached, undamaged, and readable. The fabric tush tag must also be present and clear. A Squishmallow without its tags, or with heavily damaged tags, will be worth significantly less, even if it is a rare character. I always advise keeping tags safe.

Authenticity is another critical factor. The popularity of Squishmallows has led to the production of many fake or "knock-off" versions. These fakes are not rare and hold no collector value. They often have poor stitching, incorrect tag information, or feel different to the touch. Knowing how to spot a fake is essential. Always buy from reputable sources or closely examine any Squishmallow for inconsistencies. Comparing it to pictures of authentic versions online can help. An authentic, well-maintained Squishmallow will always be more valuable.

#### Preserving Your Squishmallow's Value

Keeping your Squishmallow in excellent condition is vital. Store it away from direct sunlight to prevent fading. Avoid excessive handling to prevent wear and tear. If it gets dirty, spot clean gently rather than machine washing. Most importantly, protect its tags. Keep the hang tag attached, perhaps in a tag protector. Ensure the tush tag remains intact and legible. A well-preserved Squishmallow retains its rarity and market value.

#### Spotting Fakes: Authenticity Checks

Identifying fake Squishmallows protects your collection. Fakes often have incorrect hang tag designs or fonts. The tush tag might show a different brand name or poor quality printing. The fabric on fakes usually feels less soft and squishy than an authentic one. Stitching may appear uneven or loose. Always compare your Squishmallow to official images online. Purchase from trusted retailers to avoid counterfeits.
