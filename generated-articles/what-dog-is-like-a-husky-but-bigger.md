## The Alaskan Malamute: A Larger, Powerful Sled Dog

When you ask what dog is like a Husky but bigger, the Alaskan Malamute invariably comes to mind first. These magnificent dogs embody the strength and resilience of the Arctic. They command attention with their imposing size and striking features. Malamutes possess a deep history as heavy-duty sled dogs. They moved large loads over long distances.

Malamutes have a robust and muscular build. This makes them significantly heavier than Siberian Huskies. Adult male Malamutes can weigh between 75 to 90 pounds. Some even exceed 100 pounds. They stand taller than Huskies, typically reaching 23 to 25 inches at the shoulder. Their thick double coat keeps them warm in harsh climates. This coat gives them a very fluffy and substantial appearance.

Their facial markings and coat colors often resemble a Husky, including various shades of gray, black, sable, and red, all with white markings. However, Malamutes generally have brown eyes. Blue eyes, common in Huskies, are rare and considered a disqualifying fault in Malamutes by breed standards. They also possess a broader head and a more powerful muzzle than the Siberian Husky. ## Understanding the Alaskan Malamute's Temperament and Needs

Alaskan Malamutes are known for their friendly and affectionate nature, often described as "gentle giants." They form strong bonds with their families. Malamutes enjoy being part of family activities. They are typically good with children when properly socialized. Their strong pack drive makes them excellent companions.

However, Malamutes also possess an independent and strong-willed personality. This requires consistent training and a firm, patient handler. Early socialization is crucial for these dogs. This helps them learn how to interact appropriately with other animals and people. Their high prey drive means they may chase smaller animals. This makes a securely fenced yard essential for their safety.

## Canadian Eskimo Dog: A Rare, Robust Northern Breed

Another compelling answer to "what dog is like a Husky but bigger" is the Canadian Eskimo Dog. This breed is one of North America's oldest purebred dogs. It has a rich history with the Inuit people. They relied on these powerful dogs for transportation, hunting, and protection. The Canadian Eskimo Dog truly embodies the spirit of the Arctic wilderness.

These dogs are known for their remarkable strength and endurance. They are substantially larger than a Siberian Husky. Males can weigh between 65 to 100 pounds and stand 23 to 27 inches tall. Their build is rugged and muscular, designed for harsh conditions. They possess a very thick double coat that provides excellent insulation. Their appearance is distinctly wolf-like. They have a broad head, small erect ears, and a powerful muzzle.

The Canadian Eskimo Dog is a working breed through and through. They thrive on having a job to do. Their temperament is loyal and intelligent. However, they can be stubborn and independent. They require an experienced owner who understands their specific needs. They form strong bonds with their human companions. They are very affectionate towards their family.

## Greenland Dog: An Ancient, Hardy Sled Dog

The Greenland Dog is another large, primitive Northern breed resembling a Husky but with greater size and strength. These dogs are direct descendants of the dogs brought to Greenland by the Vikings. They have served as crucial working animals for the Inuit people for centuries. Their primary role has been as heavy sled haulers and hunting companions.

Greenland Dogs are robust and powerfully built. They typically weigh between 66 to 70 pounds. They stand 22 to 25 inches tall. While their weight might seem similar to some Malamutes, their overall build is very solid and muscular. They have an exceptionally thick double coat. This protects them from extreme Arctic temperatures. Their colors range from black, white, gray, and often have distinctive markings. Like Malamutes, they almost exclusively have brown eyes.

These dogs possess an energetic and enthusiastic temperament. They are very friendly towards people. They can be quite independent. Their strong pack drive makes them enjoy living with other dogs. However, their high prey drive necessitates careful supervision around smaller animals. Greenland Dogs require immense amounts of exercise. They are best suited for active individuals or families who can provide them with a working role or extensive outdoor activities. Their hardy nature makes them resilient and robust companions.

## FAQ Section

### Are Alaskan Malamutes good family dogs?
Yes, Alaskan Malamutes can be wonderful family dogs. They are generally affectionate and form strong bonds with their families. They are often good with children if properly socialized and raised together. However, their size and strength mean supervision is always necessary, especially with very young children. Their independent nature requires consistent training.

### What are the exercise needs of a large Husky-like dog?
Large Husky-like dogs, such as Alaskan Malamutes and Greenland Dogs, have high exercise needs. They were bred for endurance and hauling. They require daily vigorous exercise, including long walks, runs, or hikes. Activities like sledding, canicross, or backpacking are excellent for engaging their working instincts. Lack of exercise can lead to boredom and destructive behavior.

### Do large Northern breeds shed a lot?
Yes, large Northern breeds shed a lot, particularly due to their thick double coats. They experience seasonal "blowing coat" twice a year. During these periods, they shed heavily to prepare for changing temperatures. Regular brushing, at least a few times a week, is essential. This helps manage the shedding and keeps their coat healthy.

### How do Malamutes compare to Huskies in temperament?
Malamutes are often described as more dignified and less mischievous than Huskies. They are affectionate and loyal to their families but can be more independent and strong-willed. Huskies are known for being more vocal and outgoing. Both breeds require consistent training and socialization. Malamutes generally have a higher prey drive.

### What is the lifespan of these larger breeds?
The lifespan of large Husky-like breeds varies slightly by breed. Alaskan Malamutes typically live for 10 to 12 years. Canadian Eskimo Dogs also have a similar lifespan, ranging from 10 to 15 years. Greenland Dogs generally live 10 to 14 years. Proper nutrition, regular exercise, and good veterinary care contribute to a longer, healthier life.

### What are common health issues in large Northern breeds?
Large Northern breeds can be prone to certain health issues. These include hip and elbow dysplasia, which affect joint development. Chondrodysplasia, or "dwarfism," can occur in Malamutes. Eye conditions like progressive retinal atrophy and cataracts are also sometimes seen. Bloat, a serious stomach condition, is another concern. Responsible breeders screen for these genetic issues.
