## How To Get Rid Of Wall Geckos Permanently

Seeing wall geckos scurrying across your ceilings and walls can be unsettling. While these small lizards help control insects, many people do not want them inside their homes. You might find their droppings or hear their chirps. We all want a clean, pest-free living space.

Getting rid of wall geckos permanently involves a combination of methods. You need to understand what attracts them and then take steps to make your home unwelcoming. This article provides practical, actionable advice. We will cover everything from pest control to home maintenance, helping you reclaim your space from these tiny invaders.

### Takeaway

To get rid of wall geckos permanently, focus on these key actions:

*   **Eliminate Food Sources:** Reduce the insect population in your home, which geckos eat.
*   **Seal Entry Points:** Block all possible ways geckos enter your house.
*   **Use Natural Deterrents:** Apply scents and substances geckos avoid.
*   **Maintain Cleanliness:** Keep your home tidy to remove hiding spots and food for insects.
*   **Consider Humane Trapping:** Catch and release geckos found inside.
*   **Seek Professional Help:** Consult experts for severe or persistent infestations.

### Clear Answer

To get rid of wall geckos permanently, you must eliminate their food sources (insects) and seal all entry points into your home. Implement natural repellents, maintain excellent sanitation, and consider humane removal methods. This integrated approach ensures lasting gecko prevention and removal.

### Understanding Wall Geckos: Habits and Habitat

Wall geckos, often called house geckos, are common in many warm climates. They are small, nocturnal lizards. These geckos have special toe pads that allow them to cling to almost any surface. This ability makes them very adept at navigating walls and ceilings.

They primarily seek two things in your home: food and shelter. Their main diet consists of insects. This includes mosquitoes, flies, moths, cockroaches, and spiders. Your home provides a steady supply of these small creatures. Geckos are drawn to light sources because lights attract insects. This is why you often see them near windows or outdoor lamps.

Geckos also look for warm, damp, and secure places. Cracks in walls, gaps around windows, and spaces behind furniture offer ideal hiding spots. They use these areas for rest during the day and for laying eggs. Understanding these behaviors helps us plan effective removal strategies. We can target their needs directly. We focus on making your home less appealing to them.

Common species like the Common House Gecko (Hemidactylus frenatus) are particularly adaptable. They thrive in human dwellings. Recognizing these habits is the first step. It allows us to disrupt their presence in your living areas. We aim to remove their reasons for being there.

### Eliminating Food Sources to Deter Geckos

Getting rid of wall geckos permanently starts with cutting off their food supply. Geckos come indoors because they find plenty of insects. If there are no insects, there will be no geckos. This is a simple truth in pest control. My approach is always to make the environment unwelcoming.

Controlling insects requires a multi-faceted plan. First, ensure all food is stored in airtight containers. Do not leave dirty dishes in the sink. Clean up crumbs and spills immediately after meals. These actions prevent cockroaches and ants, which are primary gecko snacks, from thriving. I always advise cleaning kitchen surfaces thoroughly every day.

Moisture attracts many insects, including mosquitoes and flies. Fix any leaky pipes or dripping faucets. Ensure proper drainage around your home's foundation. Reducing humidity indoors can also help. Use dehumidifiers in damp areas like basements or bathrooms. Fewer damp spots mean fewer insects.

Addressing mold is also important, as mold can attract certain insects that geckos eat. If you have mold, clean it promptly. This denies insects a food source and also improves air quality. [Learn how to get rid of mold](https://beacleaner.com/how-to-get-rid-of-mold-2) in your home. Regularly empty trash cans and keep them sealed. This prevents fruit flies and other pests.

Outdoor lighting can also draw insects to your home's exterior. Consider using yellow "bug lights" outside. These lights are less attractive to insects. You can also install screens on windows and doors. This physical barrier keeps flying insects out. By diligently managing your insect population, you effectively starve out the geckos. This makes your home a less desirable place for them.

### Sealing Entry Points to Block Geckos

Blocking how geckos enter your home is a critical step to get rid of them permanently. Geckos are small and agile. They can squeeze through tiny cracks and openings. Think of your home as a fortress you need to secure. My experience shows that overlooked gaps are often the culprits.

Inspect the exterior of your house thoroughly. Look for cracks in the foundation or walls. Use caulk to seal these openings. Small geckos can pass through surprisingly narrow spaces. Check around window frames and door frames. Apply weatherstripping or door sweeps to eliminate gaps. I often find gaps under doors are easy entry points for many pests, including geckos.

Vents and utility pipes that enter your home are also potential entry points. Ensure all vents have tight-fitting screens. Use sealant around pipes where they pass through walls. Pay attention to attic vents, chimney openings, and crawl space entrances. Cover these with fine mesh screens. This mesh keeps geckos and other small animals out.

Repair damaged window screens promptly. Even a small tear allows easy access. Check screens for proper installation. A secure screen fits snugly in its frame. If you have a garage, ensure the garage door seals tightly when closed. Gaps along the bottom or sides can be an invitation for geckos.

Regular maintenance is key. As your house settles, new cracks can form. Periodically re-inspect and re-seal any newly discovered openings. This constant vigilance ensures that geckos remain outside. We want to prevent them from setting up permanent residence inside your walls. Sealing all entry points creates a physical barrier. This barrier makes it nearly impossible for geckos to enter your home.

### Natural Repellents and DIY Gecko Deterrents

Using natural repellents is a humane way to deter geckos from your living spaces. These methods rely on scents or textures geckos dislike. This makes your home less appealing to them without causing harm. Many homeowners prefer these solutions over chemical alternatives. I like to start with the least intrusive methods first.

One common deterrent is **eggshells**. Geckos see eggshells as a predator, such as a bird. Place broken eggshells in areas where you often see geckos. Good spots include windowsills, door entries, or behind appliances. Replace the eggshells every few weeks to keep the scent fresh. I suggest using halves of eggshells rather than crushed pieces for better visual impact.

**Garlic and onion** are also effective. Their strong odors repel geckos. You can place garlic cloves or slices of onion near entry points. Some people make a spray by mixing garlic juice with water. Spray this mixture around windows and doors. The smell quickly dissipates for humans but remains potent for geckos.

**Coffee and tobacco powder** can also work. Mix coffee grounds with tobacco powder. Place small balls of this mixture in corners or cracks. The strong aroma irritates geckos. This causes them to leave the area. Be careful with tobacco, as it can be toxic if ingested by pets or children.

**Cayenne pepper or chili powder** creates an irritating sensation for geckos. Sprinkle a small amount in areas where geckos hide or enter. This acts as a physical deterrent. It does not harm them but makes the area uncomfortable. Again, use caution if you have pets.

**Naphthalene balls** (mothballs) are another option, though I advise caution. They contain chemicals that can be harmful if misused. Place them in cloth bags and position them in attics or behind heavy furniture. Ensure they are out of reach of children and pets. The strong smell is very effective at driving geckos away.

You can also use sticky traps, but do so with care. Place them along walls where geckos travel. If a gecko gets caught, release it gently by pouring vegetable oil onto the trap. This frees the gecko without injury. [Knowing how to wash a wall](https://beacleaner.com/how-to-wash-a-wall) can be helpful if you need to clean residue from these deterrents or if a gecko leaves droppings. These natural methods, when used consistently, make your home an unpleasant place for geckos.

### Maintaining a Clean Environment to Prevent Geckos

A clean home is a gecko-free home. Maintaining excellent sanitation prevents geckos from finding food and shelter. It removes incentives for them to stay. My experience shows that a tidy house greatly reduces pest activity. A clean house is not only pleasant for us but also unwelcoming to geckos.

Regular cleaning is fundamental. Dust all surfaces frequently. Vacuum carpets and sweep hard floors daily. This removes insect eggs, food crumbs, and small insects. Geckos rely on these tiny food sources. A clean floor offers nothing for them.

Decluttering also plays a big role. Piles of newspapers, stacks of boxes, or excessive items create hiding spots. Geckos love dark, undisturbed places to rest during the day. Remove clutter from attics, basements, and closets. Keep storage areas organized. This reduces available shelters for geckos.

Clean up food spills immediately. Even small sugar spills attract ants, which attract geckos. Wipe down kitchen counters after every meal. Ensure pet food bowls are not left out overnight. Store pet food in sealed containers. These actions cut off insect food supplies.

Washing walls and surfaces regularly also helps. Gecko droppings can leave marks. Keeping walls clean removes any residue that might attract other insects. It also makes your home feel fresh. [Learning how to clean wall paneling](https://beacleaner.com/how-to-clean-wall-paneling) can be part of this routine. A thorough clean removes cobwebs and insect nests, further reducing gecko food.

Address any moisture issues. Damp areas encourage insects. Fix leaks, use exhaust fans in bathrooms, and ensure good ventilation. Geckos are drawn to areas with higher humidity. Keeping your home dry makes it less hospitable. A consistently clean and dry home discourages geckos. It takes away their primary reasons for being inside. This is a crucial step to get rid of wall geckos permanently.

### Humane Trapping and Relocation Strategies

Sometimes, despite your best efforts, a gecko might still get inside. When this happens, humane trapping and relocation are good options. These methods allow you to remove the gecko without harming it. I always prefer a gentle approach when possible. It respects the animal while solving your problem.

One simple method is using a **box trap**. You can fashion a makeshift trap from a shoebox. Cut a small opening on one side. Place a piece of fruit or a dead insect inside as bait. Position the box near where you see the gecko. Once the gecko enters, you can carefully slide a piece of cardboard underneath the opening. This secures the gecko inside. Then, you can carry the box outside for release.

**Sticky traps** can be used, but require extra care for humane release. Place sticky traps along walls or in corners where geckos usually travel. Check these traps frequently, at least every few hours. If a gecko gets stuck, do not pull it off. Instead, pour a small amount of vegetable oil onto the gecko and the sticky surface. The oil dissolves the adhesive. The gecko can then wiggle free. Once free, gently guide it into a box for release outdoors. Make sure to release it away from your home.

You can also try direct capture with a cup and cardboard. When you spot a gecko on a wall, slowly place a cup over it. Then, slide a piece of stiff cardboard underneath the cup, trapping the gecko inside. Carry the cup outside and release the gecko. This method requires a steady hand and quick action.

When releasing geckos, choose an appropriate outdoor area. A shady spot with some vegetation is ideal. Avoid releasing them directly onto hot pavement or in open, exposed areas. Release them a good distance from your house to prevent them from returning. This ensures the gecko's survival and keeps it out of your home. Humane relocation is an effective way to deal with individual geckos that slip past your defenses.

### FAQ Section

#### How do geckos get inside homes?

Geckos enter homes through small cracks, gaps around windows and doors, and unscreened vents. They also come in through open doors. They seek warmth, shelter, and food. Any small opening can be an entry point. They are very skilled at finding these tiny openings.

#### What smells do geckos hate?

Geckos dislike strong smells like garlic, onion, and cayenne pepper. Naphthalene (mothballs) also repels them due to its potent odor. Some people report success with coffee grounds. These smells make your home unpleasant for them.

#### Are wall geckos harmful to humans?

No, wall geckos are generally not harmful to humans. They are not venomous. They do not carry diseases known to affect humans. They can bite if threatened, but their bite is usually harmless. They primarily help by eating insects.

#### What attracts geckos to a house?

Geckos are attracted to houses that offer ample insect food sources, warmth, and shelter. Outdoor lights that attract insects also draw geckos closer to your home. Cracks and crevices provide good hiding and breeding spots.

#### Can geckos lay eggs in my house?

Yes, female geckos can lay eggs in your house. They often choose secluded, warm, and dark spots. These include behind furniture, in cracks, or in undisturbed areas like attics or basements. You might find small, white, spherical eggs.

#### Is it possible to completely eradicate geckos from my property?

Achieving complete eradication can be challenging, but significant long-term control is possible. By eliminating food sources, sealing entry points, and consistently applying deterrents, you can make your property highly unattractive to geckos. Professional help offers the best chance for comprehensive control.
