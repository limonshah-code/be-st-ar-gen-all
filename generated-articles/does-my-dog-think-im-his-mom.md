This behavior mirrors a child returning to a parent.

The hormone oxytocin plays a key role in this bonding process. Oxytocin is often called the "love hormone." It increases in both dogs and humans during positive interactions. This means we control resources like food, shelter, and safety. Dogs recognize this role.

### FAQ Section

**1. Do dogs see other dogs as their family?**
Yes, dogs do see other dogs as family members, especially if they grew up together. They form social bonds with their canine companions. These relationships involve play, shared rest, and mutual comfort. A dog's social group can include both humans and other dogs. They learn social rules from these interactions.

**2. Can a male dog think I'm his mom?**
A male dog will form the same deep attachment to a human caregiver as a female dog. He will not biologically think you are his mom. However, he will rely on you as his primary provider and protector. This bond functions like a maternal relationship in terms of care and security.

**3. How do dogs show they love their owners?**
Dogs show love in many ways. They may wag their tail, lick you, lean on you, or follow you around. Bringing you toys, seeking physical contact, and greeting you enthusiastically are also signs. Making eye contact and sleeping near you indicate trust and affection.

**4. Is it bad if my dog is too attached to me?**
A strong attachment is healthy. However, excessive attachment can become separation anxiety. This occurs when a dog cannot cope with being alone. If your dog shows distress, destructive behavior, or excessive vocalization when you leave, it may be too much. It needs careful management.

**5. Do dogs understand human language?**
Dogs understand certain words through association. They do not understand human language in the same way humans do. They connect specific sounds to actions or objects. Their understanding relies heavily on tone of voice, body language, and context. They are very good at reading our cues.

**6. What is the difference between an owner and a parent in a dog's eyes?**
A dog does not differentiate between "owner" and "parent" in human terms. They perceive you as their primary caregiver and protector. This role encompasses both ownership (providing resources) and parental care (providing comfort, safety, and emotional support). You are their trusted family member.
