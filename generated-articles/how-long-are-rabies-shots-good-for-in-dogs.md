### Q1: Can my dog get rabies if its vaccine lapsed?

Yes, a dog whose rabies vaccine has lapsed can absolutely get rabies. The vaccine provides immunity for a specific period. Once that period ends, their protection decreases. ### Q2: How often do dogs need rabies shots?

Dogs need rabies shots either every one year or every three years. This depends on the specific vaccine type used. After their initial puppy shot (around 12-16 weeks), they receive a booster one year later. Subsequent boosters follow the 1-year or 3-year cycle. Always follow your vet's advice.

### Q3: Is the 1-year rabies shot less effective than the 3-year shot?

No, the 1-year rabies shot is not less effective than the 3-year shot. Both provide strong immunity against the rabies virus. The difference lies in their licensed duration of efficacy. Both vaccines undergo rigorous testing. They are proven to protect for their respective durations.

### Q4: What are the common side effects of a rabies vaccine in dogs?

Most dogs tolerate the rabies vaccine well. Mild side effects can occur. These include soreness at the injection site, mild fever, or temporary lethargy. More severe reactions are rare. These can include allergic reactions. Always monitor your dog after vaccination. Contact your vet if you notice anything unusual.

### Q5: What should I do if my dog bites someone and its vaccine is expired?

If your dog bites someone and its rabies vaccine is expired, contact your veterinarian and local animal control immediately. Authorities will consider your dog as "unvaccinated." This will trigger specific protocols. These often include mandatory quarantine and re-vaccination. Legal consequences can also apply.

### Q6: At what age can a puppy get its first rabies vaccine?

Puppies typically receive their first rabies vaccine when they are between 12 and 16 weeks old. This timing allows their immune system to respond properly. It ensures they build effective immunity. This initial shot is followed by a crucial booster one year later.
