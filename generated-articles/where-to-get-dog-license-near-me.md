## Common Dog Licensing Misconceptions and FAQs

Many dog owners have questions about licensing. Let's clear up some common misunderstandings. Knowing these facts helps you comply with local laws and ensures your pet's safety.

**"My dog is microchipped, so it's licensed."**
This is a common misconception. Microchipping and licensing are different. A microchip is a permanent form of identification implanted under your dog's skin. A license is a physical tag issued by your local government. Both are valuable for pet identification, but one does not replace the other. You need both for maximum protection.

**"Indoor dogs don't need a license."**
This is not true. All dogs, regardless of whether they spend most of their time indoors or outdoors, require a license. Licensing laws apply to all pet dogs within a jurisdiction. An indoor dog can still get lost or escape. The license helps ensure their safe return.

**"Puppies are exempt from licensing."**
Puppies typically become eligible for licensing after their first rabies vaccination. This usually happens around three to four months of age. Most jurisdictions have an age requirement for licensing. You must license your puppy once they meet this age and vaccination criteria. Check your local ordinances for specific age rules.

**"The license is only for finding lost dogs."**
While finding lost dogs is a primary benefit, licensing serves other purposes. It helps animal control track animal populations. It ensures pets are vaccinated against rabies. License fees also fund crucial animal welfare services in your community.

**"I just moved, my old license is still valid."**
No, you typically need to obtain a new dog license in your new jurisdiction. Dog licenses are specific to the city or county where you reside. Your old license from a different area will not be valid. You must re-register your dog with your new local animal control or clerk's office.
