### What is an Aussie and Corgi mix called?

The most popular and widely recognized name for an Aussie and Corgi mix is an **Augie**. This name is a blend of "Aussie" from the Australian Shepherd and "Corgi" from the Pembroke or Cardigan Welsh Corgi. Sometimes, people also refer to it as an Aussie Corgi, a Corgi Australian Shepherd mix, or an Australian Shepherd Corgi mix. However, "Augie" is the affectionate and common term you will hear most often.

## Parent Breeds: Understanding the Australian Shepherd and Corgi

To truly appreciate an Augie, we must first understand its remarkable parent breeds. The Australian Shepherd and the Corgi are both highly capable and unique dogs. Each breed brings distinct characteristics to the mix. Their combined traits create the Augie's fascinating personality and appearance.

The Australian Shepherd is a medium-sized dog known for its intelligence and athleticism. It excels in herding and dog sports. Corgis, on the other hand, are smaller herding dogs with famously short legs and big personalities. They are sturdy and surprisingly quick. Both breeds share a working dog background.

Understanding these parent breeds helps predict what an Aussie and Corgi mix might be like. The Augie will inherit a blend of their physical features and temperaments. Genetic lottery means no two Augies are exactly alike. However, knowing the parents provides a good blueprint. This knowledge helps future owners prepare for their new family member.

### The Energetic Australian Shepherd

The Australian Shepherd is a highly intelligent and energetic dog. Aussies are known for their striking coats and piercing eyes, often blue or bi-colored. They are true working dogs, originally bred for herding livestock. This means they possess a strong drive to work and need a purpose.

An Aussie requires a lot of mental and physical stimulation. They thrive when given tasks or engaged in dog sports like agility. Without enough activity, an Aussie can become bored or destructive. Their loyalty to their family is unwavering, making them excellent companions. They often form strong bonds with their owners.

Aussies are also very trainable due to their high intelligence. They pick up commands quickly and enjoy learning new things. Their herding instinct can sometimes lead them to "herd" children or other pets. Early socialization and training help manage this trait. They are protective of their families and can be wary of strangers.

Their moderate size, typically ranging from 40 to 65 pounds, makes them formidable herders. Their coat requires regular grooming to prevent mats. Overall, the Australian Shepherd contributes intelligence, energy, and loyalty to the Augie. These are wonderful traits for any dog to have.

### The Spirited Pembroke Welsh Corgi

The Pembroke Welsh Corgi is a small but mighty dog with a bold personality. Despite their short stature, Corgis were also bred for herding. They would nip at the heels of cattle to move them along. This background gives them a confident and often bossy demeanor.

Corgis are highly intelligent and eager to please, though they can be stubborn sometimes. They respond well to positive reinforcement training. Their compact size makes them suitable for various living situations, including apartments. However, they still need regular exercise to stay healthy.

These dogs are known for their sturdy build, foxy faces, and expressive ears. Their double coat sheds quite a bit, especially seasonally. Corgis are generally friendly and good with children and other pets when properly socialized. They can be vocal, using barks to communicate.

The Corgi parent contributes a strong personality, a low-rider physique, and a natural herding instinct to the Augie. They are known for their charming and often humorous antics. Their compact size and loving nature make them popular choices for families. This breed adds a lot of character to the Aussie Corgi mix.

## Physical Traits of an Aussie Corgi Mix: What to Expect

When you combine an Australian Shepherd and a Corgi, you get a dog with a unique appearance. The physical traits of an Augie can vary widely. It depends on which parent's genes are more dominant. However, we can generally predict some common characteristics for this charming mix.

Most Augies inherit the Corgi's shorter stature but often with a slightly longer body. They tend to have a sturdy build. Their size will be somewhere between their two parent breeds. They often have expressive eyes, sometimes with the captivating blue or bi-colored eyes of an Aussie. Their ears might be upright like a Corgi or semi-erect like an Aussie.

Their coat is usually dense and medium in length. It often has the beautiful patterns and colors seen in Australian Shepherds. This includes merle (blue merle, red merle), black, and red, with white and tan markings. Regular grooming is necessary to maintain their coat health. I love seeing the variety in their looks.

### Size and Stature of the Augie

The size of an Augie is one of its most variable traits. It largely depends on the specific parent dogs and genetic inheritance. Generally, an Augie will be larger than a purebred Corgi but smaller than a purebred Australian Shepherd. They usually stand between 10 to 18 inches tall at the shoulder.

Their weight can range from 20 to 50 pounds. This makes them a medium-sized dog overall. The Corgi parent's short legs are often a dominant trait. This gives the Augie its distinct low-slung appearance. Despite their shorter legs, they are surprisingly agile and quick.

Consider the potential size when planning for an Augie's living space and exercise needs. A larger Augie might require more room and activity. ### Coat and Color Variations

The coat of an Aussie Corgi mix is a beautiful blend of both parents. They typically have a double coat. This coat is medium in length and dense, providing protection from the elements. They will shed, particularly during seasonal changes. Regular brushing is important for managing this shedding.

The colors and patterns are where the Australian Shepherd genetics truly shine through. Augies often display the stunning merle patterns. These can be blue merle or red merle. They also come in solid black or red, usually with white and tan copper points. Each Augie's coat is unique.

Some Augies may have the Corgi's solid colors with white markings. Others will have a full range of Aussie colors. I find their diverse coats truly eye-catching. No matter the color, their fur requires consistent care. This helps keep them looking their best and prevents matting.

## Caring for Your Augie: Health, Grooming, and Training

Caring for an Augie involves understanding their specific needs. They require attention to their health, regular grooming, and consistent training. Providing good care ensures a happy and healthy life for your Aussie Corgi mix. Let's look at each aspect in detail.

First, regular veterinary check-ups are crucial. This helps catch any potential health issues early. Second, their beautiful double coat needs regular maintenance. Finally, their intelligence and energy mean training must be ongoing. I find that a consistent routine benefits both the dog and the owner.

A balanced diet tailored to their activity level and size is also important. High-quality dog food provides essential nutrients. Fresh water must always be available. Good care is an investment in your Augie's well-being. It helps them thrive and be a joyful part of your family.

### Common Health Concerns

Like all breeds and mixes, Augies can be prone to certain health conditions inherited from their parent breeds. It's important for potential owners to be aware of these. Responsible breeders will screen their parent dogs for genetic conditions. This helps ensure healthier puppies.

Common health issues for the Aussie Corgi mix can include:

*   **Hip and Elbow Dysplasia**: These are common joint problems.
*   **Eye Conditions**: Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA) and Collie Eye Anomaly (CEA) from the Aussie side.
*   **Degenerative Myelopathy (DM)**: A spinal cord disease found in Corgis.
*   **Von Willebrand's Disease**: A bleeding disorder.
*   **Back Problems**: Due to their longer bodies and shorter legs, similar to Corgis.
*   **Patellar Luxation**: Dislocated kneecaps.
*   **Obesity**: Corgis are prone to weight gain, so an Augie can be too.

Regular veterinary care, a healthy diet, and appropriate exercise help manage these risks. Discussing health clearances with a breeder is very important. Understanding these potential issues helps you provide the best preventative care. ### Grooming Your Aussie Corgi Mix

The Augie inherits a double coat from both its Australian Shepherd and Corgi parents. This beautiful coat provides insulation but requires regular grooming. I know that managing their fur can be a commitment. Consistent care keeps their coat healthy and reduces shedding.

Here are some grooming tips for your Augie:

*   **Brushing**: Brush your Augie 2-3 times a week, or daily during shedding seasons. Use a slicker brush and an undercoat rake to remove loose fur.
*   **Bathing**: Bathe them as needed, typically every 1-3 months. Use a dog-specific shampoo. Avoid frequent bathing, as it can strip natural oils.
*   **Nail Trimming**: Trim their nails every 3-4 weeks. Long nails can cause discomfort and lead to foot problems.
*   **Ear Cleaning**: Check and clean their ears weekly to prevent infections. Use a veterinarian-approved ear cleaner.
*   **Dental Care**: Brush their teeth several times a week. Dental hygiene is crucial for overall health.

Regular grooming sessions are also a great opportunity to check for any skin issues or parasites. Making grooming a positive experience from a young age helps your Augie tolerate it well. A well-groomed Augie looks and feels better.

### Training and Mental Stimulation

Training an Aussie Corgi mix is a rewarding experience due to their intelligence. They love to learn. However, their smarts also mean they need consistent mental stimulation. Without it, they might get bored and become destructive. I believe in making training fun.

Key aspects of training an Augie include:

*   **Early Socialization**: Introduce your puppy to various people, places, and other friendly dogs. This builds confidence and good manners.
*   **Positive Reinforcement**: Use treats, praise, and toys to reward desired behaviors. This is very effective for intelligent dogs like the Augie.
*   **Consistency**: All family members should use the same commands and rules. Consistency helps your Augie understand expectations.
*   **Obedience Training**: Enroll in puppy classes and continue with basic and advanced obedience. This provides structure and mental exercise.
*   **Mental Games**: Incorporate puzzle toys, scent work, and new tricks into their routine. These activities challenge their minds.

Remember, an Augie has strong herding instincts. Channel this energy into appropriate activities like fetch or herding games. Avoid harsh training methods, as they can damage your bond. A well-trained and mentally stimulated Augie is a happy and well-behaved companion.

If you enjoy hiking, jogging, or long walks, an Augie could be your perfect partner. They excel in dog sports like agility, flyball, and obedience. These activities challenge both their body and mind. Providing outlets for their energy is key to their happiness and good behavior.

Apartment living can work for an Augie if owners commit to multiple daily exercise sessions. Simply letting them out in a small yard is not enough. They need structured activity and engagement. The intelligence of an Aussie Corgi mix means they are highly trainable. However, this also means they require a consistent and committed approach to training. They can be headstrong at times, so patience is essential. Early and ongoing training helps them become well-mannered family members.

Socialization is another crucial aspect for an Augie. Exposure to various people, places, and other animals from a young age is vital. This helps prevent fear or aggression issues. A well-socialized Augie is confident and friendly. I always stress the importance of early puppy classes.

An Augie can be a wonderful family dog, particularly with older children. Their playful and affectionate nature makes them great companions. However, their herding instinct can sometimes be an issue with very young children. They might try to "herd" kids by nipping at their heels.

## Finding an Aussie Corgi Mix Puppy: Responsible Breeding

Finding an Aussie Corgi mix puppy, or an Augie, requires careful research. You want to ensure you are getting a healthy and well-socialized puppy. Responsible breeding practices are crucial for the well-being of the dogs. Avoid backyard breeders or puppy mills at all costs. I advocate for ethical sourcing of pets.

Responsible breeders prioritize the health and temperament of their dogs. They conduct health screenings on the parent dogs. This helps prevent genetic diseases. They also provide a clean and stimulating environment for the puppies. A good breeder will be transparent about their practices.

Consider adopting from a rescue organization if you prefer. Many wonderful Augies or similar mixes need loving homes. This option provides a second chance for a dog. Always meet the puppy and its parents if possible. Ask many questions about their health and history.

### What to Look for in a Responsible Augie Breeder

Choosing a responsible breeder is perhaps the most important step in getting an Augie. A good breeder will demonstrate care and knowledge about both parent breeds. They focus on producing healthy and well-adjusted puppies. I believe in supporting ethical practices.

Here are key indicators of a responsible breeder:

*   **Health Clearances**: They should provide proof of health screenings for both parent dogs. Look for clearances for common issues like hip/elbow dysplasia, eye conditions, and genetic diseases specific to Aussies and Corgis.
*   **Transparency**: A good breeder will be open about their breeding practices. They allow you to visit their facility and meet the parent dogs. They answer all your questions honestly.
*   **Puppy Environment**: Puppies should be raised in a clean, safe, and stimulating environment. They should be well-socialized from birth.
*   **Knowledge**: The breeder should be knowledgeable about the temperament, health, and needs of the Augie mix. They can provide advice on care.
*   **Contract and Guarantees**: They offer a written contract. This includes health guarantees and a spay/neuter agreement.
*   **References**: They can provide references from previous puppy buyers or their veterinarian.
*   **No Pressure**: They will not pressure you into a sale. They care about finding good homes for their puppies.

A responsible breeder ensures the best start for your Augie. They want their puppies to go to loving and prepared homes. Always be wary of breeders who offer multiple breeds or cannot show you the parent dogs.
