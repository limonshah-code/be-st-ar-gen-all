A Blue Long Haired Chihuahua is a rare variant of the Chihuahua breed. They possess a dilute black gene, giving them a stunning blue-gray coat. Coupled with the long-haired gene, this creates a distinctive, elegant appearance. They share typical Chihuahua traits, but require special grooming.

## Defining the Blue Long Haired Chihuahua: Rarity and Genetics

The term "blue" in dogs refers to a specific coat color that appears as a dilute shade of black. This is not a true blue color, like the sky. Instead, it looks like a soft, smoky gray or slate. For a Chihuahua to be blue, it must inherit a specific dilute gene from both parents.

This dilute gene, often denoted as 'd', acts on the black pigment. If a dog has two copies of the dilute gene (dd), any black areas in its coat will turn blue. A blue long-haired Chihuahua combines this rare dilute gene with the gene for long hair. The long-haired gene is also recessive, meaning the dog must inherit it from both parents. This dual recessive inheritance makes blue long-haired Chihuahuas truly rare.

The probability of breeding a blue long-haired Chihuahua is low. Both parents must carry both the dilute gene and the long-haired gene. Even then, the chances for each puppy are 25% for both traits. This genetic lottery explains why these dogs are not common. Many people misunderstand the 'blue' color, thinking it is a designer color created artificially. It is a natural genetic variation.

This specific genetic combination results in a dog with a beautiful, flowing blue-gray coat. The rarity of this specific color and coat type makes them highly sought after by enthusiasts. Their unique appearance draws immediate attention and admiration. Understanding their genetics helps appreciate their special place within the Chihuahua breed.

## Physical Characteristics of a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua

The blue long-haired Chihuahua possesses a striking appearance that sets it apart. Their most defining feature is, of course, their coat. This coat exhibits a range of blue-gray shades, from a light silver-blue to a deeper slate gray. The long hair adds to this beauty, giving them a flowing, elegant look.

The coat itself is soft to the touch and can be either flat or slightly wavy. It forms fringes on the ears, legs, and tail, creating a delicate outline. Despite the length, the hair is generally fine and silky, not coarse or wiry. This requires regular care to keep it free from mats and tangles. Many owners also consider if they [should shave a long-haired Chihuahua](https://beacleaner.com/should-you-shave-a-long-haired-chihuahua) to manage their coat, which can be a complex decision.

Their eyes often complement their blue coat. You might find a blue long-haired Chihuahua with lighter eye colors, such as amber, light brown, or even blue. This lighter eye color is sometimes associated with the dilute gene. It adds to their distinct and captivating gaze. Their nose and paw pads will also be a gray or slate color, matching their coat.

Aside from their unique coloring and coat, they maintain all standard Chihuahua characteristics. They are small dogs, typically weighing between 2 to 6 pounds. Their heads are apple-shaped, their ears are large and erect, and their bodies are compact. Their delicate frame and spirited demeanor are classic Chihuahua traits. These physical attributes combine to create a truly beautiful and charming companion.

## Temperament and Personality: What to Expect

Blue long-haired Chihuahuas, despite their unique looks, share the classic Chihuahua temperament. They are known for being incredibly loyal and devoted to their owners. These small dogs form strong bonds and often prefer to be near their human companions. Many consider them shadow dogs because they follow their owners everywhere.

They possess a surprisingly big personality for their small size. Chihuahuas are often bold, confident, and sometimes a bit feisty. They are not afraid to stand up for themselves, even against much larger dogs. This makes early socialization very important. Proper exposure to different people, places, and other animals helps them grow into well-adjusted adults.

These intelligent dogs respond well to positive reinforcement training. They enjoy learning new tricks and can excel in obedience. Their alertness makes them excellent watchdogs, always ready to alert you to visitors or unusual sounds. They will bark to protect their home and family.

Living with a blue long-haired Chihuahua means having an active and loving companion. They thrive on attention and affection. Despite their occasional stubborn streak, their charming antics and unwavering loyalty make them wonderful pets. Providing them with consistent training and plenty of love will ensure they become cherished family members.

## Essential Care for Your Blue Long Haired Chihuahua

Caring for a blue long-haired Chihuahua requires attention to their specific needs, especially concerning their beautiful coat. Regular grooming is paramount to keep their long hair healthy and mat-free. You should brush your Chihuahua several times a week, if not daily. This prevents tangles, removes loose hair, and distributes natural oils.

Bathing should be done as needed, typically once every 3-4 weeks. Use a gentle dog shampoo and conditioner to maintain coat health. Be sure to rinse thoroughly to avoid skin irritation. After bathing, gently towel dry and then use a low-heat blow dryer while brushing to prevent mats. It is important to avoid shaving your long-haired Chihuahua unless absolutely necessary for health reasons. Shaving can alter their coat texture and may not grow back properly. You can learn more about this decision by reading [Should You Shave a Long Haired Chihuahua?](https://beacleaner.com/should-you-shave-a-long-haired-chihuahua).

Beyond coat care, dental hygiene is crucial for Chihuahuas. They are prone to dental disease due to their small mouths. Brush their teeth daily with dog-specific toothpaste. Regular professional dental cleanings at the vet are also often needed. Nail trims are another important part of routine care. Trim their nails every few weeks to prevent discomfort and foot problems.

Proper nutrition supports their overall health. Feed your Chihuahua high-quality small-breed dog food. Monitor portion sizes to prevent obesity, which can put strain on their tiny joints. Even though they are small, Chihuahuas need regular exercise. Short, daily walks and indoor play sessions keep them physically and mentally stimulated. Remember, their small size means they tire easily, so short bursts of activity are best.

## Health Considerations Unique to Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas

Blue long-haired Chihuahuas, like all dogs, can be prone to certain health issues. Some concerns are common to the Chihuahua breed, while others relate directly to their "blue" dilute coat color. Understanding these potential health problems helps you provide the best possible care.

General Chihuahua health issues include patellar luxation, a condition where the kneecap slips out of place. Heart problems, such as patent ductus arteriosus (PDA), are also seen in the breed. Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar) can affect puppies, especially very tiny ones. Dental disease is extremely common due to their small mouths. Regular veterinary check-ups are essential to catch these issues early.

The "blue" dilute coat color can sometimes be linked to a specific condition called Color Dilution Alopecia (CDA). This is not exclusive to Chihuahuas; it affects several breeds with dilute coat colors. CDA causes thinning hair, dry skin, and recurrent skin infections. The blue coat may appear dull or patchy. Some blue Chihuahuas might live their entire lives without developing CDA. Other blue Chihuahuas may show symptoms.

Symptoms of CDA usually appear between 6 months and 2 years of age. They include brittle hair shafts, hair loss, and flaky skin. There is no cure for CDA, but management focuses on alleviating symptoms. This includes special shampoos, moisturizers, and sometimes antibiotics for secondary infections. Responsible breeders screen their dogs for health conditions. They avoid breeding dogs that show signs of CDA or carry genes linked to severe health problems. This practice helps ensure healthier puppies.

When considering a blue long-haired Chihuahua, ask your breeder about their health screening practices. A good breeder will be transparent about potential health risks. They will provide health clearances for the parents of the puppy. This proactive approach helps secure a healthy companion. The decision to groom or even [shave a long-haired Chihuahua](https://beacleaner.com/should-you-shave-a-long-haired-chihuahua) can impact skin health, especially for dogs potentially predisposed to skin conditions. Always discuss grooming options with your vet.

## Finding and Choosing a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua Puppy

Finding a blue long-haired Chihuahua puppy requires careful research and patience. Due to their rarity, these puppies are not as readily available as more common Chihuahua colors. The most important step is to find a reputable and ethical breeder. A good breeder prioritizes the health and temperament of their dogs above all else.

Begin your search by looking for breeders who specialize in Chihuahuas. Ask if they have experience with blue lines. A responsible breeder will be transparent about their breeding practices. They will show you the puppy's parents and their living conditions. They will also provide health clearances for the parent dogs, especially regarding genetic conditions common in Chihuahuas. This is vital to ensure you get a healthy puppy.

Beware of breeders who offer puppies at unusually low prices or cannot answer your questions thoroughly. Avoid breeders who push you to make a quick decision. Never buy a puppy without seeing it and its parents in person if possible. Online scams are common, so be very cautious. A good breeder will also ask you questions to ensure you can provide a good home.

The cost of a blue long-haired Chihuahua puppy can be higher than other colors due to their rarity. Factor in ongoing expenses for food, vet care, and supplies. While rare, sometimes blue long-haired Chihuahuas appear in rescue organizations. This offers another ethical path to finding one of these special dogs. Choosing wisely ensures a healthy and happy start for your new companion.

## FAQ Section

### Are blue long-haired Chihuahuas rare?
Yes, blue long-haired Chihuahuas are considered rare. They require a specific genetic combination for both the dilute 'blue' color and the long hair. Both traits are recessive, meaning a puppy needs to inherit the responsible gene from both parents. This makes them less common than other Chihuahua varieties.

### Do blue Chihuahuas have health problems?
Blue Chihuahuas can be susceptible to general Chihuahua health issues like patellar luxation and dental disease. Additionally, the dilute gene responsible for their blue coat can sometimes link to Color Dilution Alopecia (CDA). CDA causes hair loss and skin problems, but not all blue Chihuahuas develop it.

### How much does a blue long-haired Chihuahua cost?
The cost of a blue long-haired Chihuahua puppy varies widely. Due to their rarity, they are often more expensive than standard Chihuahuas. Prices typically range from $1,500 to $5,000 or even higher. Factors such as breeder reputation, pedigree, and location influence the final price.

### What is the lifespan of a long-haired Chihuahua?
Long-haired Chihuahuas generally have a good lifespan. With proper care, nutrition, and regular veterinary check-ups, they can live for 12 to 20 years. Their small size often contributes to their longer life expectancy compared to larger dog breeds. Healthy genetics also play a key role.

### Do blue Chihuahuas shed a lot?
Long-haired Chihuahuas do shed, but usually not excessively compared to some other long-haired breeds. They typically shed moderately year-round, with heavier shedding during seasonal changes. Regular brushing helps manage loose hair and keeps their coat healthy. Brushing several times a week is often recommended.

### Are blue Chihuahuas recognized by kennel clubs?
Yes, blue Chihuahuas are recognized by most major kennel clubs, including the American Kennel Club (AKC). While the blue color itself is recognized, individual dogs are registered as Chihuahuas. The focus is on the breed standard, which includes various coat colors. They participate in events as Chihuahuas.
