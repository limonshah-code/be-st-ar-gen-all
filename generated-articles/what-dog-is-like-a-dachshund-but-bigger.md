## Exploring Dogs Like Dachshunds, But Bigger

Dachshunds are truly unique dogs, known for their long bodies, short legs, and bold personalities. Many people adore these "weiner dogs" but sometimes wish for a similar companion in a larger size. Perhaps you love the low-rider appearance or the distinct hound-like traits, but a standard Dachshund feels too small. Finding a dog that shares these beloved characteristics, yet comes with a bit more stature, can be a fun challenge. We are going to explore several dog breeds that fit this interesting description. This article will guide you through various options, from purebred alternatives to larger Dachshund types, helping you discover your next canine friend.

They are intelligent, brave, and often possess a strong prey drive.

### Why the Dachshund Look Resonates

The Dachshund's silhouette is truly iconic. Its elongated body and short legs create an endearing and sometimes comical appearance. This body type also points to a rich history as a working dog. They are built for digging and navigating tight spaces.

## Basset Hounds: The Quintessential Bigger Dachshund

When people ask, "What dog is like a Dachshund but bigger?", the Basset Hound often comes to mind first. These dogs share many visual similarities with their smaller cousins. Basset Hounds have very long bodies and remarkably short, sturdy legs. Their low-slung posture is unmistakable.

They also possess characteristic long, droopy ears and a perpetually mournful expression. This look gives them a unique charm. However, Basset Hounds are significantly heavier and larger than Dachshunds, typically weighing between 40-65 pounds. They are true scent hounds, known for their incredible sense of smell and their deep, melodious bay.

### Basset Hound Temperament and Care

Basset Hounds are generally mellow and good-natured dogs. They are known for being patient and tolerant, making them good family pets. Unlike the often feisty Dachshund, Bassets are usually more laid-back. However, their stubborn streak can rival that of a Dachshund. Training them requires patience and positive reinforcement. Their powerful nose can lead them astray, so a secure yard is essential.

Daily exercise is important, but they are not overly energetic. A good walk or a sniff-filled adventure satisfies them. Grooming involves regular brushing and special attention to their long ears to prevent infections. ## Swedish Vallhunds: A Nordic "Dachshund-Like" Breed

The Swedish Vallhund is another fascinating breed that fits the description of a dog like a Dachshund but bigger. These ancient Nordic dogs are often called "Viking dogs" due to their history. They were bred to herd cattle, much like a Corgi, and share that distinctive long body and short leg structure. They possess a robust, fox-like appearance with erect ears and a keen expression.

Swedish Vallhunds are medium-sized dogs, typically weighing 20-35 pounds and standing around 12-14 inches tall. This makes them noticeably larger than a standard Dachshund. Their agile build allows them to move quickly despite their short legs. They are very versatile and excel in various dog sports. Their coat is short to medium in length and comes in shades of grey, sable, and red.

### Living with a Swedish Vallhund

Swedish Vallhunds are intelligent, energetic, and eager to please. They are excellent companions for active families. Their herding instincts mean they love to have a job to do, and they can be quite vocal. Early socialization and consistent training are important for these bright dogs. They thrive on mental stimulation as much as physical exercise. A bored Vallhund can become a mischievous Vallhund.

These dogs are generally healthy and robust, with a lifespan of 12-15 years. Their short coat is relatively low maintenance, requiring regular brushing to keep it clean. Their unique appearance and friendly demeanor make them a wonderful choice for those seeking a larger, more athletic version of the low-rider dog. If you appreciate a dog with brains and brawn, a Vallhund could be perfect.

## Cardigan Welsh Corgis: Robust and Low-Slung

The Cardigan Welsh Corgi is a fantastic option if you are looking for a dog like a Dachshund but bigger and more substantial. Cardigans have a long, low body, very similar to a Dachshund, but they are heavier boned and larger overall. They typically weigh between 25-38 pounds and have a sturdy build. Their tail is long and bushy, distinguishing them from their Pembroke cousins.

Cardigans are one of the oldest dog breeds in Britain. They were bred to herd cattle, nipping at their heels and dodging kicks with their low stature. Their strong, powerful legs support a surprisingly agile body. They possess a fox-like head with large, erect ears. Their coat comes in many colors, including sable, brindle, black and white, and blue merle.

### Cardigan Corgi Personality and Needs

Cardigan Welsh Corgis are intelligent, loyal, and playful dogs. They are known for being devoted family members. They are generally outgoing and good with children and other pets, especially if socialized early. Their herding background means they can be watchful and sometimes bossy. Training them is usually straightforward due to their eagerness to learn.

These dogs require moderate exercise to stay healthy and happy. Daily walks, playtime, or even dog sports are great for them. Their double coat needs regular brushing to manage shedding, particularly during seasonal changes. They are robust and generally healthy, living 12-15 years. Their cheerful disposition and larger size make them an appealing alternative to the Dachshund.

## Pembroke Welsh Corgis: A Slight Variation

While very similar to the Cardigan, the Pembroke Welsh Corgi offers another option for a dog like a Dachshund but bigger. Pembrokes are slightly smaller and lighter than Cardigans, typically weighing 20-30 pounds. However, they are still significantly larger than most Dachshunds. The most notable physical difference is their lack of a long tail; Pembrokes usually have a naturally docked or very short tail.

Like Cardigans, Pembrokes have a long body and short legs, bred for herding cattle. They share the same distinctive low-slung profile and powerful build. Their fox-like face and erect ears are also common traits. Pembroke Corgis are widely popular due to their charming looks and energetic personalities. Queen Elizabeth II famously owned many Pembroke Corgis.

### Temperament and Care of Pembroke Corgis

Pembroke Welsh Corgis are known for being outgoing, intelligent, and affectionate. They are generally very social dogs and enjoy being part of family activities. They can be quite spirited and have a strong, independent streak. Their herding instincts might lead them to nip at heels, especially those of children, so early training is crucial. Positive reinforcement works best with these smart cookies.

These dogs need regular exercise to burn off their energy and prevent weight gain. Daily walks, games of fetch, or structured playtime are ideal. Their thick double coat sheds quite a bit, so regular brushing is necessary to keep it tidy and reduce hair around the house. Pembrokes are generally healthy, living 12-15 years. They are a beloved choice for people seeking a lively, larger low-rider.

## Petit Basset Griffon Vendéen: A Lively, Larger Dachshund Type

The Petit Basset Griffon Vendéen, often called PBGV, is another breed worth considering if you desire a dog like a Dachshund but bigger. These dogs are charming French scent hounds. They have a distinct "scruffy" appearance with rough, shaggy coats and long, low-set ears. Their bodies are longer than they are tall, giving them that familiar low-rider look, but they are much more substantial than a Dachshund.

PBGVs typically weigh between 25-40 pounds and stand 13-15 inches tall. This places them firmly in the "bigger" category compared to a Dachshund. They possess a cheerful and outgoing demeanor, known for their expressive eyes and wagging tails. They were bred to hunt rabbit and hare in the Vendée region of France, which accounts for their energetic and tenacious nature.

### The PBGV Lifestyle

Petit Basset Griffon Vendéens are lively, curious, and very friendly dogs. They thrive on human companionship and are known for their happy-go-lucky attitude. They can be quite vocal, using their baying bark to announce their presence or express excitement. Training them requires patience and consistency due to their independent hound nature. Their keen sense of smell means they love to explore.

These active dogs need plenty of exercise to keep them happy and fit. Daily walks, playtime in a secure yard, or even hiking are excellent activities. Their rough coat requires weekly brushing to prevent mats and keep them looking their best. PBGVs are generally healthy, with a lifespan of 12-14 years. If you want a bigger, scruffier, and equally endearing low-rider with a zest for life, a PBGV could be an excellent fit.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: What is the largest dog breed that looks like a Dachshund?
A1: The Basset Hound is generally considered the largest purebred dog that strongly resembles a Dachshund. They share the long body and short legs, but Bassets are much heavier and taller. They can weigh up to 65 pounds, offering a substantial presence.

### Q2: Are Corgis considered "bigger Dachshunds"?
A2: Corgis, both Cardigan and Pembroke Welsh varieties, are often seen as "bigger Dachshunds" due to their similar long body and short leg structure. They are significantly larger and heavier than standard Dachshunds, typically weighing between 20-38 pounds.

### Q3: Do any other purebred dogs have the long body and short legs like a Dachshund?
A3: Yes, besides Basset Hounds and Corgis, other breeds exhibit this unique body type. The Swedish Vallhund and the Petit Basset Griffon Vendéen (PBGV) are notable examples. These breeds vary in size, coat, and temperament but share the distinctive low-slung, elongated appearance.

### Q4: How do I choose between these larger Dachshund-like breeds?
A4: Consider your lifestyle and what you seek in a dog. Basset Hounds are more laid-back, while Corgis and Vallhunds are more energetic and active. PBGVs are lively and scruffy hounds. Research each breed's temperament, exercise needs, and grooming requirements to find the best match for your home.

### Q5: Can I find a bigger Dachshund through a mix breed?
A5: Yes, Dachshund mixes can result in a larger dog that retains some of the Dachshund's characteristics. For example, a Dachshund-Beagle or Dachshund-German Shepherd mix might be significantly bigger than a purebred Dachshund. Always research both parent breeds to understand potential traits and care needs.
