### FAQ Section

#### Q1: Do black-footed ferrets dig their own burrows?
No, black-footed ferrets do not typically dig their own burrows. They almost exclusively rely on abandoned or sometimes occupied burrows made by prairie dogs. This strong dependence on prairie dog structures is a unique aspect of their survival strategy. They adapt these existing tunnels for their own use.

#### Q2: What do black-footed ferrets eat?
Black-footed ferrets have a highly specialized diet. Their primary food source, making up over 90% of their diet, is prairie dogs. They are obligate carnivores. They hunt prairie dogs within their complex tunnel systems. They occasionally eat small rodents or birds if prairie dogs are scarce.

#### Q3: Are black-footed ferrets nocturnal?
Yes, black-footed ferrets are primarily nocturnal. They are most active during the night, hunting prairie dogs and moving between burrows. During the day, they typically rest and sleep deep inside the prairie dog dens. This nocturnal behavior helps them avoid many predators and the harsh daytime temperatures.

#### Q4: How long do black-footed ferrets live?
In the wild, black-footed ferrets typically live for about 3-4 years. In managed care or zoos, they can live longer, sometimes up to 5-8 years. Their lifespan is influenced by factors like food availability, predator presence, disease, and the overall health of their habitat.

#### Q5: Why are black-footed ferrets endangered?
Black-footed ferrets are endangered mainly due to habitat loss and disease. The widespread eradication of prairie dog towns in the 20th century removed their primary food source and homes. Sylvatic plague, a disease affecting prairie dogs, also devastated ferret populations.

#### Q6: Can black-footed ferrets live in other animals' burrows?
While black-footed ferrets primarily use prairie dog burrows, they have occasionally been observed using burrows created by other animals, such as badgers or ground squirrels. However, these are rare occurrences. Prairie dog burrows provide the optimal structure, stability, and access to their main food source for ferrets.
