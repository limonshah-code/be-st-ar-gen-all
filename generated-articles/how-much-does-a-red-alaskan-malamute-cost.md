### Health Concerns and Genetic Testing

Alaskan Malamutes are generally healthy. However, they are prone to certain genetic conditions. Reputable breeders screen for these issues. Common concerns include hip and elbow dysplasia, inherited eye diseases (like cataracts), and certain blood disorders. Chondrodysplasia, also known as dwarfism, is another genetic condition to be aware of.

*   **Hip and Elbow Dysplasia:** Can lead to arthritis and require surgery ($2,000-$6,000 per joint).
*   **Eye Conditions:** Regular ophthalmologist checks are vital ($50-$200 annually).
*   **Hypothyroidism:** Requires lifelong medication ($20-$50 per month).
*   **Bloat:** A life-threatening emergency requiring immediate surgery ($2,000-$7,000+).

Good breeders perform health clearances on parent dogs. This helps reduce the risk of these issues in puppies. Always ask for proof of these health checks. This ensures a healthier puppy and fewer vet bills for you.

### Size and Breed-Specific Needs

Alaskan Malamutes are large, powerful dogs. They need a spacious home and yard. Their size impacts the cost of everything. Larger crates, bigger beds, more food, and higher doses of medication all cost more. Their strength means they need durable equipment like leashes and harnesses.

*   **Space Requirements:** A home with a secure yard is ideal.
*   **Equipment:** Heavy-duty supplies withstand their strength and size.

Their size also means they need proper training. A large, untrained dog can be difficult to manage. Understanding their physical presence is key. ### Energy Levels and Exercise Requirements

Malamutes are working dogs with high energy levels. They need significant daily exercise. This is not just a walk around the block. They need long walks, runs, hikes, or activities like sledding or carting. A bored Malamute can become destructive or vocal. Providing outlets for their energy is crucial.
