## What Not To Do With A Corgi: Essential Mistakes to Avoid

Bringing a Corgi into your home is a joyous occasion. These dogs are famous for their charming looks and big personalities. However, like any breed, Corgis have specific needs and sensitivities. Understanding **what not to do with a Corgi** is key to their well-being. This knowledge helps you prevent common problems and ensure a happy life for your pet.

We will explore crucial areas where Corgi owners often make mistakes. This includes their diet, exercise, training, and general health. My goal is to guide you in avoiding these pitfalls. By the end, you will have clear actions to take and things to stop doing.

### Takeaway

*   Do not overfeed your Corgi; manage their weight carefully.
*   Never skip daily exercise sessions for your Corgi.
*   Avoid harsh training methods; use positive reinforcement instead.
*   Protect your Corgi's back by preventing jumping and falls.
*   Do not neglect regular grooming for their thick coat.
*   Never leave your Corgi alone for long periods; they need companionship.
*   Do not ignore early signs of health or behavioral issues.

### Clear, Concise Answer to the Main Query

To properly care for a Corgi, you must avoid overfeeding them, which leads to obesity and back problems. Do not neglect their daily exercise needs or ignore their strong herding instincts and intelligence. Always use positive training methods, protecting their long spine from jumping or rough play. Finally, never overlook regular grooming and social interaction for these smart, companion-loving dogs.

### Don't Neglect Proper Exercise: Corgi Energy Needs

Corgis are small dogs with surprisingly high energy levels. They were bred to herd cattle, which requires stamina and speed. Thinking a Corgi is a lazy lapdog is a common mistake. My Corgi needs daily activity to stay healthy and happy.

Many owners underestimate their exercise requirements. This leads to boredom, destructive behavior, and weight gain. A Corgi with too little exercise will invent its own fun, which often means chewing furniture or excessive barking. You must provide outlets for their natural energy.

**Daily Walks Are Crucial**

Your Corgi needs at least two substantial walks every day. These walks should be brisk and allow them to explore and sniff around. A short stroll around the block is not enough for an adult Corgi. I find that varied routes keep my Corgi more engaged.

Engage them in playtime in a secure yard. Fetch, puzzle toys, and short training sessions also help burn energy. Remember, mental stimulation is as important as physical activity for these intelligent dogs. They love to think and solve problems.

**Avoid Over-Exercising Puppies**

While adult Corgis need ample exercise, Corgi puppies require careful handling. Their bones and joints are still developing. Too much high-impact exercise can cause permanent damage. Always consult your vet about appropriate exercise for your puppy's age.

Short, frequent play sessions are better than long, strenuous activities for young Corgis. Focus on gentle play and short walks. As they grow, you can gradually increase the duration and intensity of their exercise. This approach protects their developing bodies.

You might be surprised by the energy of Corgis. Their unique body shape makes them distinctive pets, sometimes leading to questions about other popular [Corgi mixes](https://beacleaner.com/what-is-a-chihuahua-corgi-mix-called). Regardless of their lineage, exercise is a must.

### Never Overfeed Your Corgi: Managing Their Diet

One of the biggest mistakes Corgi owners make is overfeeding their dogs. Corgis love to eat and will often act hungry even when they are not. Their short stature can make weight gain seem less obvious at first glance. However, extra weight is extremely dangerous for Corgis.

Obesity puts immense strain on their long backs and short legs. This increases their risk for Intervertebral Disc Disease (IVDD), a severe spinal condition. Keeping your Corgi at a healthy weight is the single most important thing you can do for their long-term health. I learned this the hard way with my previous dog.

**Watch Their Weight Closely**

You must monitor your Corgi's weight regularly. Follow the feeding guidelines on your dog food bag, but adjust based on your dog's activity level. Your vet can give you an ideal weight range for your specific Corgi. You should be able to feel their ribs easily without seeing them.

Use a measuring cup to portion out meals precisely. Do not free-feed your Corgi by leaving food out all day. Divide their daily food allowance into two smaller meals. This helps manage hunger and metabolism.

**Avoid Human Food Scraps**

Resist the urge to share your human food with your Corgi. Many human foods are high in calories, fats, and ingredients that are toxic to dogs. A small bite to you can be a significant caloric intake for a Corgi. This practice quickly leads to weight gain and poor nutrition.

Table scraps also teach bad habits. Your Corgi will learn to beg and demand food from you. This behavior can be annoying and hard to break. Stick to dog-specific treats and use them sparingly for training rewards.

Healthy treats should be low in calories. Small pieces of carrots or green beans make good, safe alternatives. Always check with your vet before introducing new foods to your Corgi's diet.

### Don't Skip Training and Socialization: Corgi Behavior

Corgis are intelligent dogs with strong personalities. Some people describe them as stubborn, but they are often just very smart and eager to have their own way. Skipping early training and socialization is a big mistake. This can lead to a dog that is hard to manage.

They possess a strong herding instinct. This means they might try to "herd" children, other pets, or even you by nipping at heels. Early training helps redirect these natural behaviors into acceptable actions. A well-behaved Corgi is a joy to live with.

**Early Training is Key**

Start training your Corgi puppy as soon as you bring them home. Focus on basic obedience commands like "sit," "stay," "come," and "leave it." Use positive reinforcement methods like treats, praise, and toys. Harsh corrections are ineffective and can damage your bond.

Consistency is vital. Everyone in the household should use the same commands and expectations. Short, fun training sessions work best for Corgis. Their attention spans can be short, so keep it engaging.

**Address Barking Habits Early**

Corgis are known for being vocal dogs. They bark to alert, to play, or just to express themselves. While some barking is natural, excessive barking can become a problem for you and your neighbors. You must address this behavior early.

Teach a "quiet" command from a young age. Identify what triggers their barking and try to minimize those triggers. For example, if they bark at people passing by the window, block their view. Provide plenty of exercise and mental stimulation, as boredom often leads to barking.

Socialization is also a big part of training. Introduce your Corgi to many different people, places, sounds, and other dogs. Do this in a positive and controlled way. A well-socialized Corgi is more confident and less likely to react fearfully or aggressively.

Understanding the Corgi's distinct personality is essential. Their intelligence and spirit are part of what makes them so popular. ### Avoid Rough Handling and Back Strain: Protecting Your Corgi's Back

A Corgi's most distinct physical feature is their long body and short legs. This body type, characteristic of a chondrodysplastic breed, makes them prone to specific health issues, particularly spinal problems. Intervertebral Disc Disease (IVDD) is common in Corgis. This condition involves the discs in their spine bulging or rupturing, causing pain, nerve damage, and even paralysis. My vet always stresses protecting my Corgi's back.

Many owners unknowingly put their Corgi's spine at risk. Activities that seem harmless can cause severe injury. Understanding these risks is crucial for preventing painful and costly health problems. Prevention is always better than treatment.

**Support Their Long Spine**

Always support your Corgi's body when you pick them up. Place one hand under their chest and the other under their hindquarters. This keeps their spine straight and prevents unnecessary strain. Never pick them up by their front legs or under their armpits.

Teach children in your home how to handle the Corgi properly. Supervise all interactions to ensure gentle play. Rough play can easily lead to accidents. A Corgi's back is a sensitive area requiring constant care.

**Prevent Jumping Off Furniture**

Jumping on and off furniture is one of the biggest dangers for a Corgi's back. The impact of landing can put extreme stress on their spinal discs. This repeated stress can lead to IVDD over time. I strongly advise against letting them jump.

Provide dog ramps or stairs for them to use to get onto sofas or beds. This allows them to access elevated surfaces without risking injury. Begin using these aids when your Corgi is young. It helps them learn good habits early. Corgis are clever; they will adapt to using ramps.

Preventing falls is also important. Secure railings on stairs and block off areas where they might jump down from a height. Their eagerness can sometimes override their sense of caution. It is your job to keep them safe from these avoidable injuries.

### Don't Ignore Their Grooming Needs: Corgi Coat Care

Corgis have a beautiful double coat that provides insulation and protection. This coat, however, comes with specific grooming requirements that many new owners overlook. A common mistake is to neglect regular brushing, which leads to matting and excessive shedding. My house would be covered in Corgi hair without regular grooming.

Their double coat consists of a soft, dense undercoat and a coarser, weather-resistant outer coat. This type of coat requires consistent care to remain healthy. Improper grooming can affect their skin health and comfort. You need a consistent routine.

**Regular Brushing Is Essential**

You should brush your Corgi at least 2-3 times a week. During shedding season, which happens twice a year, daily brushing is often necessary. Use a slicker brush and an undercoat rake to remove loose fur. This prevents mats and keeps their coat healthy.

Brushing helps distribute natural oils, keeping their skin and coat shiny. It also reduces the amount of fur shed around your home. Regular grooming sessions are also a great opportunity to bond with your Corgi. Make it a positive experience with treats and praise.

**Avoid Shaving Their Coat**

A significant mistake owners make is shaving a Corgi's double coat. People often think this will keep the dog cooler in summer or reduce shedding. However, shaving a double-coated breed like a Corgi can cause more harm than good. I never shave my Corgi.

The double coat acts as natural insulation. It keeps them warm in winter and helps regulate their body temperature in summer. Shaving removes this natural protection, making them more susceptible to sunburn and heatstroke. It can also cause their coat to grow back unevenly or damaged.

Instead of shaving, focus on regular brushing to remove loose undercoat. This allows air to circulate and helps them stay cool naturally. Professional groomers can also offer deshedding services that safely remove excess fur without damaging the coat.

### Don't Treat Them Like a Small Lapdog: Understanding Corgi Temperament

While Corgis are undeniably cute and affectionate, it is a mistake to treat them solely as small lapdogs. They are intelligent, active, and have strong herding instincts. These dogs thrive when they have a sense of purpose and mental stimulation. My Corgi gets bored quickly without activities.

Underestimating their intelligence and independent spirit can lead to behavioral issues. A bored Corgi is often a mischievous Corgi. They need more than just cuddles; they need engagement. Their ancestry dictates much of their behavior.

**They Need a Job**

Corgis were bred to herd cattle, often nipping at their heels to move them. This "job" provided mental and physical stimulation. In a home environment, you need to find substitutes for this instinct. Give them tasks to do.

This can include advanced obedience training, agility classes, or scent work. Puzzle toys and interactive feeders can also provide mental challenges. Teaching them tricks or giving them "jobs" like fetching the newspaper can also fulfill this need. A working Corgi is a happy Corgi.

**Respect Their Herding Instincts**

Their herding instincts are strong and can manifest in various ways. They might try to gather family members, especially children, by nudging or nipping at heels. This is not aggression; it is simply their breed hardwired behavior. You must understand and manage it.

Early training and redirection are key. Teach them appropriate ways to interact with people and other pets. For example, you can teach them to play fetch instead of herding. Provide appropriate chew toys to satisfy their oral fixation. This helps them direct their instincts positively.

Corgis are also very social and thrive on interaction. They form strong bonds with their families. Many people love the Corgi's robust personality, making them popular. However, understanding the purebred temperament is vital for any Corgi owner.

### Don't Isolate Your Corgi: Social Needs and Attention

Corgis are highly social dogs who bond deeply with their families. They are not dogs that do well when left alone for long periods. Making the mistake of isolating your Corgi can lead to significant emotional distress and behavioral problems. My Corgi needs to be part of the family activities.

These dogs crave companionship and thrive on being involved in household life. They are intelligent and sensitive, so neglect can be very damaging. A lonely Corgi will often express their unhappiness through destructive behaviors. This is their way of saying they need you.

**Corgis Thrive on Interaction**

Your Corgi wants to be where you are. They are often described as "velcro dogs" because they love to stick close to their people. Include them in your daily routines as much as possible. This means letting them be in the same room as you and involving them in family time.

Regular play sessions, training, and just spending quiet time together are all important. Corgis are active participants in family life. They thrive on attention and affection from their human companions. This connection is vital for their mental health.

**Avoid Leaving Them Alone Too Long**

If you work long hours or have a very busy schedule, a Corgi might not be the best fit for your lifestyle. They can develop separation anxiety if left alone for extended periods. This anxiety can manifest as excessive barking, destructive chewing, or house-soiling accidents.

If you must leave your Corgi alone, even for a few hours, consider options to ease their loneliness. This might include dog walkers, doggy daycare, or having a trusted friend or family member check in on them. Provide interactive toys to keep them occupied.

Creating a safe and enriching environment when you are away helps. But nothing replaces human companionship for a Corgi. Their loyal and affectionate nature means they truly need you. This is a fundamental aspect of owning a Corgi. ### FAQ Section

**Q1: Can Corgis be left alone all day?**
A1: Corgis are social dogs and do not do well when left alone for long periods. They thrive on companionship and can develop separation anxiety or destructive behaviors if isolated too long. Aim for no more than 4-6 hours alone, or arrange for a dog walker or daycare to break up their day.

**Q2: Do Corgis bark a lot?**
A2: Yes, Corgis are known for being vocal. They often bark to alert, play, or express their feelings. While this can be managed with early training and addressing triggers, potential owners should expect a talkative dog. Consistent "quiet" command training is important.

**Q3: How much exercise does a Corgi need?**
A3: Corgis need at least two brisk walks daily, totaling 30-60 minutes, plus active playtime. Their herding background gives them high energy. Neglecting exercise leads to boredom and behavioral problems. Mental stimulation through training and puzzle toys is also crucial.

**Q4: What foods are bad for Corgis?**
A4: Avoid feeding Corgis human food scraps, especially fatty, sugary, or toxic items like chocolate, grapes, onions, and xylitol. Overfeeding any food is bad, as Corgis are prone to obesity, which severely impacts their back. Stick to high-quality dog food in measured portions.

**Q5: How do I stop my Corgi from jumping?**
A5: Prevent your Corgi from jumping on and off furniture by providing dog ramps or stairs. Jumping puts dangerous stress on their long spine, leading to potential IVDD. Train them to use these aids from a young age to protect their back and ensure safety.

**Q6: Are Corgis good for first-time owners?**
A6: Corgis can be good for first-time owners who are committed to their specific needs. They are intelligent and trainable but can be stubborn. First-time owners must be ready for consistent training, daily exercise, regular grooming, and proactive back care.
