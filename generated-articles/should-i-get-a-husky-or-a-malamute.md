### Takeaway

*   **Size Matters:** Malamutes are significantly larger and heavier than Huskies.

*   **Temperament:** Huskies are often more independent and mischievous. Malamutes are generally more dignified and loyal.

### Understanding Their Origins and Purpose

Knowing where a breed comes from explains much about its behavior. Both Huskies and Malamutes have a rich history in Arctic regions. Their original jobs shaped their physical and mental traits. These dogs were not just pets; they were essential for survival.

The Siberian Husky originated in Northeast Asia. The Chukchi people developed them for pulling light loads over vast, frozen distances. They needed speed and endurance for their sleds. These dogs worked in packs, which influenced their social nature. Their primary goal was to move quickly and efficiently through harsh terrain. This heritage means Huskies retain a strong desire to run and explore. They are built for long-distance travel and cold climates.

The Alaskan Malamute comes from Alaska. The Mahlemiut Inuit tribe bred these dogs. Their job was different from the Husky's. Malamutes pulled heavy loads over shorter distances. They needed immense strength and power, not just speed. These dogs also helped with hunting large animals. Their historical role shaped them into robust, powerful animals. Malamutes are designed for strength and resilience in extreme cold. Understanding these origins helps us appreciate their inherent drive and build.

Alaskan Malamutes are larger and more heavily built. Males typically stand 23 to 25 inches tall. They weigh 75 to 85 pounds, sometimes more. Their eyes are always brown and almond-shaped. Their ears are also triangular but set wider apart than a Husky's. Malamutes have a bushy tail carried over the back, often described as a plume. Their sheer size often makes them appear more imposing.
