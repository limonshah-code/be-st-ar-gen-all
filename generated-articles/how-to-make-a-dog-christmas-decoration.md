## Paw Print Ornaments: A Timeless Keepsake

Paw print ornaments are incredibly popular. They capture a moment in time. My dog's paw print ornament from last year is one of my favorites. It reminds me of how small his paws once were.

These decorations make wonderful gifts. Grandparents and friends who love your dog will cherish them. They are also simple to make. You can choose between different materials to create them.

### Clay Paw Print Ornaments

Oven-bake clay or air-dry clay works well for this. You need a block of clay, a rolling pin, and a plastic knife or cookie cutter. Start by softening the clay in your hands. Then roll it out to about a quarter-inch thick.

Press your dog's paw firmly but gently into the clay. Make sure to get a clear impression. Use a plastic knife or cookie cutter to shape the clay around the paw print. Make a small hole at the top for hanging with a straw or skewer. Follow the clay package instructions for baking or drying. Once dry, you can paint or seal it.

**Materials:**
*   Oven-bake or air-dry clay
*   Rolling pin
*   Plastic knife or dog-shaped cookie cutter
*   Straw or skewer
*   Optional: Acrylic paint, sealant, ribbon

**Steps:**
1.  Knead clay until soft and pliable.
2.  Roll out clay to 1/4 inch thickness.
3.  Gently press your dog's paw into the clay to make an impression.
4.  Cut around the paw print using a knife or cookie cutter.
5.  Use a straw to make a hanging hole at the top.
6.  Bake or air-dry the ornament according to clay package directions.
7.  Once dry, paint and seal if desired.
8.  Thread a ribbon through the hole for hanging.

### Salt Dough Paw Print Ornaments

Salt dough is another easy and inexpensive option. It uses ingredients you likely have in your kitchen. To make salt dough, mix 1 cup flour, 1/2 cup salt, and 1/2 cup water. Knead it until smooth.

Roll out the dough and press your dog's paw into it. Cut out the shape and make a hole for hanging. Bake at a low temperature (around 200°F or 90°C) for 2-3 hours. Baking time depends on thickness. Let it cool completely. You can paint it and add a clear sealant to protect it. Salt dough ornaments make beautiful, rustic decorations. They are also a heartfelt way to [give a dog a Christmas present](https://beacleaner.com/how-do-you-give-a-dog-a-christmas-present) in the form of a lasting memory.

**Materials:**
*   1 cup all-purpose flour
*   1/2 cup salt
*   1/2 cup water
*   Rolling pin
*   Cookie cutter or plastic knife
*   Straw
*   Optional: Acrylic paint, clear sealant, ribbon

**Steps:**
1.  Mix flour, salt, and water in a bowl until a dough forms.
2.  Knead the dough on a floured surface until smooth.
3.  Roll out dough to 1/4 inch thickness.
4.  Press your dog's paw into the dough.
5.  Cut out the shape and create a hanging hole.
6.  Bake at 200°F (90°C) for 2-3 hours, or until hardened.
7.  Allow to cool completely.
8.  Paint and seal if desired, then add a ribbon.

## Personalized Dog Silhouette Ornaments

Personalized ornaments add a modern and elegant touch. A silhouette captures your dog's unique profile. I made one for my friend with her pug's profile. She absolutely loved it. It is a stylish way to celebrate your pet.

These ornaments look great on any Christmas tree. They can be made from various materials. The key is getting a good outline of your dog. This makes the ornament truly personal.

### Photo Transfer Ornaments

Photo transfer is a cool technique. You can put your dog's picture onto a wooden slice or ceramic tile. Print your dog's photo onto special transfer paper. Or use a high-quality printout and mod podge.

Apply the image carefully following product instructions. Once dry, seal it for durability. This creates a charming, rustic ornament. It is a fantastic way to display your dog's happy face.

**Materials:**
*   Wooden slices or ceramic tiles
*   Printed photo of your dog (laser print is best for Mod Podge transfer)
*   Mod Podge (photo transfer medium)
*   Foam brush
*   Water
*   Rubbing tool (e.g., credit card)
*   Clear sealant
*   Eye hook or ribbon for hanging

**Steps:**
1.  Print your dog's photo, mirroring the image if it has text.
2.  Apply a thick layer of Mod Podge Photo Transfer Medium to the image side of the print.
3.  Carefully place the photo face down onto the wooden slice or tile.
4.  Smooth out any bubbles with a rubbing tool.
5.  Let it dry completely for at least 24 hours.
6.  Dampen the paper with a wet sponge and gently rub off the paper backing. The image will remain.
7.  Apply a clear sealant to protect the image.
8.  Attach an eye hook to the top or glue a ribbon for hanging.

### Cut-Out Silhouette Ornaments

For cut-out silhouettes, you will need a clear profile picture of your dog. Print this picture to your desired ornament size. Trace the outline onto felt, craft foam, or thin wood. Then carefully cut it out.

You can use fabric glue to layer different colors of felt. For example, a black silhouette on a white felt circle. Add a small loop of ribbon for hanging. These look very striking on a tree. They show off your pet's distinctive shape. These ornaments can blend well if you want to [decorate a Christmas tree like a designer](https://beacleaner.com/how-to-decorate-a-christmas-tree-like-a-designer).

**Materials:**
*   Profile photo of your dog
*   Felt, craft foam, thin wood, or cardstock
*   Scissors or craft knife
*   Pen or marker
*   Optional: Fabric glue, glitter, ribbon

**Steps:**
1.  Print a clear profile photo of your dog to your desired ornament size.
2.  Cut out the dog's silhouette from the printed photo.
3.  Trace this silhouette onto your chosen craft material (felt, foam, wood).
4.  Carefully cut out the traced silhouette.
5.  If using felt, you can layer it onto a contrasting felt circle.
6.  Add embellishments like glitter or paint if desired.
7.  Attach a ribbon loop to the top for hanging.

## Upcycled Dog Toy Decorations

Giving old items new life is a great way to craft. Many dogs have old toys they no longer play with. You can transform these into fun Christmas decorations. This is an eco-friendly approach. It also creates conversation starters.

My dog has a pile of neglected squeaky toys. I turned some into a festive garland last year. It was a hit with everyone. This section will show you how to do something similar.

### Old Toy Garland Ideas

Gather a collection of small, clean dog toys. Avoid toys with sharp edges or parts that could easily break off. You will also need sturdy twine or thick string and a needle with a large eye. Thread the toys onto the twine.

Alternate toys with festive beads or small bells for more visual interest. You can even tie small bows between toys. Hang this garland across a doorway or along a mantel. It is a playful and colorful way to decorate. This kind of playful decoration can also help when you need to [block a dog from a Christmas tree](https://beacleaner.com/how-to-block-a-dog-from-a-christmas-tree) by diverting their attention elsewhere.

**Materials:**
*   Small, clean, old dog toys
*   Sturdy twine or thick string
*   Large-eye needle
*   Optional: Festive beads, small bells, ribbon for bows

**Steps:**
1.  Clean all dog toys thoroughly.
2.  Thread your twine or string through the large-eye needle.
3.  Carefully string each dog toy onto the twine.
4.  Leave space between toys or add beads/bells for decoration.
5.  Tie knots on either side of each toy to secure its position.
6.  Once all toys are on, tie loops at each end of the garland for hanging.

### Toy-Filled Clear Ornaments

Clear, fillable plastic or glass ornaments are versatile. You can fill them with mini versions of your dog's old toys. Cut up a small, soft squeaky toy into tiny pieces. Or use small, colorful fabric scraps from dog bandanas.

You can also add small dog treats (like mini milk bones) to the ornament. Just make sure the treats are dry and won't spoil. Seal the ornament tightly. Hang it on your tree for a surprising and personal touch. This idea works best with plastic ornaments for safety.

**Materials:**
*   Clear, fillable plastic ornaments
*   Small, clean pieces of old dog toys (e.g., fabric scraps, mini squeakers)
*   Optional: Small, dry dog treats, glitter, colorful shredded paper

**Steps:**
1.  Ensure clear ornaments are clean and dry.
2.  Gather small, clean pieces of old dog toys.
3.  Carefully fill the ornament with the toy pieces.
4.  You can also add dry dog treats or glitter.
5.  Securely close the ornament.
6.  Add a hanging ribbon or hook to the top.

## Festive Dog Treat Ornaments

Imagine your dog's delight seeing edible decorations on the tree! These ornaments serve a dual purpose. They are festive additions to your home. They are also tasty rewards for your good boy or girl. Just remember to hang them where your dog can only get them when you say so.

I love making these because my dog gets so excited. It is a fun way to share the holiday spirit. This ensures your dog gets a special holiday treat.

### Baked Dog Biscuit Ornaments

Homemade dog biscuits can be made into ornaments. You need a safe dog biscuit recipe. Look for recipes that use flour, peanut butter, and water. Avoid ingredients like xylitol, which is toxic to dogs. Use bone-shaped or festive cookie cutters.

Make a small hole at the top of each biscuit before baking. This is for the hanging ribbon. Bake them until they are very firm and dry. This helps them last longer. Once cooled, you can decorate them with pet-safe icing. A simple icing of yogurt and a tiny bit of honey works. Remember to remove them from the tree before your dog helps himself!

**Materials:**
*   Pet-safe dog biscuit recipe ingredients (e.g., whole wheat flour, peanut butter, water, egg)
*   Bone-shaped or festive cookie cutters
*   Rolling pin
*   Baking sheet and parchment paper
*   Straw or skewer (for hole)
*   Optional: Pet-safe icing (e.g., plain yogurt mixed with a little honey), ribbon

**Steps:**
1.  Prepare your dog biscuit dough according to a pet-safe recipe.
2.  Roll out the dough to 1/4 inch thickness.
3.  Cut out shapes using cookie cutters.
4.  Use a straw or skewer to create a small hole at the top of each biscuit.
5.  Place biscuits on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
6.  Bake according to your recipe until firm and dry.
7.  Allow to cool completely.
8.  Decorate with pet-safe icing if desired.
9.  Once dry, thread a ribbon through the hole for hanging.

### Dog Treat Garland

A dog treat garland is an easy decoration to assemble. You need sturdy string or twine and a variety of hard dog treats. Biscuits with pre-made holes are perfect. You can also use treats that have a natural indentation.

Carefully thread the treats onto the string. Space them out evenly. You can intersperse them with wooden beads or fabric scraps. This creates a charming and edible garland. Hang it in a safe place. Your dog will love getting a special treat from the garland each day. Just remember to supervise treat consumption.

**Materials:**
*   Sturdy twine or string
*   Assorted hard dog treats (biscuits, chews)
*   Large-eye needle (optional, for easier threading)
*   Optional: Wooden beads, felt shapes

**Steps:**
1.  Cut a length of twine for your desired garland length.
2.  If treats do not have holes, you may need to carefully pierce them with a skewer.
3.  Thread each dog treat onto the twine.
4.  Vary the treats or add beads for visual interest.
5.  Tie knots to secure treats in place.
6.  Form loops at each end for hanging.

## Crafting a Dog-Themed Christmas Wreath

A wreath on your door shows festive spirit. A dog-themed wreath goes a step further. It declares your love for your furry family member. These wreaths can be as simple or as intricate as you like. They make a welcoming statement for guests.

I enjoy making wreaths because they offer a large canvas for creativity. You can combine different elements. This results in a truly unique piece. Let's make a beautiful dog-themed wreath.

### Mini Dog Bone Wreath

This wreath uses mini dog biscuits or bone-shaped cutouts. You need a small foam wreath form. Purchase or bake tiny bone-shaped dog biscuits. You can also cut bone shapes from craft foam or sturdy cardstock.

Attach these bones to the wreath form using hot glue (for non-edible versions). Or use pet-safe craft glue. Overlap them slightly for a full look. Finish with a festive ribbon bow. This wreath is adorable. It is a clear celebration of your dog.

**Materials:**
*   Small foam wreath form
*   Mini bone-shaped dog biscuits (non-edible for durability) or bone-shaped craft foam/cardstock cutouts
*   Hot glue gun and glue sticks (for non-edible crafts) or pet-safe craft glue
*   Festive ribbon

**Steps:**
1.  Gather your bone-shaped items.
2.  Apply glue to the back of each bone.
3.  Attach bones to the wreath form, overlapping them slightly.
4.  Continue until the entire wreath form is covered.
5.  Tie a festive ribbon into a bow and attach it to the top of the wreath.
6.  Add a loop of ribbon to the back for hanging.

### Paw Print Ribbon Wreath

This wreath features ribbons with paw print patterns. You will need a wire wreath frame. Buy various paw print ribbons and plain festive ribbons. Cut the ribbons into 6-8 inch strips.

Tie each strip onto the wire frame using a simple knot. Alternate patterns and colors. Push the ribbons close together to create a full, fluffy wreath. You can also add a central embellishment. A small wooden sign saying "Woof!" or "Merry Woofmas!" works well. This wreath is colorful and lively. For more inspiration on general wreath making, you can explore [how to make a beautiful Christmas grapevine wreath](https://beacleaner.com/how-to-make-a-beautiful-christmas-grapevine-wreath).

**Materials:**
*   Wire wreath frame
*   Various paw print and festive ribbons (wired ribbon works best)
*   Scissors
*   Optional: Small wooden sign, craft glue

**Steps:**
1.  Cut ribbons into strips, about 6-8 inches long.
2.  Take one ribbon strip and fold it in half.
3.  Loop the folded end under a wire section of the wreath frame.
4.  Pull the tails of the ribbon through the loop and tighten. This creates a knot.
5.  Continue tying ribbons all around the frame, alternating colors and patterns.
6.  Push ribbons together to create a full look.
7.  Add a decorative sign or embellishment to the center or top if desired.

## FAQs About Making Dog Christmas Decorations

### What are the safest materials for dog Christmas decorations?

The safest materials are non-toxic and natural. Consider salt dough, oven-bake clay, or felt. Ensure paints and glues are child-safe or pet-safe. Avoid small, loose parts that a dog could chew off and choke on. Always supervise your pet around any homemade decorations.

### How do I get my dog's paw print for an ornament?

To get a paw print, ensure your dog is calm. Use air-dry clay or salt dough rolled out smoothly. Gently press one paw firmly into the material. Hold it for a few seconds. Lift the paw straight up. Reward your dog with a treat after. You may need a helper for active dogs.

### How long do homemade dog treat ornaments last?

Homemade dog treat ornaments can last several weeks if baked very dry. Store them in an airtight container if not hanging. Always check for mold or staleness. It is best to bake these closer to Christmas. Remove them from the tree after the holidays.

### Can I use store-bought dog treats for garlands?

Yes, you can use store-bought, hard dog treats for garlands. Choose biscuits with holes or treats that are easy to pierce. Avoid soft or sticky treats, as they may attract pests or spoil quickly. Hard biscuits work best. Ensure they are appropriate for your dog's size and diet.

### How do I make my dog's silhouette accurate?

For an accurate silhouette, take a clear side-profile photo of your dog. Stand at their eye level for the best angle. Print the photo. Then carefully cut out the outline. You can trace this cut-out onto your chosen craft material for precision.

### What if my dog tries to eat the decorations?

If your dog tries to eat non-edible decorations, hang them higher. Place them out of your dog's reach. Use plastic ornaments instead of glass. Secure items firmly to the tree or wreath. Consider using a dog gate to limit access to decorated areas if needed. This helps keep your dog safe.
