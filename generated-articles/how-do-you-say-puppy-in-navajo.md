### The Direct Answer: Puppy in Navajo

In the beautiful Navajo language, you typically refer to a "puppy" as **łééchąąʼí yázhí**. This phrase literally translates to "small dog" or "little dog." The word "łééchąąʼí" means "dog," and "yázhí" signifies "small" or "young." This simple combination captures the essence of a young canine friend.

## Mastering Navajo Pronunciation: Saying Łééchąąʼí Yázhí

Pronouncing Navajo words correctly can be a challenge for new learners. Navajo is a tonal language. This means the pitch of your voice changes the word's meaning. It also has sounds not found in English. When you learn how do you say puppy in Navajo, focusing on pronunciation is key. The word "łééchąąʼí yázhí" requires attention to specific sounds and tones. I recommend listening to native speakers whenever possible. Their guidance is invaluable.

Let's break down "łééchąąʼí yázhí" phonetically. The 'ł' sound is important. It is a voiceless lateral fricative. Think of it like an 'L' sound where air flows over the sides of your tongue. The 'ąą' is a nasalized 'a' sound. Imagine saying 'ah' while air comes out of your nose. The glottal stop, represented by 'ʼ', is like the break in "uh-oh." The tones on vowels also matter. High tones are marked with an accent, like 'á'. Low tones have no mark. Mastering these sounds will help you speak more authentically.

### Step-by-Step Pronunciation Guide

1.  **łéé (łéé):** Start with the 'ł' sound. It is a soft 'L' sound, but with air hissing past the sides of your tongue. The 'éé' is a high-toned, long 'e' sound, like "eh" in "bed" but held longer and with a high pitch.
2.  **chąą (chąą):** The 'ch' is like the 'ch' in "church." The 'ąą' is a long, nasalized 'a' sound. Imagine saying 'ah' through your nose. It also has a low tone.
3.  **ʼí (ʼí):** This is a glottal stop followed by a high-toned 'ee' sound, like in "feet." The glottal stop is a brief pause, like the sound between the two parts of "uh-oh."
4.  **yá (yá):** The 'y' is like in "yes." The 'á' is a high-toned 'a' sound, like "ah" in "father."
5.  **zhí (zhí):** The 'zh' is like the 's' in "measure" or 'g' in "beige." The 'í' is a high-toned 'ee' sound, as in "feet."
