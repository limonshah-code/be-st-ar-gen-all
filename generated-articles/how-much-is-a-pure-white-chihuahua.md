## How Much Is A Pure White Chihuahua?

The allure of a pure white Chihuahua puppy captures many hearts. These tiny dogs, with their snow-colored coats, appear delicate and unique. Many people wonder about the special care and cost involved in owning such a distinctive pet. I understand why you might feel drawn to a pure white Chihuahua. This guide explains the true investment required for bringing one home. We will cover the initial purchase price, ongoing expenses, and essential care needs for these beautiful little companions.

### Takeaway

Bringing home a pure white Chihuahua involves understanding several key costs:
*   Initial purchase prices range from $1,500 to over $6,000, depending on lineage, breeder, and location.
*   Reputable breeders often charge more due to health testing and proper care.
*   Ongoing costs include food, veterinary visits, grooming, and supplies.

### Clear, Concise Answer

A pure white Chihuahua typically costs between $1,500 and $6,000, or even more for show-quality puppies from champion bloodlines. This price reflects their rarity, specific breeding requirements, breeder reputation, and health clearances. Ongoing expenses for food, vet care, and supplies add to the total cost of ownership.

### The Price Tag: Initial Cost of a Pure White Chihuahua Puppy

Puppies from reputable breeders with strong bloodlines cost more. These breeders invest in health screenings for parent dogs. They also provide proper nutrition and early socialization for their puppies. This responsible approach ensures healthier, well-adjusted dogs.

Location also plays a role in pricing. Puppies sold in major metropolitan areas may have higher prices. Demand in certain regions can drive costs up. I have seen prices differ widely even within the same state.

The rarity of a truly pure white coat also impacts the price. Not all Chihuahuas carry the gene for a solid white coat. Breeders specifically trying for this color invest considerable time and money. This extra effort reflects in the puppy's final cost.

### Factors That Influence the Price of a White Chihuahua

A reputable breeder charges more for their puppies. They invest in health testing, proper nutrition, and vaccinations. Good breeders also offer health guarantees and support after you take the puppy home. They care about their dogs' well-being.

#### Pedigree and Bloodlines

Puppies from champion bloodlines or those with a strong pedigree cost more. These dogs often have desirable traits and good health histories. Breeders document their lineage carefully. This documentation assures you of the puppy's background.

A puppy intended for showing or breeding commands a higher price. Pet-quality puppies, while still purebred, usually cost less. They still make wonderful companions. Your purpose for the dog affects the price.

#### Health Clearances and Genetic Testing

Responsible breeders perform extensive health clearances on parent dogs. This includes screening for common Chihuahua issues. For white Chihuahuas, specific tests for deafness are important. These tests ensure healthier offspring.

The cost of these tests is significant for breeders. They pass some of this cost on to the buyer. This ensures you receive a puppy less prone to hereditary diseases. It is a worthwhile investment in your pet's future health.

A solid, unblemished pure white coat is genuinely rare in Chihuahuas. Achieving this specific color requires careful breeding. Breeders focus on dogs carrying the gene for white. This can be a challenging genetic pursuit.

### Pure White Chihuahuas: Special Considerations and Health

Pure white Chihuahuas, while beautiful, come with specific considerations. Their coat color is often linked to certain genetic factors. Understanding these helps you provide the best care. I make sure to be well-informed about any special needs.

#### Genetics of the White Coat

The pure white coat in Chihuahuas can result from different genetic pathways. One common cause is the presence of the "merle" gene, which, when homozygous (two copies), can lead to an all-white coat. However, a truly pure white Chihuahua often results from an extreme piebald gene. This gene completely removes pigment from the fur, leaving it solid white.

It is important for breeders to understand these genetics. Breeding two merle dogs can produce pure white puppies, but it also increases the risk of serious health problems. Ethical breeders avoid this combination. They aim for healthy white puppies through safer genetic pairings.

#### Risk of Deafness

One of the most significant health concerns for pure white dogs is deafness. This is often linked to the genes responsible for an all-white coat. The genes affecting pigment can also impact the development of cells in the inner ear. This condition is known as congenital sensorineural deafness.

Not all white Chihuahuas are deaf. However, the risk is higher than in Chihuahuas with other coat colors. Reputable breeders perform BAER (Brainstem Auditory Evoked Response) testing on white puppies. This test checks for hearing ability. It is a vital screening for these dogs.

#### Skin Sensitivity

Pure white Chihuahuas may have more sensitive skin. Their lack of pigment offers less natural protection from the sun. This makes them more prone to sunburn. Prolonged sun exposure can also increase the risk of skin cancer.

Limit their time in direct sunlight, especially during peak hours. You might need dog-specific sunscreen for outdoor activities. Regular checks for any skin irritation are also important. I always watch my Chihuahua's skin carefully.

#### Vision Issues

Some pure white dogs can also have vision problems. These issues include microphthalmia (small eyes) or iris hypoplasia. These conditions are also linked to pigment genes. Again, proper genetic screening by breeders helps reduce these risks.

Ensure the breeder has screened for eye conditions. A thorough veterinary check-up should include an eye examination. Early detection helps manage any potential issues effectively.

#### Temperature Regulation

Chihuahuas generally struggle with temperature regulation due to their small size. White Chihuahuas are no exception. They can get cold easily in winter and overheat quickly in summer. Provide appropriate clothing for cold weather.

Ensure access to shade and fresh water during hot days. Never leave a Chihuahua in a hot car. Their tiny bodies are very vulnerable to extreme temperatures. My Chihuahua always has a cozy blanket available.

#### Dental Health

Chihuahuas are prone to dental problems, regardless of coat color. Their small mouths can lead to overcrowding of teeth. This makes them more susceptible to plaque and tartar buildup. Regular dental care is essential.

Brush your Chihuahua's teeth daily. Provide dental chews. Schedule professional dental cleanings with your vet as needed. Good dental hygiene helps prevent pain and serious health issues.

### FAQ Section

#### Are pure white Chihuahuas rare?
Yes, truly pure white Chihuahuas are relatively rare. The genetic combination required for a solid, unblemished white coat is not common. This rarity contributes to their higher price and demand. Many "white" Chihuahuas actually have cream or light tan markings. Finding a completely pure white one requires specific breeding efforts.

#### What health problems do pure white Chihuahuas commonly have?
Pure white Chihuahuas have an increased risk of deafness, especially if the white coat results from certain genetic factors. They can also have sensitive skin, making them prone to sunburn. Like all Chihuahuas, they are susceptible to dental problems, patellar luxation, and heart conditions. Reputable breeders screen for these issues.

#### How much does it cost to feed a Chihuahua per month?
Feeding a Chihuahua typically costs $20-$40 per month. This depends on the brand of food and your Chihuahua's specific dietary needs. High-quality small-breed kibble is recommended. Some owners choose fresh food diets, which can be more expensive. Quality food contributes to their overall health.

#### Is a pure white Chihuahua recognized by kennel clubs?
Yes, a pure white Chihuahua is recognized by major kennel clubs, like the AKC (American Kennel Club), as a standard Chihuahua color. They are not a separate breed. The AKC's breed standard allows for all solid colors, including white. However, a truly solid white coat with no markings is less common.

#### Do pure white Chihuahuas get sunburned easily?
Yes, pure white Chihuahuas have lighter pigmentation in their skin and fur, offering less natural protection from the sun. This makes them more prone to sunburn than Chihuahuas with darker coats. Limit their time in direct sunlight and consider dog-specific sunscreen for outdoor activities to protect their sensitive skin.

#### How long do pure white Chihuahuas live?
Pure white Chihuahuas have a lifespan similar to other Chihuahuas, typically ranging from 12 to 20 years. Their longevity depends on genetics, diet, exercise, and overall healthcare. Providing proper nutrition, regular vet check-ups, and a loving environment helps them live a long and healthy life.
