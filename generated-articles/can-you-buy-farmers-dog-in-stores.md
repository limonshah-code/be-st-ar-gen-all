### Transitioning Your Dog to Fresh Food

Switching your dog to a new diet, especially from kibble to fresh food, should be done gradually. A sudden change can upset your dog's digestive system, leading to issues like diarrhea or vomiting. I always recommend a slow transition over several days or even weeks. This gentle approach helps your dog's stomach adjust to the new ingredients and textures.

Start by mixing a small amount of The Farmer's Dog food with your dog's current food. A good ratio to begin with is about 25% new food and 75% old food. Maintain this ratio for a few days, observing your dog for any signs of digestive upset. If they are tolerating it well, you can gradually increase the proportion of the new food.

Over the next week or two, increase the fresh food amount while decreasing the old food. For example, move to a 50/50 mix for a few days, then 75% new food and 25% old food. Finally, transition to 100% fresh food. This slow progression gives your dog's gut microbes time to adapt. Every dog is different, so adjust the speed of transition based on your pet's reaction. Some dogs adapt quickly, while others need more time.

During the transition, keep an eye on your dog's stool and general behavior. Healthy stools indicate a smooth transition. If you notice any issues, slow down the process or consult your veterinarian. Providing fresh water and maintaining a consistent feeding schedule also aids in a successful switch. A gradual transition ensures your dog enjoys their new diet without any discomfort.
