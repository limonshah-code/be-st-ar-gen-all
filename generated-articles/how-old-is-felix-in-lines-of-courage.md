## The Historical Context of Young Soldiers Like Felix

Understanding the historical backdrop is crucial when we discuss **how old is Felix in Lines of Courage**. During World War I, armies on all sides accepted underage recruits. Official enlistment ages often stated 18 or 19, but enforcement was loose. Boys as young as 14 or 15 regularly joined up, either by lying about their age or by looking older than they were. They felt immense pressure from society and from their peers to serve their country. Propaganda often glorified military service, attracting many eager, young volunteers.

These young soldiers, often called "boy soldiers," faced the same horrific conditions as older men. They endured trench warfare, dangerous battles, and the psychological toll of combat. Many were not physically or emotionally ready for such experiences. Felix's story reflects this grim reality perfectly. He represents the countless young individuals whose childhoods ended abruptly on the battlefield. The novel underscores how war did not discriminate by age, pulling even the youngest into its violent embrace. Their bravery was immense, but their sacrifice was even greater.
