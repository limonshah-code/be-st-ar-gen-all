## Feeding Your Leopard Gecko: How Many Crickets Do They Need?

Bringing a leopard gecko into your home is an exciting experience. These fascinating reptiles have unique needs, especially when it comes to their diet. Many new owners wonder, "how many crickets should a leopard gecko eat?" Getting the feeding schedule and quantity right is crucial for their health and happiness.

Proper nutrition directly impacts your gecko's growth, energy levels, and overall well-being. Feeding too much or too little can lead to serious health problems. This article explores the precise quantities of crickets your leopard gecko needs at different life stages. We will cover cricket size, gut loading, supplementation, and common feeding mistakes to help you become a confident gecko parent. Understanding these details ensures your pet thrives.

### Takeaway

*   **Age Matters**: Young geckos eat more frequently and require more crickets than adults.
*   **Size is Key**: Crickets should be no larger than the space between your gecko's eyes.
*   **Gut Load Always**: Feed crickets nutritious food before offering them to your gecko.
*   **Supplement Regularly**: Dust crickets with calcium and multivitamins to prevent deficiencies.
*   **Monitor Health**: Adjust feeding based on your gecko's activity, tail fat, and shedding cycle.
*   **Vary Diet**: Offer a range of feeder insects, not just crickets, for complete nutrition.

### How Many Crickets Should a Leopard Gecko Eat?

A leopard gecko's cricket intake varies by age. Hatchlings and juveniles may eat 5-10 small crickets daily. Sub-adults can consume 7-10 medium crickets every other day. Adult leopard geckos generally need 5-7 medium crickets two to three times per week. Always gut load crickets and dust them with appropriate supplements before feeding.

## Understanding Your Leopard Gecko's Dietary Needs

Leopard geckos are insectivores. This means their natural diet consists entirely of insects. In the wild, they hunt a variety of small arthropods. Providing the right feeder insects in captivity is vital for their health.

Crickets are a staple for many gecko owners. They are widely available and relatively easy to gut load. However, a gecko's diet should not be limited to crickets alone. Offering a varied diet helps ensure your gecko gets all essential nutrients. This can prevent deficiencies that might arise from feeding only one type of insect. Other suitable feeders include mealworms, Dubia roaches, and superworms. You can learn more about alternative feeders, such as [how many mealworms should a leopard gecko eat](https://beacleaner.com/how-many-mealworms-should-a-leopard-gecko-eat), to diversify your pet's diet.

The nutritional value of a cricket depends on what it eats. This is why gut loading is so important. A cricket that has eaten a nutritious diet passes those nutrients on to your gecko. Without proper gut loading, crickets offer little nutritional benefit. This can lead to serious health issues for your gecko over time. My own geckos show much more energy when their crickets are well-fed.

Beyond insects, leopard geckos also need proper hydration. They often get some moisture from their food. However, a shallow water dish with fresh water available at all times is essential. Even though they are desert animals, access to water is non-negotiable for their well-being. ## Cricket Quantity for Leopard Gecko Juveniles

Young leopard geckos grow very quickly. This rapid growth requires a high amount of protein and calcium. Therefore, juveniles need to eat more frequently than adults. Their feeding schedule is crucial for proper development.

### Hatchlings and Young Geckos

Hatchlings are tiny, usually only a few inches long. They need very small crickets, often called "pinhead" or 1/4 inch crickets. You should offer them food every day. I usually give my hatchlings 5-7 crickets each day. They eat until they are full or lose interest.

It is important to remove any uneaten crickets after about 15-20 minutes. Live crickets left in the enclosure can stress a small gecko. They might even nibble on the gecko, especially during shedding. This is why it's a good idea to research topics like [can you leave crickets in a leopard gecko cage](https://beacleaner.com/can-you-leave-crickets-in-a-leopard-gecko-cage) to understand the risks. Continue daily feeding until your gecko reaches about three to four months of age.

### Sub-Adult Geckos

As your gecko grows, it moves into the sub-adult stage. This typically happens between 4 and 10 months old. At this stage, they are still growing but not as rapidly as hatchlings. Their feeding frequency can be reduced slightly.

I feed my sub-adult geckos every other day. They can handle slightly larger crickets, usually 1/2 to 3/4 inch in size. A typical feeding consists of 7-10 crickets. Always ensure the crickets are appropriate for your gecko's size. A good rule of thumb is that the cricket should be no wider than the space between your gecko's eyes. Too large crickets can cause impaction.

Monitor your sub-adult's tail. A healthy young gecko will have a plump tail. This indicates good fat reserves. If the tail looks thin, you might need to offer more food or increase feeding frequency. Consistent growth and a strong appetite are good signs. However, if you notice your gecko is not eating, it is good to understand [what to do if leopard gecko is not eating](https://beacleaner.com/what-to-do-if-leopard-gecko-is-not-eating) to address potential issues.

## Adult Leopard Gecko Cricket Feeding Schedule

Once your leopard gecko reaches about 10-12 months of age, it is generally considered an adult. At this stage, their growth slows significantly. Their energy requirements change, meaning they need to eat less frequently than juveniles. Adjusting their feeding schedule is important to prevent obesity.

Adult geckos thrive on a less frequent feeding schedule. I typically feed my adult leopard geckos two to three times per week. Each feeding session involves offering 5-7 medium to large-sized crickets. Remember the rule: the cricket should not be wider than the space between your gecko's eyes. This ensures easy digestion and prevents choking or impaction.

The specific number of crickets can vary based on your individual gecko's activity level and metabolism. Some geckos are more active and may burn more calories. Others might be more sedentary and require fewer crickets. Observe your gecko's body condition closely. A healthy adult leopard gecko should have a plump tail. This tail stores fat reserves, indicating good health and sufficient nutrition. A very thin tail suggests underfeeding, while an overly large or "sausage" tail might mean overfeeding.

Maintaining a proper feeding schedule prevents many health problems. Overfeeding can lead to obesity, which strains organs and shortens their lifespan. Underfeeding results in lethargy, poor growth, and weakened immunity. My adult geckos maintain a healthy weight on this schedule. I also make sure to vary their diet to include other insects, which contributes to a balanced intake of nutrients. This helps provide a full spectrum of vitamins and minerals.

It is important to note that adult geckos can go for a period without eating, but consistent feeding is best for their health. ## The Importance of Cricket Size and Gut Loading

Feeding your leopard gecko the correct size cricket is just as important as feeding the right quantity. Incorrect cricket size can lead to significant health problems. Equally vital is gut loading your crickets. This practice enhances their nutritional value before they become a meal for your gecko.

The general rule for cricket size is simple: never offer a cricket larger than the space between your gecko's eyes. For hatchlings, this means tiny "pinhead" crickets. Juveniles can handle 1/4 to 1/2 inch crickets. Adults can usually eat 1/2 to 3/4 inch crickets. Feeding crickets that are too large poses several risks. Your gecko might struggle to swallow them. This can cause choking. Large crickets can also lead to impaction, a serious and potentially fatal digestive blockage. Always err on the side of smaller crickets if you are unsure. I always check cricket size before feeding my geckos.

Gut loading refers to feeding the feeder insects a highly nutritious diet before giving them to your reptile. Crickets, by themselves, are not a complete meal. Their nutritional content depends entirely on what they have eaten. If crickets are fed only stale bread or low-quality food, they offer minimal nutrients to your gecko. A well gut-loaded cricket acts as a vitamin and mineral delivery system. This practice boosts your gecko's intake of essential vitamins and minerals.

To gut load crickets, feed them a nutritious diet for at least 24-48 hours before offering them to your gecko. Commercial gut-loading diets are available, or you can use a mix of fresh vegetables. Good options include carrots, sweet potatoes, dark leafy greens like collard greens or mustard greens, and fruit pieces. Always ensure the food is clean and free of pesticides. Providing water gel crystals keeps them hydrated. Proper gut loading significantly improves your gecko's overall health and longevity. Without gut-loaded insects, your gecko will miss out on critical nutrients.

## Supplementation: Calcium and Multivitamins

While gut loading improves the nutritional content of crickets, it is often not enough. Leopard geckos require additional calcium and vitamin D3, as well as a range of other essential vitamins and minerals. Supplementation is a critical part of a healthy leopard gecko diet.

Calcium is especially important for bone development and preventing metabolic bone disease (MBD). MBD is a severe condition that causes weak bones, deformities, and can be fatal. Leopard geckos also need vitamin D3 to absorb calcium properly. In the wild, they get D3 from UVB exposure. In captivity, indoor geckos typically do not get enough natural UVB. Therefore, D3 must be provided through supplements or specific UVB lighting. I ensure my geckos get their calcium and vitamin D3.

You will need two main types of supplements:
*   **Calcium without D3**: This can be dusted on every feeding.
*   **Calcium with D3**: This should be dusted less frequently, typically every other feeding or once a week for adults. The frequency depends on whether your gecko has access to UVB lighting. If they do, they need less D3 in their diet. If they don't, it is more critical.
*   **Multivitamin powder**: This contains a broad spectrum of vitamins and trace minerals. It should be used less frequently, perhaps once or twice a week for adults.

To apply supplements, place the crickets in a plastic bag or a small container with a pinch of the powdered supplement. Gently shake the bag or container until the crickets are lightly coated. Then, immediately offer them to your gecko. Do not over-dust; a light coating is sufficient. Over-supplementation can also be harmful. Finding the right balance ensures your gecko gets everything it needs without excess. This approach prevents deficiencies and promotes strong bones and a robust immune system. My geckos have thrived on this supplementation regimen.

## Monitoring Your Gecko's Health and Weight

Closely observing your leopard gecko is essential for ensuring their diet is effective. Their appearance and behavior provide clear indicators of their health. Proper feeding maintains a healthy weight and overall well-being. Knowing what to look for helps you adjust feeding as needed.

One of the most important indicators of a healthy leopard gecko is the tail. A healthy gecko's tail should be plump and fleshy. It should be wide at the base and taper gradually. This is where they store fat reserves. A very thin tail, especially at the base, suggests your gecko is not getting enough food. Conversely, an overly thick, "sausage-like" tail might indicate overfeeding and obesity. I regularly check my geckos' tails.

Beyond the tail, observe your gecko's general activity level. A well-fed gecko is active and alert, especially during its crepuscular hours. Lethargy or a lack of interest in food can be signs of underfeeding or illness. Also, pay attention to their shedding cycle. Healthy geckos shed regularly and without difficulty. Issues with shedding can sometimes point to nutritional deficiencies or dehydration. Regular weight checks can also be helpful, especially for young geckos. You can use a small digital scale to track their progress. Consistent weight gain in juveniles and stable weight in adults are good signs. Keep a log of their weight and feeding schedule. This helps you notice trends and make informed adjustments. If you notice any significant changes or your gecko suddenly stops eating, it is crucial to investigate. Sometimes, changes in appetite can indicate health problems, and understanding [what to do if leopard gecko is not eating](https://beacleaner.com/what-to-do-if-leopard-gecko-is-not-eating) can provide guidance. By being attentive, you can ensure your leopard gecko lives a long and healthy life.

## Common Cricket Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Feeding your leopard gecko seems straightforward, but several common mistakes can impact their health. Avoiding these pitfalls is crucial for their long-term well-being. Proper feeding practices extend beyond just quantity and frequency.

One major mistake is *overfeeding*. While it might seem kind to give your gecko extra treats, too much food leads to obesity. Obese geckos often develop fatty liver disease, heart problems, and other health issues. They also become less active. Always stick to recommended feeding guidelines for their age. I try my best to avoid overfeeding my geckos.

Another common error is *underfeeding*. This can happen if you feed too few crickets or not often enough. Underfed geckos become lethargic, have thin tails, and are more susceptible to illness. Their growth can also be stunted. Make sure to provide enough appropriately sized crickets according to their life stage. Monitoring their health, as discussed earlier, helps prevent this.

*Leaving live crickets in the cage* for extended periods is also a significant mistake. Crickets left unattended can stress your gecko. They might nibble on your gecko's toes or tail, especially when your gecko is sleeping or shedding. This can cause painful injuries. Always remove any uneaten crickets after a feeding session, usually within 15-20 minutes. If you are unsure about the risks, it is good to learn more about [can you leave crickets in a leopard gecko cage](https://beacleaner.com/can-you-leave-crickets-in-a-leopard-gecko-cage) to understand why removal is vital.

*Not gut loading or supplementing* crickets is another critical oversight. As we discussed, crickets alone do not provide a complete nutritional profile. Without gut loading, they are essentially "empty calories." Lack of proper supplementation with calcium and D3 often leads to metabolic bone disease (MBD), a debilitating and irreversible condition. Always ensure your feeder insects are gut-loaded and dusted. My geckos rely on these crucial steps for their health.

Finally, *feeding only one type of insect* can lead to nutritional imbalances over time. While crickets are a staple, varying your gecko's diet with other feeders like mealworms or Dubia roaches provides a broader spectrum of nutrients. A diverse diet is key to a truly healthy and thriving leopard gecko.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: Can I feed only crickets to my leopard gecko?

While crickets are a staple, feeding only crickets is not ideal. It can lead to nutritional deficiencies over time. A varied diet of different feeder insects, such as mealworms and Dubia roaches, provides a wider range of essential nutrients. This ensures your leopard gecko receives complete and balanced nutrition for optimal health.

### Q2: What if my gecko doesn't want to eat crickets?

If your leopard gecko refuses crickets, first check environmental factors like temperature and humidity. Stress, shedding, or illness can cause appetite loss. Try offering different feeder insects to see if it's a preference issue. If the refusal persists or your gecko shows other symptoms, consult a reptile veterinarian. Also, understanding [what to do if leopard gecko is not eating](https://beacleaner.com/what-to-do-if-leopard-gecko-is-not-eating) can help you address this common concern.

### Q3: How often should I dust crickets with calcium?

Dust crickets with calcium without D3 at almost every feeding for juveniles and adults. Use calcium with D3 once or twice a week, depending on UVB exposure. Multivitamin powder should be offered once or twice a week. Rotate these supplements to ensure a balanced intake and prevent over-supplementation, which can also be harmful.

### Q4: What size crickets should I buy for my leopard gecko?

The rule of thumb is that the cricket should be no larger than the space between your leopard gecko's eyes. For hatchlings, use pinhead crickets. Juveniles need 1/4 to 1/2 inch crickets. Adults can handle 1/2 to 3/4 inch crickets. Feeding appropriately sized crickets prevents choking and impaction.

### Q5: Can I feed dead crickets to my leopard gecko?

Leopard geckos are natural predators that respond to movement. They primarily eat live, moving prey. They typically will not recognize or eat dead crickets. If you have dead crickets, it is best to discard them. Always offer live, gut-loaded crickets to ensure your gecko's interest and nutritional intake.

### Q6: How do I store crickets?

Store crickets in a well-ventilated container with egg cartons or toilet paper rolls for hiding. Provide them with a constant source of hydration like water gel crystals. Feed them a nutritious diet of fresh vegetables and a commercial gut-loading formula. This keeps them healthy and gut-loaded for your gecko.
