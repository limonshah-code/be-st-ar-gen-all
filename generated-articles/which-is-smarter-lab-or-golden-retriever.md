## Defining Dog Intelligence: More Than Just Tricks

What does it mean for a dog to be "smart"? We often think of tricks, but dog intelligence goes deeper. Stanley Coren, a renowned dog psychologist, proposes three main types. These categories help us understand dog smarts better. It is not just about learning commands.

### Instinctive Intelligence
Instinctive intelligence refers to a dog's inherent abilities. These are behaviors bred into them for generations. For example, herding dogs naturally gather sheep. Retrievers, like Labs and Goldens, instinctively fetch objects. This is a core part of who they are. Their breed history shapes these natural talents.

### Adaptive Intelligence
Adaptive intelligence shows how well a dog learns from its environment. This includes problem-solving skills. A dog might figure out how to open a gate. It might learn how to get a treat from a puzzle toy. This intelligence helps dogs adjust to new situations. It means they can learn from past experiences.

### Working and Obedience Intelligence
Working and obedience intelligence is what we usually measure. This is how quickly a dog learns commands. It also includes how well they follow those commands. Breeds with high working intelligence are easy to train. They enjoy performing tasks for humans. Both Labs and Goldens rank high in this area. We explore how these aspects make [Labrador Retriever or Golden Retriever smarter](https://beacleaner.com/is-a-lab-or-golden-retriever-smarter) in further sections. Understanding these types helps us compare intelligence fairly.

### How can I tell if my Lab or Golden Retriever is smart?
