## Feeding Your Crested Gecko: A Complete Guide

Bringing a crested gecko into your home is an exciting experience. These fascinating reptiles have specific dietary requirements. Knowing what to feed your crested gecko ensures a long, healthy life for your pet. A proper diet provides all essential nutrients. It promotes strong bones, vibrant colors, and good energy levels.

This article will explain everything about feeding your crested gecko. We cover complete gecko diets, essential live insects, and safe fruit treats. You will learn about important supplements, hydration needs, and setting a good feeding schedule. We also discuss common mistakes to avoid. By the end, you will understand how to provide the best diet for your crested gecko.

### Takeaway:
*   Crested geckos need a specialized commercial diet.
*   Live, gut-loaded insects are vital protein sources.
*   Offer safe fruits as occasional treats.
*   Provide fresh water daily and maintain proper humidity.
*   Supplements like calcium and multivitamins are crucial.
*   Follow a consistent feeding schedule based on age.
*   Avoid feeding harmful foods or over-supplementation.

Crested geckos thrive on a diet primarily consisting of a high-quality commercial Crested Gecko Diet (CGD) powder, supplemented with gut-loaded and dusted live insects. Occasional small servings of appropriate mashed fruits can be offered as treats. This combination meets their unique nutritional needs for proper growth and health.

## The Essential Crested Gecko Diet (CGD)

The foundation of any crested gecko's diet is a high-quality commercial Crested Gecko Diet (CGD). This powdered food contains all the necessary nutrients your gecko needs. It includes proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals. CGD is designed to be a complete meal for your pet.

You should always choose reputable brands. Brands like Repashy, Pangea, and Zoo Med offer excellent CGD products. These products are carefully formulated for crested geckos. Different flavors are available to entice picky eaters.

Preparing CGD is simple. You mix the powder with water to create a consistency similar to ketchup or a thick smoothie. Always follow the specific instructions on the product packaging. Serve the mixed diet in a shallow dish. Remove any uneaten CGD after 24-36 hours to prevent spoilage.

CGD should form the bulk of your gecko's diet. It provides essential balanced nutrition. Relying solely on insects or fruits will lead to deficiencies. While the dietary needs of other gecko species, such as [how to feed a leopard gecko](https://beacleaner.com/how-much-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko), focus heavily on live insects, crested geckos thrive on a specially formulated powder. This complete diet makes sure your crested gecko gets everything it needs daily.

## Live Insects: A Vital Nutritional Boost

Live insects are a critical part of a crested gecko's diet. They provide essential protein and stimulate natural hunting behaviors. Insects add variety and enrichment to their meals. They are also a great source of different nutrients not always found in CGD.

You should offer a variety of insect types. Crickets and dubia roaches are excellent choices. Mealworms and waxworms can be offered sparingly as treats. They are higher in fat and should not be a staple. Always provide insects that are smaller than the space between your gecko's eyes. This prevents choking.

Gut loading insects is extremely important. This means feeding the insects a nutritious diet before offering them to your gecko. Feed insects fresh fruits, vegetables, and commercial gut-loading formulas for at least 24-48 hours. This process transfers nutrients from the insect to your gecko. A well-fed insect is a healthy meal for your pet.

Dusting insects with calcium and multivitamin supplements is also necessary. This ensures your gecko receives adequate calcium and other vital nutrients. We will discuss supplementation in more detail later. Offer live insects 1-2 times per week for adult geckos and more frequently for juveniles.

## Fruits and Occasional Treats

Fruits can be a small, occasional part of your crested gecko's diet. They offer natural sugars and some vitamins. Remember, fruits are treats, not staples. They should never replace CGD or insects. Overfeeding fruit can cause digestive problems.

Only offer safe and appropriate fruits. Good choices include ripe papaya, mango, berries (like raspberries and blueberries), and apricots. Bananas can be given sparingly due to their high phosphorus content, which can interfere with calcium absorption. Mash the fruit into a fine pulp. This makes it easier for your gecko to eat.

Avoid all citrus fruits, such as oranges and lemons, as they are too acidic. Onions, garlic, and avocado are toxic to geckos. Never feed these items. Always research a fruit before offering it. Ensure it is safe for your crested gecko.

Offer fruit treats once every week or two. Use a very small amount, perhaps a pea-sized portion. Watch for any signs of digestive upset. If your gecko experiences loose stools, reduce or stop fruit offerings. Moderation is key to healthy fruit supplementation.

## Water and Hydration for Crested Geckos

Proper hydration is just as important as a balanced diet for your crested gecko. These animals absorb water through their skin and by drinking. Providing consistent access to fresh water is essential for their overall health and well-being. Dehydration can cause serious health problems.

Always provide a shallow, clean water dish in the enclosure. Geckos may drink from this dish, especially at night. Change the water daily to keep it fresh and free of bacteria. A heavy, shallow dish prevents spills and makes it easy for your gecko to access. Ensure the water is free of chlorine or other harmful chemicals.

Crested geckos also absorb water through misting. Misting the enclosure daily or every other day is vital. Use a spray bottle to lightly mist the substrate and decorations. This simulates dew drops that geckos lick from surfaces. Misting also helps maintain appropriate humidity levels in the enclosure.

Humidity plays a crucial role in your gecko's hydration. Crested geckos require humidity between 60-80%. Use a hygrometer to monitor these levels. Proper humidity prevents dehydration and aids in healthy shedding. Regular misting helps achieve and maintain these necessary humidity ranges. Without proper hydration, your gecko cannot digest food well or shed its skin correctly.

## Understanding Crested Gecko Supplements

Supplements are vital to ensure your crested gecko receives all necessary vitamins and minerals. Even with a good CGD, additional supplementation is often required, especially for growing geckos and breeding females. The two main supplements are calcium with D3 and a multivitamin. These supplements prevent common deficiencies.

Calcium with D3 is crucial for bone health and preventing metabolic bone disease (MBD). MBD is a severe condition caused by calcium deficiency. Vitamin D3 helps geckos absorb calcium from their diet. You can dust live insects with this powder. Gently shake insects in a bag with calcium powder before feeding.

A good multivitamin supplement provides other essential vitamins and trace minerals. These include Vitamin A, E, and B vitamins. Multivitamins support immune function, good vision, and overall vitality. These supplements are often dusted onto insects or mixed into CGD, but less frequently than calcium. Always follow the product's specific instructions for dosage and frequency.

It is important to avoid over-supplementation. Too much of certain vitamins, especially D3, can be harmful. Always use supplements specifically formulated for reptiles. Consult with a reptile veterinarian if you are unsure about dosing. Proper supplementation supports a strong skeletal system and healthy bodily functions. This helps your crested gecko thrive for many years.

## Establishing a Crested Gecko Feeding Schedule

A consistent feeding schedule helps maintain your crested gecko's health and routine. The frequency and amount of food depend on your gecko's age and activity level. Understanding these differences is key to proper care. Feeding at the right times supports natural behaviors.

Juvenile crested geckos, typically under six months old, need to eat more often. They are growing rapidly and require more energy and nutrients. Offer CGD every day or every other day. Provide live insects 2-3 times per week. Small, frequent meals are best for their fast metabolism. Monitor their growth to ensure they are gaining weight steadily.

Adult crested geckos, generally over one year old, have slower metabolisms. They do not need to eat as frequently. Offer CGD 3-4 times per week. Provide live insects once or twice a week. Adjust the amount based on your gecko's appetite and body condition. A healthy adult crested gecko should have a plump tail base.

Always feed your crested gecko in the evening. Crested geckos are nocturnal, meaning they are most active at night. This aligns with their natural foraging patterns. Remove any uneaten CGD or live insects the following morning. This prevents spoilage and keeps the enclosure clean. Consistent meal times reduce stress for your pet.

## Common Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Avoiding common feeding mistakes is as important as providing proper nutrition. Errors in diet can lead to various health issues. Being mindful of these pitfalls ensures your crested gecko remains healthy. Knowing what not to do protects your pet.

One major mistake is feeding human food. Many human foods are harmful or lack the correct nutritional balance for geckos. Foods like bread, dairy, processed snacks, and most cooked meats are not suitable. Stick to reptile-specific diets and approved treats. Human food can cause digestive problems or nutrient imbalances.

Another common error is overfeeding or underfeeding. Overfeeding leads to obesity, which stresses organs. Underfeeding causes stunted growth and malnutrition. Monitor your gecko's body condition and adjust portion sizes accordingly. A healthy weight is crucial for a long life.

Lack of diet variety is also a problem. Relying on only one type of CGD or a single insect species can lead to nutrient gaps. Rotate CGD flavors and offer a mix of appropriate insects. Variety ensures a broad spectrum of nutrients. It also keeps your gecko interested in food.

Ignoring gut loading and supplementation can cause severe deficiencies. Live insects not gut-loaded offer minimal nutritional value. Skipping calcium and vitamin dusting can lead to metabolic bone disease. Always prepare insects properly and use supplements as advised. Finally, poor hygiene in food preparation and dish cleaning can introduce harmful bacteria. Always clean food dishes thoroughly after each use. These simple steps make a big difference.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can crested geckos eat baby food?
No, crested geckos should not eat baby food. Human baby food lacks the correct nutritional balance for geckos. It often contains sugars and preservatives that are harmful. Stick to commercial crested gecko diets (CGD) which are specifically formulated for their needs. These provide complete and safe nutrition.

### How often should I feed live insects?
Adult crested geckos should receive live insects 1-2 times per week. Juvenile geckos can be fed insects 2-3 times per week due to their faster growth. Always gut-load and dust insects with calcium and D3 before feeding. This enhances their nutritional value.

### Do crested geckos need calcium?
Yes, crested geckos absolutely need calcium with D3. Calcium is vital for strong bones and to prevent metabolic bone disease. Vitamin D3 helps with calcium absorption. Dust live insects with calcium powder. Some CGD formulas contain calcium, but extra supplementation is still often needed.

### What fruits are bad for crested geckos?
You should avoid feeding citrus fruits like oranges, lemons, and grapefruits, as they are too acidic. Also, do not offer avocado, onion, or garlic, which are toxic. Rhubarb, wild mushrooms, and most seeds are also unsafe. Always research a fruit before offering it.

### How much CGD should I offer?
Offer enough mixed CGD to fill a small, shallow dish. For most geckos, this is about 1-2 teaspoons. Watch how much your gecko eats. Adjust the amount to prevent waste. Remove uneaten CGD within 24-36 hours. This keeps the enclosure clean and prevents bacterial growth.

### Can crested geckos eat crickets daily?
No, crested geckos should not eat crickets daily. Crickets are a good protein source but are not a complete diet. Overfeeding crickets can lead to nutritional imbalances. Offer crickets 1-2 times a week for adults and 2-3 times a week for juveniles, alongside their primary CGD.
