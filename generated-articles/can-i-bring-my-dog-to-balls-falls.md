#### Hydration and Heat Safety

Dogs do not sweat like humans. They cool themselves primarily by panting. On warm days, this system can become overwhelmed. This leads to heatstroke, a life-threatening condition. Always provide ample water. Take frequent breaks in shady spots. If your dog shows signs of heat stress (heavy panting, weakness, bright red gums), cool them down immediately. Pour water on their head and belly. Seek vet care right away.

It is best to visit Balls Falls during cooler parts of the day. Morning or late afternoon are often good choices. Avoid midday hikes in summer. If the pavement is too hot for your bare hand, it is too hot for your dog's paws. Paw pads can burn easily.

#### Q2: What facilities are available for dogs at Balls Falls?
Balls Falls provides marked trails suitable for leashed dogs. There are also waste bins for dog waste disposal. However, there are no specific dog parks, watering stations, or designated off-leash areas for pets. Plan to bring your own water and waste bags.

#### Q3: Is there an entrance fee for dogs at Balls Falls?
Typically, there is no separate entrance fee for dogs at Balls Falls. The fee usually covers the vehicle or per-person entry to the conservation area. Always check the Niagara Peninsula Conservation Authority website or on-site signage for the most current fee information.

#### Q4: Can service animals access all areas of Balls Falls?
Yes, legitimate service animals are permitted in all public areas of Balls Falls, including places where pet dogs might be restricted. Service animals are specially trained and recognized under accessibility laws. They are not considered pets.
