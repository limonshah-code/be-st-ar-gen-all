### How does a dog get kennel cough?

A dog gets kennel cough by exposure to infected dogs or contaminated environments. The disease spreads through airborne droplets from coughs and sneezes. Direct contact with an infected dog, or sharing bowls and toys, can also transmit the illness. Viruses and bacteria, like *Bordetella bronchiseptica*, cause it.

### How Kennel Cough Spreads: Modes of Transmission

Kennel cough is very contagious. It spreads easily from one dog to another. Understanding how it spreads is key to preventing it. There are three main ways dogs catch this illness. Knowing these routes helps owners take precautions. My vet always stresses preventing spread.

First, kennel cough spreads through the air. When an infected dog coughs or sneezes, tiny droplets carrying germs fly into the air. Other dogs breathe in these droplets. This is a common way the infection moves. Dog parks or crowded kennels are high-risk places for this spread.

Second, direct contact causes spread. Dogs greet each other by sniffing and licking. If a dog interacts closely with an infected dog, germs can pass. Sharing toys or water bowls also counts as direct contact. These items become contaminated.

Third, contaminated objects can spread the disease. Germs can live on surfaces for a short time. This includes dog beds, food bowls, or even human hands. If you touch an infected dog and then pet your own dog, you might transfer germs. It is important to know [how long are dogs contagious with kennel cough](https://beacleaner.com/how-long-are-dogs-contagious-with-kennel-cough) to prevent further spread. Always wash your hands after handling dogs outside your home. Clean your dog's items often.

### Key Pathogens: The Culprits Behind Kennel Cough

Kennel cough is not caused by just one germ. It comes from a mix of different bacteria and viruses. These pathogens work together to cause the infection. Understanding these culprits helps us know [how a dog gets kennel cough]. It also guides vaccine choices. My vet taught me about these tiny invaders.

The main bacterial cause is *Bordetella bronchiseptica*. This bacterium is similar to whooping cough in humans. It attacks the lining of the dog's trachea and bronchi. This leads to irritation and that distinctive cough. Many kennel cough vaccines target *Bordetella*.

Several viruses also contribute to kennel cough. Canine parainfluenza virus is a common one. Canine adenovirus-2 is another culprit. Canine herpesvirus and canine distemper virus can also play a role. These viruses weaken the dog's respiratory system. This makes it easier for *Bordetella* to cause a secondary infection.

When a dog gets infected, these germs inflame the respiratory tract. The body tries to clear the infection. This leads to coughing. Different combinations of these pathogens can cause kennel cough. This is why a dog can get kennel cough even after vaccination. The vaccine might cover some but not all strains. Knowing these pathogens helps us understand the disease.

### Understanding the Incubation Period and Contagiousness

When a dog gets exposed to kennel cough, symptoms do not appear right away. There is an incubation period. This is the time between exposure and when signs show. Knowing this period helps in understanding the spread. My vet explained this to me when my neighbor's dog got sick.

The incubation period for kennel cough is typically short. It usually lasts between two and fourteen days. Most dogs start showing symptoms within three to four days of exposure. During this time, the germs are multiplying inside the dog's body. The dog might not seem sick yet.

Dogs can be contagious even before they show symptoms. They can spread the illness during the incubation period. They remain contagious throughout their illness. This period can last for several weeks after the cough stops. *Bordetella* bacteria can be shed for six to fourteen weeks. Viruses might shed for a shorter time. This extended contagiousness makes kennel cough hard to control.

This is why isolating a sick dog is very important. Even if your dog seems better, they might still spread germs. Avoid dog parks or doggy daycare during this time. ### Preventing Kennel Cough: Vaccination and Hygiene

Preventing kennel cough is always better than treating it. There are several steps you can take. These steps reduce the risk of your dog getting sick. My vet always emphasizes prevention for my pets.

Vaccination is a key preventative measure. Your vet can recommend the right vaccines. The *Bordetella* vaccine is the most common. It comes in different forms: injection, nasal spray, or oral. The nasal and oral vaccines often offer faster protection. They are given directly to the respiratory tract. Ask your vet which option suits your dog best. Even with vaccines, a dog can still get a mild form of kennel cough. The vaccine reduces severity.

Good hygiene plays a vital role. Wash your dog's food and water bowls often. Clean their bedding regularly. Use disinfectants on surfaces if a sick dog has been present. Wash your hands after petting other dogs. This prevents you from carrying germs to your own dog.

Avoid crowded dog areas during outbreaks. If you know there is a kennel cough outbreak, keep your dog home. Limit visits to dog parks or boarding kennels. If your dog must go to a facility, check their vaccination policy. Reputable facilities require all dogs to be current on vaccines. These steps help lower the chance of your dog getting kennel cough.

### FAQ Section

#### Can a vaccinated dog still get kennel cough?
Yes, a vaccinated dog can still get kennel cough. Vaccines protect against common strains like *Bordetella*. However, many different viruses and bacteria can cause the illness. The vaccine reduces the severity and duration of symptoms. It might not prevent every single infection. A vaccinated dog usually gets a milder case.

#### How long does kennel cough last in dogs?
Kennel cough usually lasts for one to three weeks. Mild cases might resolve faster. More severe infections, or those with complications, can last longer. Young puppies, older dogs, or dogs with weaker immune systems might take more time to recover. Always monitor your dog's symptoms.

#### Is kennel cough dangerous for puppies?
Kennel cough can be more dangerous for puppies. Their immune systems are still developing. Puppies are more prone to complications like pneumonia. If a puppy gets kennel cough, seek vet care quickly. Early treatment helps prevent serious issues. Puppies need careful monitoring.

#### Can humans catch kennel cough from dogs?
No, humans cannot catch kennel cough from dogs. Kennel cough is a species-specific illness. The bacteria and viruses that cause it in dogs do not infect humans. However, people with weakened immune systems should practice good hygiene around sick animals. This prevents any rare transfer of other germs.

#### What are the first signs of kennel cough?
The first signs of kennel cough often appear within 3-4 days of exposure. These include a sudden, dry, hacking cough. The cough often sounds like a goose honk. It might worsen with excitement or exercise. Some dogs also have sneezing, a runny nose, or mild lethargy.

#### How can I make my dog more comfortable with kennel cough?
To make your dog more comfortable, ensure they have a warm, quiet resting place. Use a humidifier to moisten the air. This helps soothe their throat. Avoid using a collar that pulls on their windpipe; use a harness instead. Keep them hydrated with fresh water. Follow your vet's advice for any medication.
