### How Long Do Yorkshire Terrier Dogs Live?

Yorkshire Terrier dogs typically live for a good number of years. Their average lifespan falls between 11 and 15 years. Many factors influence this range. With proper care and a little luck, some Yorkies can even exceed this estimate.

Yorkshire Terriers are known for their relatively long lives. Their small size often contributes to this extended [Yorkie life expectancy]. Many small dog breeds live longer than larger breeds. This is a general rule in the dog world.

### Genetics and Breed-Specific Health Concerns

Genetics play a big part in how long your Yorkshire Terrier lives. Like all purebred dogs, Yorkies can inherit certain health problems. These health issues can reduce a [Yorkie's lifespan] if not managed. Knowing about these conditions helps you work with your vet. Early detection makes a real difference.

Some common hereditary conditions in Yorkshire Terriers include patellar luxation. This is a knee cap issue. Another concern is tracheal collapse, affecting their windpipe. Portosystemic shunt is a liver condition that also appears in some Yorkies. Dental disease is also very common due to their small mouths. These problems can impact their comfort and longevity.

I always recommend choosing a puppy from a reputable breeder. Good breeders test their breeding dogs for genetic diseases. This helps reduce the risk of passing on health problems. Ask breeders for health clearances for the parents. This step is important for increasing the chances of a healthy, long-living Yorkshire Terrier. Regular vet visits help monitor for these issues. This proactive approach improves the quality and duration of your Yorkie’s life.

*   **Patellar Luxation:** This condition involves the kneecap slipping out of place. It causes pain and limits movement. Surgery can often correct severe cases, improving the dog's quality of life.
*   **Tracheal Collapse:** This affects the windpipe, causing a honking cough. It is more common in small breeds. Management often involves medication and avoiding neck pressure.
*   **Portosystemic Shunt (PSS):** This is an abnormal blood vessel that bypasses the liver. It prevents the liver from filtering toxins properly. Diet changes or surgery can treat this.
