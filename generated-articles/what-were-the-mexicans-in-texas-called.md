## What Were The Mexicans In Texas Called

Names hold power. They shape identity and tell stories. When we look at the history of Texas, the question "What Were The Mexicans In Texas Called" opens a door to a complex past. Early Mexican residents of Texas used specific names for themselves. Understanding these terms helps us recognize their vital role in shaping the region. This article explores the various names used over time. We will trace how these names reflected shifting political landscapes and cultural identities.

I will guide you through the journey of these names. We will start with the original Tejanos. Then we will move to the emergence of "Mexican American." We will also look at other important cultural identifiers. This exploration offers unique insights into the rich history of Texas. It honors the people who lived it.

### Takeaway

Understanding the historical names for Mexicans in Texas clarifies their enduring legacy.
*   **Tejanos:** The primary term for early Mexican residents. They had deep roots in Texas before Anglo settlement.
*   **Mexican Americans:** This identity emerged after the U.S.-Mexican War. It reflected a new political status.
*   **Evolving Terms:** People used "Hispanic," "Latino," and "Chicano" at different times. These terms show changing self-identification and social movements.
*   **Cultural Preservation:** Many people today use "Tejano" to honor their heritage. They keep their unique history alive.

The Mexicans in Texas were primarily called Tejanos. This term referred to people of Mexican descent living in Texas, especially before and during the Texas Revolution. After the U.S.-Mexican War, the term "Mexican American" became common. This reflected their new citizenship status within the United States.

### The Original Texans: Tejanos and Their Legacy

Long before Texas joined the United States, Mexican people lived on this land. These early settlers called themselves Tejanos. They established ranches, towns, and missions. Their roots ran deep in the soil of Texas.

Tejanos built a distinct culture over generations. This culture blended Spanish, Indigenous, and Mexican influences. They navigated life under Spanish and then Mexican rule. Their communities thrived.

#### Who Were the Tejanos?

Tejanos were Spanish-speaking Texans. They lived in Texas during its time as a Spanish colony and a Mexican state. Their families often settled here for generations. They considered Texas their homeland. Many Tejanos fought for Texas's independence from Mexico. They believed in a republican form of government.

Tejanos included diverse groups of people. Some were descendants of original Spanish colonists. Others had Indigenous ancestry. Many were mestizos, a mix of Spanish and Indigenous heritage. They all shared a common Texas identity.

#### Life Before the Revolution

Tejanos built strong communities. San Antonio, Goliad, and Nacogdoches were key centers. They developed a ranching economy. Vaqueros, or cowboys, managed vast herds of cattle. This lifestyle shaped their unique identity.

Tejanos also faced challenges. Indigenous groups like the Comanches posed threats. The Mexican government was often unstable. Despite these issues, Tejanos kept their culture alive. They established institutions. They contributed greatly to early Texas.

### Shifting Identities: From Mexican Citizen to Mexican American

The Texas Revolution brought massive changes. Tejanos found themselves in a difficult position. They had to choose sides in a conflict between their homeland and a new republic. Some Tejanos supported the revolution. They believed in the cause for independence.

The outcome of the revolution deeply impacted Tejano life. Texas became an independent republic. Then it joined the United States. This political shift changed how Tejanos were seen and how they saw themselves. Their identity evolved.

#### The Republic of Texas Era

After 1836, Texas became its own republic. Many Anglo-American settlers arrived. Tejanos, despite their contributions, often faced discrimination. Their land claims were challenged. Their political influence decreased.

The new government viewed them with suspicion. Some Tejanos lost their homes and properties. They struggled to maintain their way of life. This period marked a difficult transition for Tejano communities.

#### The Treaty's Impact

The U.S.-Mexican War ended in 1848. The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo followed. This treaty dramatically changed the border. It gave vast Mexican territories to the United States. Texas was part of this transfer.

Mexicans living in these territories became U.S. citizens. They had to choose between returning to Mexico or staying in their homes. Those who stayed became "Mexican Americans." This name reflected their dual identity. They were ethnically Mexican, but politically American. This change brought new legal and social challenges.

#### The Rise of Chicano Identity

In the 1960s and 1970s, a new identity emerged. This was the "Chicano" movement. Young Mexican Americans embraced this term. They used it to assert their political power and cultural pride. It was a rejection of assimilation.

Chicano identity was strong in Texas. It focused on civil rights. It also highlighted Indigenous heritage. Chicanos sought social justice. They worked to empower their communities. The term "Chicano" represented a distinct political consciousness.

### The Impact of Anglo Settlement on Terminology

Anglo-American settlement in Texas had a profound impact on language. As more Anglos arrived, they brought their own terms. These terms often simplified or misunderstood Mexican identity. Sometimes, they were used to assert dominance.

The influx of Anglos changed the demographics of Texas. It also changed the social hierarchy. Mexicans, once the majority, became a minority. This shift influenced how they were labeled by outsiders. Their land and culture faced new pressures.

#### Outsider Perspectives and Labels

Anglo settlers often used generic terms. They might refer to all Mexicans as "Mexicans" or "Spanish." These terms did not recognize the distinct Tejano identity. They often grouped diverse people under one label. This simplified a complex heritage.

Some terms used by Anglos were derogatory. They were used to demean or marginalize. This language reinforced racial inequalities. It showed the power imbalance. These labels became part of the struggle for dignity.

#### Loss of Land and Political Power

As Anglo population grew, Tejanos lost power. They lost political representation. They also lost land. Anglo settlers moved into Tejano communities. They often claimed land through legal and illegal means. This land loss was devastating.

The change in power dynamics affected identity. It also affected how people were named. Terms became tools of social control. Tejanos struggled to maintain their standing. Their history shows great resilience in the face of these challenges.
