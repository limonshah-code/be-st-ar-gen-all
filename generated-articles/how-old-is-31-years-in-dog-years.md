#### Physical Changes to Expect

As dogs enter their senior years, several physical changes occur:
*   **Slowing Down**: They might move slower. Joint stiffness or arthritis can become more apparent. They might not jump as high or run as fast.
*   **Changes in Coat**: Their fur may get gray, especially around the muzzle and eyes. The coat might also become thinner or coarser.
*   **Weight Fluctuations**: Some dogs gain weight due to reduced activity. Others might lose weight due to underlying health issues or decreased appetite.
*   **Sensory Decline**: Vision and hearing can worsen. They might bump into things or not respond to commands as readily.
*   **Dental Issues**: Dental disease is very common in older dogs. It can cause pain and affect eating.

These changes are a natural part of aging. Regular vet check-ups can help manage many of these conditions. We can improve their comfort and quality of life.

#### Mental and Behavioral Shifts

Aging also affects a dog's mind and behavior:
*   **Cognitive Decline**: Some dogs may show signs of canine cognitive dysfunction (CCD). This is similar to dementia in humans.
*   **Confusion or Disorientation**: They might get lost in familiar places or stare blankly.
*   **Changes in Sleep Patterns**: Older dogs might sleep more during the day. They may also be restless at night.
*   **Increased Anxiety**: They could become more anxious or irritable. This might be due to pain or sensory loss.
*   **House-training Accidents**: Decreased bladder control can lead to accidents. This is not defiance, but an age-related issue.

Mental stimulation is still important. Puzzle toys and gentle training can help keep their minds active. Patience and understanding are key when dealing with these behavioral changes.

#### Adjusting Care for Senior Dogs
