Even good movies have areas where they could improve. "Beverly Hills Chihuahua" is no exception. While charming, some viewers find the plot to be predictable. However, it can make the movie feel a little too straightforward for some.

The portrayal of some characters also draws criticism. Chloe's initially spoiled attitude might not appeal to everyone. Her character arc is clear, but her early actions can be off-putting. Some viewers also find the human characters to be less developed. They mainly serve to move the dog's story along. This can make them feel a bit one-dimensional. The humor, while mostly good, can sometimes rely on clichés. Animal humor can be repetitive if not handled carefully.

Another point of discussion involves the film's realism, or lack thereof. The dogs talk and behave like humans. This is a common trope in animal movies. However, some wish for a bit more grounding in reality. The focus on heavily anthropomorphized animals might not suit all tastes. While children usually love it, some adults may prefer more nuanced animal storytelling.

The film's pacing also comes up occasionally. Some parts of Chloe's journey feel rushed. Other parts might drag a little. Balancing adventure with character development is key. The movie does a decent job, but it is not flawless. Despite these points, the film's overall positive message and engaging dog characters still win over many viewers. It delivers on its promise of a fun, family-friendly animal adventure. For many, these small criticisms do not stop the movie from being an enjoyable watch.
