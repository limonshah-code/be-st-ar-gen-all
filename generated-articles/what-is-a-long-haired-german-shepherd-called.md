## Unveiling the Long-Haired German Shepherd: What Are They Called?

Many dog lovers marvel at the striking appearance of a German Shepherd with a flowing, luxurious coat. You might wonder, "What is a long haired German Shepherd called?" It is a common question, and understanding their designation helps clarify their place within the breed. These magnificent dogs are not a separate breed but a variation within the German Shepherd lineage.

This article explores the official and unofficial names for these beautiful dogs. We will discuss their genetic background, breed standard acceptance, and specific care requirements. You will learn how to identify them, understand their temperament, and distinguish them from similar breeds. My goal is to provide clear, helpful information for current and prospective owners.

*   Long-haired German Shepherds are simply a coat variation of the standard German Shepherd breed.
*   They are often called Long Coat German Shepherds or simply German Shepherds (Longhaired variety).

### What is a long haired German Shepherd called?

A long haired German Shepherd is simply called a **Long Haired German Shepherd** or a **Long Coat German Shepherd**. They are not a distinct breed. They are a variety of the standard German Shepherd dog. Their longer coat results from a specific recessive gene within the breed.

## Defining the Long Coat German Shepherd

The Long Coat German Shepherd stands out with its beautiful, flowing fur. This coat type is a natural variation within the German Shepherd breed itself. We do not consider them a separate breed. Their genetic makeup determines their coat length.

Breed enthusiasts and registries use clear terms to describe these dogs. Most commonly, people refer to them as "Long Haired German Shepherds." Sometimes, you hear the term "Long Coat German Shepherd." These terms clearly indicate their primary distinguishing feature.

### The Recessive Gene Behind the Long Coat

A specific gene, known as the FGF5 gene, controls coat length in many dog breeds. In German Shepherds, a recessive allele on this gene causes the long coat. This means a puppy must inherit two copies of this recessive gene, one from each parent, to display the long-haired trait. If a puppy receives only one copy, it will have a standard coat but can pass the long-coat gene to its offspring. This genetic mechanism explains why long-haired puppies can appear in litters from two standard-coated parents.

### Coat Varieties in German Shepherds

German Shepherds generally come in three main coat types based on length and presence of an undercoat:

*   **Short Coat (Stock Coat):** This is the most common and widely recognized coat type. It features a dense, straight, harsh outer coat with a softer undercoat.
*   **Plush Coat:** This coat is slightly longer than the short coat but still has a dense undercoat. It offers a fuller appearance than the short coat.
*   **Long Coat:** This type has significantly longer hair, especially around the ears, chest, legs, and tail. The presence or absence of an undercoat can vary within this type, impacting its recognition by different kennel clubs. My personal opinion is that the long coat is stunning to behold.

## Breed Standards and Acceptance for Long Haired German Shepherds

The acceptance of the long-haired German Shepherd varies significantly among different canine organizations. This is an important distinction for breeders and owners, especially those interested in showing their dogs. Some clubs consider the long coat a fault, while others accept it. This difference often stems from historical views on the breed's working capabilities.

Understanding these different standards helps clarify why you might see varying opinions on these beautiful dogs. Each organization has its own criteria for what constitutes a "standard" German Shepherd. It shapes breeding practices and show ring eligibility.

### International Canine Federation (FCI) Stance

The Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI) is a global organization that oversees many national kennel clubs. For a long time, the FCI considered the long coat without an undercoat a disqualifying fault. This meant dogs with this coat type could not compete in FCI-sanctioned shows or receive championship titles. However, the FCI recently revised its standard. They now recognize the long coat with an undercoat as acceptable. They still consider the long coat *without* an undercoat a fault. This change reflects a shifting perspective within the international dog community.

### American Kennel Club (AKC) View

The American Kennel Club (AKC) in the United States maintains a stricter standard regarding coat type. The AKC breed standard for German Shepherds specifies a "double coat of medium length." It describes the outer coat as dense, straight, and harsh, with a dense undercoat. They consider a long, soft, or woolly coat a serious fault. This means that a true long-haired German Shepherd, especially one without a substantial undercoat, cannot be shown in AKC conformation events. My experience tells me that this does not diminish their quality as companions.

### Verein für Deutsche Schäferhunde (SV) Perspective

The Verein für Deutsche Schäferhunde (SV) is the original German Shepherd Dog Club in Germany. It holds significant influence over the breed worldwide. Historically, the SV has been very firm on its coat type requirements. They generally consider the long coat a fault. Their focus is on the functional aspects of the breed, and they believe a medium-length double coat provides optimal protection and utility. The SV's strict standards have influenced many other kennel clubs globally. These standards shape the breeding goals of many German Shepherd breeders.

## Identifying a Long-Haired German Shepherd Puppy

Identifying a long-haired German Shepherd puppy is a fun and interesting process. Sometimes, it can be obvious, but other times, you need to look closer. The distinguishing features become more apparent as the puppy grows. However, some early signs can help you determine their coat type.

Knowing what to look for can prevent confusion later. It also helps set expectations for grooming needs. I always enjoy watching how a puppy's coat develops over time.

### Observing Coat Characteristics

When examining a German Shepherd puppy for long hair, several key characteristics stand out. Look for feathers of longer hair. These often appear behind the ears, on the back of the legs (sometimes called "trousers"), and on the tail. A long-haired puppy will usually have noticeably longer hair on these areas compared to its short-haired littermates. The fur may also feel softer and silkier to the touch. It lacks the harsh, coarse texture of a standard stock coat.

### Puppy Coat vs. Adult Coat

A puppy's coat can sometimes be deceiving. All German Shepherd puppies have soft, fluffy fur. This "puppy coat" will shed and be replaced by the adult coat. This transition typically happens between 4 to 6 months of age. For long-haired puppies, the longer hairs will become more pronounced during this change. The feathering will extend further, and the tail will become much bushier. A short-haired puppy's coat will shorten and become denser as it matures. Observing this development is a key part of telling the difference. ## Care and Grooming Needs of the Long Coat German Shepherd

Long-haired German Shepherds have specific grooming requirements due to their luxurious coats. Their beautiful fur demands regular attention to stay healthy and mat-free. Neglecting their coat can lead to tangles, skin issues, and discomfort for the dog. I find that a consistent grooming routine makes a huge difference.

These dogs are a joy to care for, but owners must commit to proper grooming. Understanding their needs will keep them looking their best and feeling good. Regular care also strengthens the bond between you and your dog.

### Daily Brushing is Key

Regular brushing is the most important part of grooming a long-haired German Shepherd. I recommend brushing them daily, or at least every other day. This routine prevents mats and tangles, which can quickly form, especially in areas like behind the ears, under the legs, and on the tail. Use a slicker brush to remove loose hair and dirt, then follow with a wider-toothed comb to ensure no tangles remain. Brushing also distributes natural oils, which keep the coat shiny and healthy.

### Managing Shedding Seasons

Like all German Shepherds, the long-haired variety sheds. They experience heavier shedding seasons, often twice a year, usually in spring and fall. During these periods, increased brushing is essential. A deshedding tool can be very effective in removing the loose undercoat. It helps to prevent excessive hair accumulation around your home. Regular grooming minimizes the amount of hair your dog sheds indoors. It also helps to keep their skin healthy by removing dead fur. ### To Trim or Not to Trim?

Many long-haired German Shepherd owners wonder about trimming their dog's fur. Generally, professional groomers advise against shaving a German Shepherd. Their double coat provides insulation in both cold and hot weather. Shaving disrupts this natural insulation. It can also lead to improper regrowth, skin issues, and a dull coat.

However, light trimming of certain areas, like the hair between paw pads or around the hocks, can be beneficial. This prevents mats and improves hygiene. If you consider any trimming, always consult with a professional groomer or veterinarian. They can offer advice specific to your dog's coat and health. ## Temperament and Health of Long Haired German Shepherds

People often ask if the long-haired German Shepherd has a different temperament or unique health concerns. The good news is that they are fundamentally the same dog as their short-haired counterparts. Their coat length does not impact their personality or inherent health predispositions. My own observations confirm this.

You can expect the same loyal, intelligent, and protective companion. Understanding their general health risks is crucial for all German Shepherds, regardless of coat type. Proper care helps them live a full, healthy life.

### Same Loyal, Intelligent Disposition

Long-haired German Shepherds share the exact same temperament traits as short-haired German Shepherds. They are renowned for their intelligence, making them highly trainable. These dogs are loyal and devoted to their families, often forming strong bonds. They are protective by nature, which makes them excellent guard dogs. Early socialization and consistent training are vital for all German Shepherds to develop into well-adjusted adults. My experience shows they thrive on having a job to do and spending time with their people.

### Common Health Concerns

Like all purebred dogs, German Shepherds, regardless of coat length, are prone to certain genetic health conditions. Being aware of these helps owners provide proactive care. Some of the most common issues include:

*   **Hip and Elbow Dysplasia:** These are hereditary conditions affecting the joints. Responsible breeders screen their dogs.
*   **Degenerative Myelopathy (DM):** A progressive disease of the spinal cord, usually appearing in older dogs.
*   **Bloat (Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus - GDV):** A life-threatening condition where the stomach twists.
*   **Allergies:** German Shepherds can be susceptible to various allergies, leading to skin or digestive issues.

Regular veterinary check-ups and a balanced diet are crucial for maintaining a long-haired German Shepherd's health. With good care, they can live a healthy lifespan. ## Distinguishing Long Haired German Shepherds from Other Breeds

Sometimes, people confuse long-haired German Shepherds with other breeds. Their flowing coats can resemble those of different dog types. This confusion often arises when people are unfamiliar with the specific characteristics of various breeds. It is important to know the differences.

Understanding these distinctions helps properly identify your dog. It also helps appreciate the unique traits of each breed. I want to help clarify these common misconceptions for you.

### Long Haired GSD vs. King Shepherd

The King Shepherd is a distinct breed. It developed from a mix of German Shepherds, Great Pyrenees, and Alaskan Malamutes. King Shepherds are significantly larger and heavier than German Shepherds. They have a very dense, long double coat. While they share some German Shepherd ancestry and look similar due to their long coats, they are not the same. King Shepherds were bred for their impressive size and gentle temperament. Long-haired German Shepherds retain the standard German Shepherd size and build. Knowing this helps answer questions like [Is a long haired German Shepherd a King Shepherd?](https://beacleaner.com/is-a-long-haired-german-shepherd-a-king-shepherd).

### Long Haired GSD vs. Belgian Shepherd

The Belgian Shepherd is another breed sometimes confused with the long-haired German Shepherd. There are four varieties of Belgian Shepherds: Malinois, Tervuren, Groenendael, and Laekenois. The Groenendael and Tervuren varieties have long coats. The Groenendael has a solid black long coat, while the Tervuren has a fawn or mahogany coat with black overlay. Belgian Shepherds are generally more finely built and agile than German Shepherds. Their heads and muzzles are typically more refined. The German Shepherd has a more robust bone structure and often a more sloped back. These distinctions are key to recognizing each breed. ## FAQ Section

### Q1: Is a long haired German Shepherd a different breed?
No, a long haired German Shepherd is not a different breed. It is a coat variation within the standard German Shepherd breed. Their long coat results from a recessive gene. They possess all the same characteristics, temperament, and health predispositions as short-haired German Shepherds.

### Q2: Are long haired German Shepherds rare?
Long haired German Shepherds are less common than the standard short-haired variety. This is because the long coat gene is recessive. Both parents must carry the gene for a long-haired puppy to be born. They are not considered extremely rare, but they do stand out.

### Q3: Do long haired German Shepherds shed more?
Long haired German Shepherds often appear to shed more due to the sheer volume and length of their hair. However, they do not necessarily shed more *frequently* than short-haired GSDs. Regular brushing is crucial to manage their shedding. This prevents mats and keeps your home cleaner.

### Q4: Can long haired German Shepherds work as service dogs?
Yes, long haired German Shepherds can absolutely work as service dogs. Their coat length does not affect their intelligence, trainability, or temperament. These qualities make German Shepherds excellent candidates for service work. Their loyalty and focus are ideal for assistance roles.

### Q5: How do I identify a long haired German Shepherd?
You can identify a long haired German Shepherd by looking for distinct feathering. This includes longer hair on their ears, chest, legs, and a very bushy tail. Their overall coat appears longer and often softer than a standard short-haired German Shepherd. You can find more detail on [How do I know if my German Shepherd is long haired?](https://beacleaner.com/how-do-i-know-if-my-german-shepherd-is-long-haired).

### Q6: Do long haired German Shepherds have unique health issues?
No, long haired German Shepherds do not have unique health issues related to their coat type. They are susceptible to the same hereditary conditions as all other German Shepherds. These include hip and elbow dysplasia, degenerative myelopathy, and bloat. Their health is tied to their genetic lineage, not their coat length.
